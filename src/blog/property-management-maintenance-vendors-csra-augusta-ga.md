## The Vendor Network Is the Hidden Core of Property Management

When most owners think about what a property manager does, they think about rent collection, tenant placement, and answering phone calls. Those are the visible parts. The maintenance vendor network is invisible until something breaks.

Maintenance is the single largest variable cost in owning a rental property in the CSRA. A single-family home in [Evans](/evans/) or Grovetown will typically need two to four maintenance calls per year under normal operating conditions — service calls, make-ready work, appliance failures, HVAC tune-ups, pest control treatments, and so on. Over 12 months, those repairs can run anywhere from a few hundred dollars to several thousand depending on the age of the property and its systems. You can see a full breakdown of what these costs look like across CSRA property types in our [summer maintenance checklist for Augusta-area rentals](/blog/summer-maintenance-checklist-augusta-rental-property/).

Who does that work, how fast they respond, how much they charge, and whether the issue is resolved correctly on the first visit directly determines your net operating income. A vendor who misdiagnoses an HVAC problem, charges above-market rates, and leaves before confirming the system is running turns a manageable repair into a compounding problem. A vendor who doesn't show up for a scheduled appointment turns a routine repair into a tenant retention issue.

It includes licensed plumbers, electricians, HVAC technicians, pest control companies, painters, flooring specialists, make-ready crews, and landscapers. ## How We Vet and Qualify Every Vendor in Our Network

Before a vendor sets foot in any McBride PM property, they clear four requirements.

**Valid trade license.** The [Georgia State Construction Industry Licensing Board](https://sos.ga.gov/professionals-licensing) administers licensing for plumbing, electrical, conditioned air (HVAC), and general contracting work under O.C.G.A. Title 43. We verify the license is active, not suspended, and appropriate to the scope of work the vendor performs. We do not authorize unlicensed tradespeople for licensed trades, and we don't make exceptions based on cost or availability.

**General liability insurance, minimum $500,000.** If a vendor causes property damage while working in your rental — a flood from a burst compression fitting, fire from an electrical error — their general liability policy pays. An uninsured vendor means that damage lands on your landlord insurance, your deductible, and potentially your personal assets. We require current certificates of insurance before vendor approval and verify renewal when policies lapse.

**Workers' compensation coverage.** Under O.C.G.A. § 34-9, contractors with three or more employees must carry workers' compensation insurance in Georgia. A worker injured at your property without workers' comp coverage can pursue a claim directly against the property owner. We require proof of workers' comp for any vendor that sends employees into our managed properties.

**Track record with our team.** Documentation doesn't tell you everything. We also evaluate vendors on response time, completion quality, communication with residents, invoice accuracy, and whether they stand behind their work when something fails. A vendor who clears the paperwork check but shows up four hours late to an emergency, or leaves a job unfinished, doesn't stay on our approved list long.

| Qualification Requirement | What We Verify | Verification Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Trade license (plumbing, electrical, HVAC) | Active license, Georgia State Construction Industry Licensing Board | At onboarding + annually |
| General liability insurance | Minimum $500K, current certificate of insurance | At onboarding + at each renewal |
| Workers' compensation | Coverage for all employees sent to our properties | At onboarding + at each renewal |
| Performance record | Response time, completion rate, warranty claims honored | Ongoing, reviewed quarterly |

This vetting process is the baseline. It is not the process a self-managing landlord runs at 9 PM when something breaks.

## What the Work Order Process Looks Like From Submission to Invoice

When a resident submits a maintenance request — through their AppFolio resident portal, by text, or by calling our office — the request enters our system and is categorized within the same business day.

Here is the complete sequence from request to resolution:

1. **Request logged.** Every request is time-stamped in AppFolio with the description and any photos the resident submits.
2. **Triage.** Is this an emergency (no heat in winter, active water leak, electrical hazard, no hot water, no AC in summer heat) or a routine request? Emergency calls are dispatched within two hours, around the clock. Routine requests are scheduled within one to five business days.
3. **Vendor assignment.** The appropriate vendor from our approved network is contacted. Vendor selection depends on the trade, the property's location in the CSRA, and current availability.
4. **Owner notification.** For repairs above $500, we contact the owner before authorizing work. Exceptions: emergencies requiring immediate action to prevent further property damage or a habitability violation.
5. **Scheduling confirmation.** The vendor confirms the appointment window; we notify the resident with a scheduling window.
6. **Work performed.** The vendor contacts our office upon arrival and upon completion. Before-work and after-work photos are required for all jobs above minor routine maintenance.
7. **Invoice posted.** The invoice posts to your AppFolio owner portal within 24 to 48 hours of job completion. McBride PM passes vendor invoices through at cost — we do not mark up maintenance invoices.
8. **Warranty period.** Our standard vendor agreements include a minimum 30- to 90-day parts and labor warranty. If the same issue recurs within that window, the vendor returns at their cost.

This documented process matters beyond logistics. A written record of every repair — time-stamped, photographed, invoiced — is what protects you in a security deposit dispute, a habitability complaint under Georgia's Safe at Home Act, or an insurance claim. A memory or a handwritten receipt is not defensible. A complete job record in AppFolio is.

## What Georgia Law Requires of Contractors Working in Your Rental

This is the area that creates the most risk for self-managing landlords who aren't tracking it.

Georgia licenses specific trades through the Secretary of State's professional licensing boards. The trades most frequently needed in residential rental maintenance — plumbing, electrical, and HVAC — each require a valid Georgia license for non-trivial work. Performing this work without a license violates O.C.G.A. Title 43 and can result in stop-work orders, permit revocations, and fines.

Why does this matter to you as the property owner? Two concrete reasons.

First, if an unlicensed contractor performs work on your rental and that work causes injury or property damage, your landlord insurance policy may deny the claim. Many policies specifically exclude damage caused by contractors who lacked proper licensure for the work performed. You absorb the liability.

Second, Georgia's Safe at Home Act (House Bill 404, effective 2024) establishes minimum habitability standards for residential rentals, including functioning HVAC, plumbing, and electrical systems. If a repair performed by an unlicensed contractor fails and the property falls below habitability standards as a result, you face potential tenant remedies. The [Georgia Department of Community Affairs publishes guidance on these habitability standards](https://dca.ga.gov/safe-affordable-housing/rental-housing/housing-resources) — they're not optional.

Beyond state licensing, the [EPA's Section 608 regulations](https://www.epa.gov/section608) require that any technician who handles refrigerants in HVAC systems hold a valid EPA 608 certification. This is a federal requirement, separate from the state license. Every HVAC technician in our vendor network carries both their Georgia state conditioned air certification and their EPA 608 card.

A checklist for what to confirm before authorizing any repair on a CSRA rental:

- [ ] Plumbing work? Verify Georgia plumbing contractor license.
- [ ] Electrical work? Verify Georgia electrical contractor license.
- [ ] HVAC work? Verify Georgia conditioned air license AND EPA 608 certification.
- [ ] Any permitted work? Confirm permit will be pulled before work begins.
- [ ] Any contractor? Verify general liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
- [ ] Job value above $500? Get written estimate and owner authorization before proceeding.

*This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal advice. Consult a licensed Georgia attorney for specific questions about contractor liability or habitability obligations.*

## The Cost Reality: PM Vendor Relationships vs. Going It Alone

A common assumption is that a property manager's vendor relationships add cost — that the firm is marking up invoices, or using vendors who charge more because they know a professional firm will pay. At McBride PM, and at most [NARPM](https://www.narpm.org)-member property management firms, this assumption runs backward.

Here's why preferred vendor relationships typically lower costs for owners:

**Volume generates favorable rates.** We send consistent, reliable work to our network vendors throughout the year. In exchange, those vendors extend pricing that reflects an ongoing business relationship — not the transactional one-off rates a single owner receives when calling cold.

**No emergency surcharge.** When a self-managing landlord calls an HVAC company at 7 PM on a Friday in July, they pay whatever emergency after-hours rate is on that company's schedule. Our after-hours vendors are pre-agreed on pricing. There is no surprise emergency markup.

**First-call resolution reduces total cost.** Vendors who know our properties and our standards are more likely to diagnose correctly and resolve completely on the first visit. A misdiagnosis that requires two or three return trips — each billed separately — is consistently the most expensive maintenance outcome. We track first-call resolution rates for our vendors as a performance metric.

**Transparency on invoices.** McBride PM passes vendor invoices to owners at cost. You see the actual invoice; you pay the actual amount. The management fee covers coordination, oversight, and documentation — not a margin on labor and parts.

The honest answer is this: without a professional property manager, you don't have real-time visibility. You receive a call from a resident, take their word for what the problem is, call a contractor you found online, hope the diagnosis is correct, and wire money based on an invoice you can't independently verify.

With McBride PM, your AppFolio owner portal gives you real-time access from your phone. Every maintenance request appears there with the submission time and resident description. Every work order shows the assigned vendor, scheduled time, and scope. Every invoice is attached to the job record. Before-and-after photos are accessible when they're captured. You have a complete record of every dollar spent on your property without having to manage the call at 10 PM from another time zone.

For repairs above $500, we contact you before authorizing. You can ask questions, request a second estimate, or approve and move on. You're in the loop without needing to be the one fielding the vendor call.

Georgia's HB 399, effective in 2026, requires out-of-state landlords to engage a [Georgia-licensed property manager or broker](/blog/georgia-hb-399-csra-out-of-state-landlord-compliance/) for all residential rental properties in the state. That requirement exists precisely because the coordination and accountability a licensed professional provides is substantively different from informal management at a distance. The maintenance vendor network — and the documentation infrastructure behind it — is a concrete example of that difference.

## Five Questions to Ask Any Property Manager About Their Vendor Network

Before you sign a management agreement, ask these directly:

1. **What are your vendor qualification requirements?** Get the answer in writing, not verbally. Minimum license type, minimum insurance limits, and re-verification frequency should all be specified.
2. **Do you mark up vendor invoices?** The answer should be no — or the markup percentage and structure should appear clearly in the management agreement.
3. **Who handles after-hours emergencies, and what is the typical dispatch time?** Confirm there is a designated on-call vendor, not just an answering service that collects messages.
4. **What is the owner-authorization threshold?** Know the dollar amount above which work requires your approval. This varies by firm and should appear in your management agreement.
5. **How are residents kept informed during a repair?** If a vendor is delayed, does the PM communicate that proactively to the resident, or does the resident have to follow up? The answer directly affects tenant retention.

If a property manager can't answer these five questions directly and in writing, that tells you something concrete about the depth of their vendor management process.
