## Why Augusta Keeps Making Investors' Short Lists

Augusta's rental market has four demand anchors that hold even when national job growth slows. [LinkedIn named Augusta its top "City on the Rise" in July 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/07/23/augusta-tops-new-linkedin-list-cities-rise/), citing the expanding cybersecurity and defense corridor as the primary driver. That momentum traces back to concrete institutional commitments.

**Fort Gordon.** The Army's Cyber Center of Excellence is headquartered here, housing thousands of active-duty service members, civilian employees, and defense contractors. Military tenants receive Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH), which tracks closely to market rents and is paid automatically — a meaningful advantage for landlords who care about payment consistency. The Army's cyber headquarters presence, which concentrated well-paying civilian and contractor jobs in the market, continues to attract defense-sector employers to the area. For a full breakdown of BAH rates and military tenant dynamics, see our [Fort Gordon rental demand guide](/blog/fort-gordon-military-tenant-rental-demand-augusta/).

**Savannah River Site (SRS).** The nuclear cleanup and research facility on the South Carolina side employs a large civilian workforce that rents in both Aiken County and Richmond/Columbia County. SRS employment tends to be stable, with long contract cycles that translate to multi-year tenancy patterns. The [Alliance for Fort Gordon](https://www.fortgordonalliance.com/csra-is-in-its-largest-economic-renaissance-with-more-investment-coming-out-of-srs-and-fort-gordon/) describes the CSRA as being in its largest economic renaissance, with investment growth expected from both Fort Gordon and SRS.

**Augusta University Medical Center / Wellstar MCG.** The combined health system is the largest non-military employer in the metro, drawing medical residents, nurses, and healthcare staff who need rentals near the hospital corridor downtown. The opening of Wellstar's first Columbia County facility has broadened that demand footprint into the northern suburbs.

**Augusta National Golf Club.** Augusta National hosts the Masters Tournament each April and supports a hospitality sector that sustains service-industry employment year-round — a smaller but stable contributor to overall tenant demand.

Together, these four anchors give Augusta lower vacancy risk than a comparably-priced market dependent on a single employer or sector. For a deeper look at these demand drivers, see our post on [Augusta's four demand anchors](/blog/four-demand-anchors-augusta-ga-rental-market/).


## The CSRA Submarket Map: Where Your Money Goes Further

The CSRA is not one market — it is four or five distinct submarkets with different price-to-rent ratios, tenant profiles, and risk characteristics. Out-of-state investors who screen "Augusta, GA" and filter on gross yield often overlook important differences between these zones.

The core tradeoff: Augusta proper offers higher gross yields on paper, but net returns depend heavily on neighborhood selection, home condition, and management quality. Columbia County (Evans, Grovetown, Martinez) produces lower gross yields but commands a more homogeneous, stable tenant pool driven by Fort Gordon families, Wellstar employees, and Columbia County School District demand.

## Augusta Proper: Rent Ranges, ZIP Codes, and What to Underwrite

Augusta proper offers the CSRA's widest spread between purchase price and rent, which is why it draws investors hunting for cash flow over appreciation. As of November 16, 2026, the 12 homes we have listed for rent in Augusta are asking between $950 and $4,100 a month, depending on size and location. Breaking it down by bedroom count: two-bedroom homes are asking $995 to $1,650, three-bedroom homes $1,295 to $2,050, and four-bedroom homes $1,250 to $4,100. The upper end of that four-bedroom range reflects premium renovated properties near the medical corridor.

For comparison, [SFR Analytics](https://sfranalytics.com/rental-yields/ga/augusta-richmond-county-ga-sc) reported a median asking rent of $1,700 for single-family homes across the Augusta-Richmond County metro as of July 2026, weighted toward the larger suburban homes that dominate active listings. Apartment List reported a city-wide median of $1,202 for all housing types as of April 2026, up 0.5% year over year — a figure that includes apartments and skews lower than the single-family rental segment most investors target.

A few ZIP-code notes that don't appear in screeners:

**ZIP 30904 (north Augusta proper).** Mix of older brick ranches and modest 1990s construction. Medical corridor workers and university staff. Rents on the lower end of the 3BR range; purchase prices often under $160,000.

**ZIP 30907 (stretching from Augusta into Columbia County).** One of the stronger-performing ZIPs in the metro by both rents and appreciation. Proximity to both Evans employment and the medical corridor. TopHap's August 2026 data shows this ZIP at a $273,000 median sale price with one-year appreciation of 12.8%.

**ZIP 30906 (south Augusta).** Higher gross yields on paper; materially higher vacancy and turnover risk. Best suited for experienced investors with established local management relationships.

**ZIP 30909 (midtown Augusta).** Near Augusta University's main campus; mix of student and professional tenants. Good demand but more tenant churn than the suburban Columbia County markets.

*This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice. Talk to a Georgia-licensed real estate attorney and a CPA for your specific situation.*


## What Gross Yields and Net Cap Rates Look Like Right Now

[SFR Analytics](https://sfranalytics.com/rental-yields/ga/augusta-richmond-county-ga-sc) pegs the Augusta-Richmond County gross rental yield at **8.68%** as of July 2026, compared to a Georgia statewide average of **8.28%**. That is a gross figure: annual rent divided by home value. It does not account for vacancy, operating expenses, or management fees.

[Zillow's](https://www.zillow.com/augusta-ga/home-values) September 2026 estimate puts Augusta's typical home value at **$187,663** (up 2.4% year over year for Richmond County). [Redfin-based data reported by Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/georgia/augusta/whats-happening-house-prices-augusta-2026) shows a higher median sale price of **$294,805**, reflecting that sold-price medians skew higher than index-based valuations. The gap matters for underwriting: use actual comps pulled from a licensed agent in the specific ZIP, not a metro average.

A realistic gross-to-net translation for Augusta proper:

| Expense Line | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Vacancy (Augusta proper) | 7–10% of gross rent |
| Property management fee | 8–10% of collected rent |
| Leasing fee (new tenant) | One month's rent, amortized |
| Maintenance + repairs | 8–12% of gross rent |
| CapEx reserve | 5–8% of gross rent |
| Landlord insurance | $1,200–$2,000 / year |
| Property tax (Richmond County) | ~1.0–1.3% of assessed value |

After these line items, a property hitting an 8.68% gross yield might net 4.5–6.5% — higher if the home is newer and well-maintained, lower if it is a 1960s brick ranch with deferred HVAC and roofing. Our post on [CapEx planning for CSRA rentals](/blog/capex-planning-csra-rental-properties/) walks through realistic reserve budgets by home age and system type.

The Augusta rental market's gross yield advantage over Georgia statewide is real but modest. The stronger case for Augusta is the combination of yield, demand stability, and purchase price. You can buy a solid 3-bedroom rental in Augusta for $160,000–$220,000 — a price range that has largely disappeared in Atlanta, Charlotte, and most Southeast metros.

## Running the Numbers: A Sample Augusta Deal

Here is a simplified underwriting model for a $185,000 three-bedroom home in a mid-tier Augusta ZIP, financed with a 25% down payment at a 7.1% conventional mortgage rate. These numbers are illustrative — verify current rates and your specific tax situation before using them.

| Item | Annual |
|---|---|
| Gross rent ($1,600 / month) | $19,200 |
| Vacancy at 8% | -$1,536 |
| Effective gross income | $17,664 |
| Management (9% of collected) | -$1,590 |
| Leasing fee (amortized) | -$800 |
| Maintenance + repairs (10%) | -$1,920 |
| CapEx reserve (6%) | -$1,152 |
| Landlord insurance | -$1,500 |
| Property tax (1.1% of $185K) | -$2,035 |
| **NOI** | **$8,667** |
| **Net cap rate (NOI / $185K)** | **~4.7%** |

Mortgage (P+I on $138,750 at 7.1%, 30-year) ≈ $936 / month / $11,232 per year.
Cash flow after debt service ≈ **–$2,565 / year (negative).**

That deal does not cash-flow on current leverage costs. The same property bought with more equity — say, 40% down — produces a mortgage of roughly $749 / month ($111,000 at 7.1%), reducing the annual debt service to $8,988. Cash flow: approximately **–$321 / year (near-flat).**

At all-cash, the deal generates roughly a 4.7% cash-on-cash return while building equity and generating depreciation deductions. The practical takeaway: Augusta is not a turn-key cash-flow market at current interest rates unless you bring significant equity or find a property priced meaningfully below the median. Investors who make Augusta work are buying below $160,000 on renovated properties, bringing more equity, holding long-term, or targeting Columbia County where rents are higher relative to vacancy and turnover costs.

## What Actually Eats Into Your Return

Out-of-state investors often model optimistically on three line items: vacancy, turnover cost, and CapEx.

**Vacancy.** A 5% vacancy assumption means roughly 18 days vacant per year. In Augusta proper, the realistic range is 30–45 days between tenancies, particularly in lower-demand ZIPs. Columbia County (Evans, Grovetown) consistently runs shorter vacancy periods. Use 7–10% for Augusta proper and 5–7% for strong Columbia County submarkets.

**Turnover cost.** Each tenant change in Augusta carries $800–$1,500 in make-ready costs (cleaning, minor repairs, paint touch-ups, carpet cleaning) even on a well-maintained property, plus a leasing fee. A property that turns every 18 months is burning $600–$1,000 per year in amortized turnover cost on top of vacancy.

**CapEx.** The majority of Augusta's investor-grade rental inventory was built between 1960 and 2000. A 1985 brick ranch with original HVAC, aging roof, and 20-year-old water heater is a CapEx event waiting to happen. Underfunding the CapEx reserve — common among first-time investors — produces negative cash-flow years that feel like bad luck but were baked into the underwriting from day one.

## Buying Sight-Unseen: Due Diligence From 1,000 Miles Away

Most of our out-of-state owners never saw their property before the first tenant moved in. That is not unusual — but it requires a reliable due-diligence chain you cannot shortcut.

Here is the checklist we walk out-of-state investors through before they sign closing docs:

1. **Pull rent comps from a local source.** Contact a CSRA property manager or buyer's agent to pull rental comps for the specific ZIP code and bedroom count. Asking rents on listing portals and your PM's current inventory are more accurate than metro-wide averages for underwriting.
2. **Order a third-party inspection.** Hire a Georgia-licensed home inspector who reports in writing. For older Augusta properties, request HVAC service history and a roof certification. Never waive inspection on a sight-unseen purchase — this is where investors get burned.
3. **Review title, HOA rules, and flood zone.** Check for open permits, liens, HOA restrictions, and FEMA flood zone designation. Augusta properties near the Savannah River corridor sit in Special Flood Hazard Areas; flood insurance adds $900–$2,500 per year to operating costs. See our [HOA restrictions guide](/blog/georgia-hoa-rental-restrictions-csra-landlords/) for what to look for in HOA covenants.
4. **Confirm Georgia HB 399 compliance.** Out-of-state LLCs and individuals must register with the Georgia Secretary of State or appoint an in-state registered agent before renting. Your property manager can often serve in this role. See our [Georgia HB 399 compliance guide](/blog/georgia-hb-399-csra-out-of-state-landlord-compliance/) for details.
5. **Get a pre-close repair estimate.** Ask your PM to do a walkthrough and produce a deferred-maintenance list with rough costs. This becomes your negotiation leverage and your Year 1 CapEx budget.
6. **Model the deal with actual costs.** Use 8–10% vacancy for Augusta proper and 5–7% for Columbia County suburbs. Include management fee, leasing fee, insurance, taxes, CapEx reserve, and repairs before deciding a deal works.
7. **Set up owner distributions.** Open a dedicated account to receive owner draws. McBride PM wires owner distributions directly — typically by the 10th of the month following collection.
