## Aiken SC Neighborhoods: What Renters Need to Know

Aiken covers a meaningful amount of ground, and where you land matters both for commute and daily convenience.

**Downtown Aiken** is the most walkable part of the city—a historic commercial district with restaurants, boutiques, and the Aiken Center for the Arts. Rentals here tend to be renovated historic homes or small apartment buildings, and they command somewhat higher rents. The character is distinct; if you want to walk somewhere after work, downtown Aiken is your best option in the area.

**South Aiken and the Woodside area** offer quieter, more residential neighborhoods with larger single-family homes. Rental inventory here leans toward three- and four-bedroom houses. The commute to SRS is still well under 30 minutes from this part of the city.

**Graniteville** (eastern Aiken County, along I-20) is more affordable and convenient for renters commuting to both SRS and Augusta. The I-20 access makes this corridor practical for anyone working on either side of the Savannah River. Fewer walkable amenities than downtown Aiken, but it's the right trade for renters prioritizing price and highway access.

**Warrenville and surrounding rural communities** offer the lowest rents in Aiken County. Rental inventory is limited, and you'll need a car for everything, but for renters who prioritize space and quiet it's a workable option.

## South Carolina Tenant Rights: What the SCRLTA Requires

Residential rentals in South Carolina are governed by the **South Carolina Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (SCRLTA)**, codified at [SC Code §27-40-10 through §27-40-940](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php). Here's what matters most to a tenant renting in Aiken:

**Your landlord must keep the property habitable.** Under SC Code §27-40-440, landlords must comply with applicable building and housing codes materially affecting health and safety, maintain structural components in good repair, and provide functioning heating, cooling, plumbing, and electrical systems. If your landlord fails to make a required repair after written notice, you have remedies under §27-40-610—but the rules for rent withholding and repair-and-deduct are specific and a South Carolina-licensed attorney should be consulted before you take any self-help action.

**Landlord entry requires 24-hour notice.** SC Code §27-40-530 requires your landlord to give at least 24 hours' advance notice before entering and to enter only at reasonable times. The exception is a genuine emergency—fire, flooding, or similar—where immediate entry is permitted without notice.

**How notice periods work when leaving or being asked to leave:**

- Month-to-month tenancy: either party must give **30 days' written notice** to terminate (SC §27-40-770)
- Week-to-week tenancy: **7 days' written notice**
- Fixed-term lease: expires at the end of the stated term; no additional notice is required from either party if both sides agree to end at the term date

**If you fall behind on rent:** Your landlord must deliver a **5-day written notice to pay or vacate** before filing an ejectment action for nonpayment (SC §27-40-710(B)). If you pay the full amount owed within those 5 days, the landlord cannot proceed with the case. For lease violations other than nonpayment, the notice period is 14 days to cure. ## Security Deposits, Pet Policies, and What to Do Before You Sign

**Security deposits in SC:** South Carolina's SCRLTA (§27-40-410) sets no maximum on the security deposit amount. In practice, one to two months' rent is the most common range in Aiken. Budget for at least one full month's rent as deposit when planning your move-in costs, in addition to first month's rent.

Your landlord must return the deposit—or send an itemized written statement of deductions—within **30 days** after you vacate and deliver possession. Each deduction must be listed by description and dollar amount. If the landlord misses the 30-day deadline without cause, you may be entitled to the full deposit regardless of any actual damage to the unit.

**The move-in inspection is your most important protection.** Request a written walk-through before or on move-in day, document the condition of every room and appliance with timestamped photos, and make sure the landlord signs the inspection form. This is your evidence if the landlord tries to charge you for pre-existing damage at move-out. McBride PM provides a written inspection form for every new tenant—you can download our [Move-In Inspection Checklist](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Move-In-Inspection-Checklist.pdf) and use it as your reference even if you're renting from a different landlord.

**Pet policies** are set entirely by each landlord—SC law does not require acceptance of pets. When landlords do accept them, typical Aiken-area terms run $250–$500 as a refundable pet deposit or $25–$75 per month as a non-refundable pet fee. Service animals and emotional support animals with proper documentation under the Fair Housing Act are fully exempt from all pet fees and deposits. ### Pre-signing checklist for renters in Aiken, SC

Before you sign a lease anywhere in the CSRA, confirm these items:

- [ ] Walk the unit with the landlord or property manager; get a written move-in inspection form signed by both parties before or on move-in day
- [ ] Confirm in writing which utilities are included in rent (water, trash, gas, electric) and which are your responsibility
- [ ] Verify the notice period required to terminate or renew, and the exact lease end date
- [ ] Get a written pet policy addendum if you have a pet
- [ ] Confirm the security deposit amount, how it is held, and the 30-day return deadline
- [ ] Test smoke detectors and carbon monoxide detectors on move-in day and document that they work
- [ ] Get your landlord's full legal name, emergency contact number, and the process for submitting maintenance requests in writing
