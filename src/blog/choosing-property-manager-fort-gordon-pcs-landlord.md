That approach works fine when things are routine. It fails—sometimes expensively—when something goes wrong with an SCRA termination, a maintenance emergency you can't respond to from Germany, or a trust account dispute.

## Why PCS Landlords Face Different Risks Than Other Owners

Most landlords who hire a property manager live nearby. If something goes sideways, they can drive over. They can call an attorney. They can show up at the magistrate court in Columbia County or Richmond County if it comes to that.

You can't. That asymmetry is the reason that picking a property manager deserves more than a quick Google search. For a Fort Gordon PCS landlord, the manager becomes your legal and operational proxy. Every decision they make on your behalf — repair authorizations, tenant communications, lease enforcement — happens without your direct involvement.

There's also a Fort Gordon-specific wrinkle: a meaningful share of your likely tenant pool will be active-duty military. That means your manager needs to be fluent in the [Servicemembers Civil Relief Act](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/), which governs how military tenants can break their lease on 30 days' notice when they receive PCS or deployment orders. A manager who handles that procedure incorrectly — or drags their feet, or tries to hold the tenant to penalty clauses — creates liability for you, not for them.

None of this means you need an exceptionally complex management arrangement. It means you need a manager who has handled these situations before and has written protocols for each one. The vetting process below is designed to find that manager quickly.

## The Non-Negotiable First Check: Georgia Licensure

This isn't a nice-to-have — it's the law. Under [O.C.G.A. Title 43, Chapter 40](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2022/title-43/chapter-40/section-43-40-10/), anyone who manages real property for compensation on behalf of another person must hold an active Georgia real estate broker's license, or work directly under a licensed broker. An unlicensed manager cannot legally collect rent, sign leases, or take fees for property management services.

The practical risk: if something goes wrong and your manager isn't licensed, you lose the legal protections that come with licensed brokerage. Trust account requirements, E&O insurance obligations, and regulatory oversight all depend on that license. An unlicensed operator has no regulator to answer to.

Verification takes two minutes. Go to the [Georgia Real Estate Commission's](https://grec.state.ga.us) public license search, enter the company name or the broker's name, and confirm:

- **Status:** Active (not expired, suspended, or revoked)
- **License type:** Broker (not salesperson — a salesperson cannot operate independently)
- **Disciplinary history:** No recent sanctions or consent orders

Also ask which individual broker holds the license and whether that person is actively involved in managing your property. Some companies list a broker who signed the paperwork years ago but isn't present in day-to-day operations. You want a named broker who supervises the team that handles your account.

## SCRA Fluency: The Test That Separates Serious Managers From the Rest

Ask every candidate this question directly: "Walk me through exactly what happens when a military tenant presents PCS orders and wants to terminate their lease early."

The correct answer follows a specific procedure under [50 U.S.C. § 3955](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title50-section3955):

1. The tenant delivers written notice of termination plus a copy of official military orders.
2. If the lease requires monthly rent, the termination is effective 30 days after the next rent due date following the date notice is delivered.
3. The tenant gets their security deposit back within 30 days of departure (subject to normal condition deductions).

A manager who handles this correctly treats it as a standard, expected event — because it is, near Fort Gordon. A manager who expresses frustration, says they "try to work something out," or suggests charging a lease-break fee to a departing service member is showing you exactly how they'll handle the next one.

If you're a Fort Gordon landlord, this will happen to you at some point. The question is whether your manager has a documented process or makes it up each time.

You can deepen this test by asking about the [DMDC SCRA database verification](https://scra.dmdc.osd.mil/) — the Defense Manpower Data Center portal that landlords and managers can use to verify active-duty status before accepting or processing an SCRA request. A manager who references that tool unprompted has done this work before.

Also ask how they handle the scenario where a tenant receives deployment orders (not PCS — just a 6-month deployment). That's different: the SCRA doesn't automatically terminate the lease for a deployment, though it does provide protections against eviction under certain circumstances. The nuance matters, and a manager who can explain it has the depth of knowledge you need.

## Financial Transparency and Trust Account Management

Every licensed Georgia property manager is required by [Georgia Real Estate Commission rules](https://grec.state.ga.us) to maintain client funds — your security deposits and rental proceeds — in a designated, separate trust account. It cannot be commingled with the company's operating funds. This is the law, and it's enforced.

What this means for you: when you interview a manager, ask specifically how their trust account is structured. Acceptable answers include a named bank, a separate account maintained in the company's broker name on behalf of clients, and a reconciliation process. Vague answers about "we keep everything organized" are not acceptable.

Beyond the trust account, verify what financial reporting you'll actually receive. For PCS landlords, monthly transparency is essential — you need to see rental income, management fees, maintenance charges, and your net distribution in a format you can read on a phone from wherever you're stationed. At McBride PM, [owners see real-time data through the AppFolio owner portal](/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/), including every maintenance charge and work order before they're applied to your account.

Also confirm how distributions reach you. If you'll be stationed overseas, ask specifically whether their ACH system supports international transfers or whether you'll need to designate a U.S.-based account. Some managers haven't set this up and will tell you they can "work something out" — which means you'll be chasing wire transfers manually. That's not a sustainable arrangement for a Fort Gordon PCS owner.

## Communication Systems for Remote and Deployed Owners

The question isn't just whether a manager returns phone calls. It's what happens when you're in a 30-day communications blackout, an exercise window where your personal cell is locked up, or a time zone 7 hours ahead of Georgia.

A capable manager for a PCS landlord has three things in place:

**An owner portal with 24/7 access.** You should be able to log in at 2 a.m. from a base in Europe and see whether your tenant paid rent, what maintenance requests are open, and what your current balance is. This isn't optional — it's the minimum standard for remote property management.

**A written maintenance authorization threshold.** This is the dollar amount the manager can spend on a repair without reaching you. For deployed owners, this number needs to be calibrated carefully. Too low, and every $200 plumbing call requires a phone tag with an overseas number. Too high, and you might find out about a $2,000 HVAC repair after the fact. Most CSRA owners working with McBride PM set this between $300 and $500, with higher-dollar decisions going through the owner portal as a documented request you can respond to whenever you have connectivity.

**An emergency contact designation.** If you'll be genuinely unreachable for periods, your management agreement should allow you to designate a family member, attorney, or trusted contact who can make time-sensitive decisions on your behalf. Confirm the manager has a formal process for this, not just an informal "just give us your spouse's number."

For [military families PCSing from Fort Gordon](/blog/pcs-fort-gordon-rent-home-landlord-timeline/), the 60-to-90-day window before departure is the right time to get these systems configured. Don't leave that setup to the week before you move out.

## The 10 Questions to Ask Every CSRA Property Manager

Use this list in your interviews. Take notes. The answers reveal operational depth faster than any reference check.

| # | Question | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|----------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | What is your license number and who is the supervising broker? | Named broker, verifiable number, broker is active day-to-day |
| 2 | Walk me through your SCRA lease termination procedure. | Cites statute, 30-day rule, written notice plus orders |
| 3 | How do you handle maintenance when I'm overseas and unreachable? | Named authorization threshold, written policy |
| 4 | What platform do owners use to view their account? | AppFolio, Buildium, or similar — not spreadsheets |
| 5 | How is my security deposit held and in which bank? | Separate trust account, named bank, broker name on account |
| 6 | When do owners receive their monthly distribution? | Specific date — "by the 10th" is a real answer |
| 7 | What is your vacancy rate across your current portfolio? | Should be able to give a rough figure — if they can't, they're not tracking it |
| 8 | What happens if a tenant I haven't placed causes damage that exceeds the deposit? | Describes a process, not a shrug |
| 9 | Have you managed a property that went through a Georgia dispossessory? Walk me through it. | Specific answer citing Richmond or Columbia County Magistrate Court procedures |
| 10 | What do you charge? Give me the full fee schedule in writing. | Delivers it immediately, without hedging |

The last one is a process test as much as a pricing question. Managers who say "it depends" without an immediate follow-up document are either making fees up case by case or hoping you won't notice the extras. ## Red Flags to Watch For During the Interview

A single red flag in isolation isn't necessarily disqualifying. A pattern is. Watch for:

**Inability to cite the SCRA procedure.** This is the Fort Gordon-specific test. A manager who can't explain the statutory procedure for military lease terminations without looking it up is not set up to serve PCS landlords. This gap creates real exposure for you.

**No owner portal.** If monthly reporting means a PDF emailed to you at some point in the month, that's a 2015-era operation. Owner portals aren't optional for 2026 property management — they're how you verify your numbers from wherever you are.

**Vague or shifting fee structures.** If you ask for a written fee schedule and get a verbal summary with caveats, that manager is not operating transparently. Full-fee disclosure in writing before you sign is a minimum standard under NARPM's [Code of Ethics](https://www.narpm.org/members/standards-of-professionalism/code-of-ethics/).

**They've never heard of [BAH rates](/blog/fort-gordon-bah-rental-pricing-csra-2026/).** A Fort Gordon-aware manager tracks Basic Allowance for Housing rates because they affect what military tenants can pay and how to price your property. If your candidate has no awareness of BAH rates in the CSRA, they're not operating in the military tenant market in any meaningful way.

**Pushback on the authorization threshold conversation.** Some managers resist putting a written maintenance threshold in the management agreement because it limits their flexibility. That flexibility is at your expense. Any threshold written into the contract is there to protect you — a manager who doesn't want that in writing has a reason.

**No written emergency protocol for deployed owners.** If their answer to "what happens if I'm deployed and you can't reach me" is "we'll figure it out," that's not a plan.

## A Checklist Before You Sign

Before executing any management agreement, confirm you have:

- [ ] Verified the broker's license at grec.state.ga.us — Active status, no sanctions
- [ ] Received a full written fee schedule (management fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, maintenance markup policy)
- [ ] Confirmed the trust account bank and account structure in writing
- [ ] Verified they have a documented SCRA lease termination procedure
- [ ] Confirmed the owner portal (AppFolio or equivalent) with 24/7 access
- [ ] Agreed on the maintenance authorization threshold and written it into the agreement
- [ ] Set up or confirmed your preferred distribution method, including international options if needed
- [ ] Designated a stateside emergency contact if you'll be periodically unreachable
- [ ] Received a timeline for the baseline inspection, pre-listing work, and marketing launch

That checklist takes about an hour to work through with a candidate. Any capable manager will walk through it with you without resistance — these are standard owner protections.
