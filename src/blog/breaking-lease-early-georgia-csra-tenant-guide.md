**Can you break a lease early in Georgia?**
Georgia law gives residential tenants almost no general early termination right — if you leave without a recognized legal basis, you can owe rent through the end of your lease on a vacant unit. Three statutory exits exist: military PCS orders under the SCRA, a qualifying protective order for family violence or stalking (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-23), and landlord-caused uninhabitability.

</div>

Something in your life has changed. PCS orders arrived last week. A job relocated you out of state. A situation at home made staying unsafe. Whatever brought you here, you're looking at a lease with months remaining and asking whether you can leave without financially wrecking yourself in the process.

Here's what you need to understand before any calls, before packing, before you make a decision you can't take back: Georgia is one of the harder states for tenants who want to exit early. Under long-standing Georgia common law, confirmed in *Peterson v. P.C. Towers, L.P.*, landlords face no general duty to try to re-rent after you leave. Your landlord can, in most cases, leave your unit vacant and pursue you for every month of remaining rent on a legally binding contract.

That doesn't mean you're trapped. But it does mean knowing exactly which exits exist, whether you qualify for one, and — when none apply — how to negotiate the most favorable outcome. This guide covers every scenario for CSRA renters, from Evans and Grovetown to North Augusta and Aiken. Whether your situation is military, medical, personal, or financial, start here.

*This is general guidance from a property manager, not legal advice. For your specific situation, talk to a Georgia attorney or contact [Georgia Legal Aid](https://www.georgialegalaid.org) at 1-800-498-9469.*

## Georgia's Default Rule: You Owe the Lease

Georgia has no statute giving residential tenants a general right to terminate a lease early. If you sign a 12-month lease and leave after month five, your landlord is entitled to pursue you for the seven remaining months — even on an empty unit.

This rule puts real weight on what you signed. Before assuming you're stuck, review your lease closely. Some leases include a negotiated early termination clause that names a specific buyout fee — typically one to two months' rent. If your lease has one, that's your cleanest path: follow it exactly and get any release confirmed in writing. If it doesn't, the statutory exits below are what matter.

For a fuller look at your baseline rights as a Georgia renter, our [Georgia Tenant Rights 2026 guide](/blog/georgia-tenant-rights-2026-csra-renter-guide/) covers the full landscape of protections in force for CSRA residents.

## Exit #1: Military PCS or Deployment Orders

For active-duty service members and their households renting near Fort Gordon, this is the most reliable and well-defined early exit available.

Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-22](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-44-property/ga-code-sect-44-7-22/) and federal law under [50 U.S.C. § 3955](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/legal-assistance/legal-assistance-topics/servicemembers-civil-relief-act-scra/) (the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act), a service member can terminate a residential lease early when:

- They receive permanent change of station (PCS) orders to a new duty location, **or**
- They receive deployment orders to a location at least 35 miles from the rental unit for a period of 90 days or more, **or**
- The lease was signed **before** entering active military service

**What you need to deliver:**

1. Written notice of your intent to terminate the lease
2. A copy of your official PCS or deployment orders — if official orders haven't been issued yet, a memorandum from your commanding officer stating when you're expected to deploy is acceptable

**When the lease ends:** Your lease terminates 30 days after the next regularly scheduled rent payment due date following delivery of your notice. If you deliver notice on October 20 and rent is due November 1, your lease ends November 30. You owe rent through that full termination date.

**How to deliver:** Use a method that creates a paper trail — certified mail with return receipt is standard. Submit notice through your resident portal as well and note that certified mail is following. Keep copies of everything.

**One critical detail:** The SCRA protection cannot be waived inside a lease. Any clause attempting to strip you of these rights is unenforceable under federal law.

For families renting in [Evans](/evans/), Grovetown, or Martinez — neighborhoods that sit within the main commute corridors for Fort Gordon's main gates and the Army Cyber Center of Excellence — this provision is used regularly. Fort Gordon's Legal Assistance Office can help confirm your specific orders qualify and review your notice letter before you send it. Our separate guide on [SCRA lease protections for CSRA landlords](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/) explains how the same provision looks from the property owner's side, which may be useful context.

## Exit #2: Family Violence or Stalking (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-23)

[O.C.G.A. § 44-7-23](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-44-property/ga-code-sect-44-7-23/) gives tenants who have received a qualifying civil or criminal protective order for family violence or stalking the right to terminate a lease early with 30 days' written notice.

**To use this exit:**

- You must have a qualifying civil or criminal protective order issued by a Georgia court to protect you or your minor child
- You provide the landlord written notice of termination with a copy of the protective order attached
- If the order is a **temporary ex parte protective order**, you must also include a copy of the police report filed in connection with the violence or stalking

Your lease terminates 30 days after the landlord receives this notice. You are responsible only for prorated rent through the termination date. No early termination fee can be charged. This provision cannot be waived or modified by any lease agreement, under any circumstances.

**A note on 2025–2026 legislative activity:** The Georgia General Assembly was considering amendments to § 44-7-23 in its current session — SB 413 would revise definitions of qualifying orders, and HB 188 would extend related tenant protections. Check [legis.ga.gov](https://legis.ga.gov) or contact Georgia Legal Aid to confirm the current statutory language before you rely on it.

If you need to use this exit while renting a McBride PM property, you may contact ops@mcbride-pm.com directly. Amber McBride, our operations manager, handles these tenant transitions with discretion and will guide you through the documentation process.

## Exit #3: Constructive Eviction (Uninhabitable Unit)

If your landlord has failed to maintain the unit in a fit and habitable condition — and has refused to fix serious problems after receiving your written notice — Georgia law may allow you to terminate through constructive eviction.

Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13](https://legis.ga.gov), as strengthened by the Safe at Home Act (HB 404, effective July 1, 2024), landlords must keep residential rental units safe, sanitary, and fit for human habitation. Conditions serious enough to support constructive eviction typically include:

- Sewage backup or complete loss of functioning plumbing
- No heat source during cold weather (landlords must provide a unit capable of being heated to at least 68°F)
- Severe documented mold with established health impact
- Structural failures creating immediate safety risk

**This exit requires thorough documentation before you leave.** You must put your landlord on written notice of the specific condition, allow a reasonable period for repair, and document that they failed to act. Leaving without that paper trail puts you in a weak position if the landlord pursues unpaid rent.

Do not rely on this exit without talking to a Georgia attorney first, or without first [contacting McBride PM](/contact/) so we can assess whether the condition you're describing crosses the threshold. Constructive eviction claims are fact-dependent and courts take them seriously as a legal standard, not a general complaint about poor conditions.

## When None of the Legal Exits Apply

Most tenants who need to leave early — job transfers, relationship changes, financial hardship, personal decisions — don't qualify for any statutory exit. Georgia law does not recognize these as early termination grounds. Your options when there's no statutory basis:

| Option | What You're Negotiating | Typical Cost | What It Requires |
|--------|------------------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Mutual early termination agreement | Written release from the landlord | 1–2 months' rent (negotiated) | Landlord's willingness |
| Sublease or lease assignment | New tenant takes over your lease | Screening fee; landlord approval required | Lease allows it; landlord approves new tenant |
| Month-to-month notice | Standard 30-day notice to end tenancy | No ETF; 30 days' notice only | You're already on month-to-month |
| Help find a replacement tenant | Offer to cooperate in re-renting | Overlap rent until unit is re-rented | Time and landlord cooperation |
| Walk away with no agreement | Nothing — you stop paying and leave | Up to full remaining term; potential judgment | Accepting the full financial and credit risk |

**The most practical path for most tenants: communicate early and negotiate.** Contact your property manager before you stop paying rent, before you've already left, and before a formal demand is necessary. Many professional property management companies — including McBride PM — would rather work with a cooperative, communicative tenant on a reasonable buyout than go through the cost and time of a dispossessory action and a debt-collection effort.

The earlier you come to the table, the more options you have. A tenant who contacts us two months before they need to leave creates time to pre-market the unit, run applications, and arrange a clean handoff. A tenant who disappears and stops responding leaves us no choice but to pursue the available legal remedies.

**Subletting:** Most professionally managed leases require written approval before you can sublet or assign your lease to another person. If you find a qualified replacement tenant on your own, presenting them to your property manager is the right move — but understand that you remain responsible under the original lease until the new tenant signs a new agreement with the landlord. Approval is not guaranteed.

**The credit and collection risk is real.** A dispossessory judgment in Columbia County or Richmond County Magistrate Court can result in wage garnishment under Georgia law. It will show up in tenant-screening databases and on your credit report. Professional property managers screen for prior eviction judgments — including from other Georgia markets — as a standard part of the application process. A judgment follows you.

1. **Review your lease agreement.** Find the renewal, termination, and notice provisions. Check for an early termination clause before assuming the default rule applies.
2. **Identify which exit, if any, applies.** Military orders? Protective order? Habitability issue? Confirm the required documentation is in your hands before you notify anyone.
3. **Contact your property manager in writing.** Use the AppFolio resident portal or email ops@mcbride-pm.com. Do not give only verbal notice and assume it's on record.
4. **Send formal written notice with required attachments.** Download the [30-Day Notice to Vacate form](/pdfs/McBride-PM_30-Day-Notice-to-Vacate.pdf). For statutory exits, attach the required documentation — copy of orders, copy of protective order, or police report as applicable. Send by certified mail with return receipt as a paper trail backup.
5. **Continue paying rent through the notice period.** Stopping payment before the lease is formally terminated creates an independent unpaid-rent claim on top of the early termination issue. Don't give your landlord two problems to work with.
6. **Document the unit's current condition.** Photograph and video every room before you start moving furniture. Save all AppFolio maintenance request records and communications.
7. **Schedule your move-out inspection.** Download the [Move-Out Inspection Checklist](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Move-Out-Inspection-Checklist.pdf) and walk through the unit before your scheduled inspection date. Bring it with you.
8. **Return all keys, access cards, fobs, and remotes on your final day.** Provide a forwarding address in writing — your security deposit return notice goes to that address.

## What Happens to Your Security Deposit When You Leave Early?

Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-34](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-44-property/ga-code-sect-44-7-34/), your landlord has 30 days from the end of your tenancy to either return your deposit in full or send an itemized written statement of deductions with any remaining balance. Early termination doesn't change that window.

What it can change is what's deductible. If you break a lease without a statutory exit, your landlord may be entitled to deduct legitimate re-renting costs from your deposit — advertising expenses, make-ready cleaning and repairs beyond normal wear, and costs related to vacancy. These are in addition to any legal claim for unpaid rent, not a substitute for it.

If you leave under a statutory exit — SCRA, § 44-7-23, or constructive eviction — you're responsible only for prorated rent through your termination date and physical damages beyond normal wear and tear. Lost future rent and re-renting expenses cannot be charged under these statutory exits.

Our detailed guide on [getting your security deposit back in Georgia](/blog/georgia-security-deposit-return-csra-tenant-guide/) walks through what landlords can and cannot deduct, the itemized statement requirement, and what to do if you believe you've been charged improperly.

One practical note: Always provide a written forwarding address when you return your keys. Under Georgia law, if the landlord cannot locate you within 90 days to return the deposit, they may be entitled to keep it. Leave no room for that outcome.
