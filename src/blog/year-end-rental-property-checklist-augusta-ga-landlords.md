**What should CSRA landlords review before the end of the year?**
Before December 31, residential rental owners in Augusta, Evans, Grovetown, and surrounding communities should reconcile their AppFolio owner statements, project their Schedule E positions, assess maintenance reserves, make renewal decisions for Q1 2027 leases, and build a written property budget for 2027. Mid-November is the right window — you still have time to act on what you find.

You have roughly seven weeks left in 2026. That's enough time to run a thorough year-end financial review for a small rental portfolio — but not enough time to put it off until December and still do it well.

Most CSRA landlords who self-manage or work with a property management company don't do a formal year-end review. They wait for their CPA to ask for the Schedule E documents in February and scramble to piece together twelve months of receipts. That approach leaves money on the table, misses Q4 tax-planning moves, and means you're making 2027 decisions — lease renewals, rent prices, maintenance capital — without the full picture of how 2026 actually performed.

This guide walks through what a year-end review should cover, in the order that makes the most practical sense to work through it. Property owners working with McBride Property Management have the advantage of AppFolio's owner portal, which makes most of the data retrieval straightforward. If you're self-managing, the same framework applies — you'll need to gather the records yourself.

## Start With Your Owner Statements, Not Your Bank Account

Your AppFolio owner statement is the right starting document for year-end reconciliation — not your bank account, and not a spreadsheet you've been updating on and off. The owner statement shows gross rent collected, vacancy periods, all expenses paid by McBride PM on your behalf, and net distributions transferred to you, organized by property and period.

To pull yours for the full year:
1. Log into your AppFolio owner portal (accessible at any time, including from your phone).
2. Navigate to Reports → Owner Statement.
3. Set the date range to January 1–December 31, 2026.
4. Download the PDF.

This is the document your CPA will want to see. The first reconciliation task is simple: compare the total distributions shown on the statement against your bank records. If they match, you're starting clean. If there's a gap, flag it now — before year-end, while the detail is easy to recover — rather than trying to reconstruct it in March.

For a detailed walkthrough of what every line on the statement means, see the [guide to reading your monthly owner statement from McBride PM](/blog/reading-owner-statement-property-management-augusta-ga/).

## Seven Numbers Every CSRA Landlord Should Know Right Now

Before making any 2027 decisions, you need to know where your portfolio actually stands in 2026. These seven metrics give you the full picture:

| Metric | What It Measures | Where to Get It |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rent collected | Total rent billed and paid | AppFolio Owner Statement |
| Vacancy rate | Days vacant ÷ total days × 100 | AppFolio or manual calculation |
| Operating expense ratio | Total operating expenses ÷ gross rent | Calculate from statement |
| Net operating income (NOI) | Gross rent − vacancy loss − operating expenses | Calculate from statement |
| Maintenance reserve balance | Funds set aside for future repairs | AppFolio or your records |
| Average days to fill vacancy | Days from listing to signed lease | AppFolio leasing data |
| Rent-to-price ratio | Annual gross rent ÷ purchase price | Manual calculation |

Your NOI is the most important of these for year-end planning. A property that shows positive cash flow on paper but has a depleted maintenance reserve and an aging HVAC system has a very different risk profile than one where the reserve is healthy and all systems are under ten years old. Both numbers tell you something the other doesn't.

For single-family rentals in the CSRA, a healthy operating expense ratio typically runs 35–50% of gross rent. If yours is running above 55%, something is elevated — usually repairs, vacancy, management fees above market rate, or a capital event you didn't plan for. That's a conversation worth having before January.

## What to Reconcile for Schedule E Before December 31

Your Schedule E for the 2026 tax year will organize income and expenses into the IRS's standard categories: rents received, advertising, cleaning and maintenance, insurance, legal and professional fees, management fees, mortgage interest, taxes, and depreciation. For a step-by-step breakdown of how Schedule E works for Georgia rental property owners, the post on [Schedule E for CSRA landlords](/blog/schedule-e-first-year-landlord-georgia-csra/) goes through each line.

The goal of a November reconciliation isn't to do your taxes now — it's to identify whether any categories need action before December 31:

**Repairs and maintenance:** Under IRS Publication 527, repairs that restore a property to its prior condition are generally deductible in the year completed. Repairs completed and invoiced by December 31 are deductible in 2026. If you have items on your inspection list that you've been deferring — a water heater showing its age, exterior paint starting to peel, a heat pump that needs service — doing them in November or December rather than January captures the deduction a year earlier. Don't rush poor-quality work for the deduction, but don't delay a needed repair into the new year arbitrarily either.

**Capital improvements versus repairs:** This distinction matters. A new roof, a full HVAC replacement, a deck addition, or a full kitchen remodel are capital improvements that must be depreciated over time, not deducted in the year of completion. Confirm with your CPA which 2026 work falls into each category. See [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527) for the official guidance.

**Insurance premiums:** Confirm your landlord insurance policy (not homeowners — a rental requires a [landlord-specific policy](/blog/landlord-insurance-georgia-rental-property-guide/)) is current and that the 2026 premium is recorded as a rental expense.

**1099-NEC requirement:** If you paid any individual contractor, handyman, or vendor $600 or more directly (not through a property management company) for rental property work during 2026, you're generally required to issue Form 1099-NEC by January 31, 2027. Collect W-9 forms from anyone you paid directly but haven't documented yet. This is general guidance — not legal or tax advice; talk to a CPA for your situation.

**Depreciation basis:** If you completed significant capital improvements in 2026 — a new HVAC system, a roof replacement — confirm that your CPA has recorded them separately from the original basis. Each capital improvement has its own depreciation schedule under [IRS Schedule E instructions](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-e-form-1040).

## Review Your Maintenance Reserve and CapEx Timeline

The maintenance reserve is the buffer between you and an unexpected $6,500 HVAC replacement call in January. The [CapEx planning guide for CSRA rental properties](/blog/capex-planning-csra-rental-properties/) goes deep on how to build and right-size this reserve. For the year-end review, the question is simpler: does your reserve position reflect the actual age and condition of each property's systems?

A widely-used rule of thumb for single-family rentals: hold 5–10% of annual gross rent in a dedicated maintenance reserve. For a home renting at $1,800 per month ($21,600 per year), that's $1,080 to $2,160 in reserve. If 2026 was a rough year for repairs — a failed water heater, a pest remediation, an emergency repair — your reserve may be depleted. November is the time to replenish it, not in March when a cold snap hits.

For each property in your portfolio, work through this checklist before year-end:

**Systems age and condition:**
- [ ] HVAC — when was it last serviced? How old is the unit? (Expected life in CSRA heat and humidity: 12–15 years for most units)
- [ ] Water heater — age and condition (most tank heaters last 8–12 years; tankless last 15–20)
- [ ] Roof — last inspection date; gutters cleared after fall leaf drop?
- [ ] Electrical panel — any aging or undersized panel flagged in a recent inspection?
- [ ] Plumbing — slow drains, evidence of leaks, corroded fittings at supply lines?
- [ ] Exterior — caulking around windows and doors, weatherstripping, exterior paint condition?
- [ ] Smoke and CO detectors — tested and in compliance with Georgia's habitability requirements?

If your property is in Evans (ZIP 30809) or Grovetown (ZIP 30813) — the Columbia County submarket that sees the highest concentration of Fort Gordon and medical professional tenants — expect a higher standard of system maintenance. Tenants in those ZIP codes have strong alternatives, and a property with aging systems shows up in vacancy numbers faster than you'd expect.

For specific cost benchmarks on water heaters and HVAC in the CSRA, see the [water heater replacement guide](/blog/water-heater-repair-replace-csra-rental-property-landlord-guide/) and [HVAC guide for Augusta-area rentals](/blog/hvac-csra-rental-property-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/).

## Making Renewal Decisions Before the Holidays

If you have tenants whose leases expire in January or February 2027, your window to send a renewal offer is right now — not in mid-December. Leases sent for signature the week before Christmas rarely get signed before New Year's. A tenant who goes into January without a signed renewal is a tenant who's already browsing Zillow.

The renewal decision breaks into three parts:

**1. Renew or not renew?** Has the tenant paid on time? Maintained the property? Generated no HOA complaints or neighbor issues? If the answer to all three is yes, keeping this tenant is almost always the better financial decision. Tenant turnover in the CSRA typically costs $1,500–$3,500 in vacancy, cleaning, make-ready repairs, and leasing costs. A good tenant at the same rent beats a good new tenant who costs you two months to find.

**2. At what rent?** The [lease renewal pricing guide for CSRA rental owners](/blog/lease-renewal-pricing-csra-rental-property-owners/) covers the market math in full. For the 2027 renewal cycle, [Trulia rent trend data from mid-2026](https://www.trulia.com/average-rent-market-trends/augusta-ga/) shows average rents in the Augusta area running roughly $1,350 across all property types, with 3-bedroom homes in Columbia County ranging from $1,550 to $1,850 depending on submarket and condition. A 3–5% renewal increase on a good tenant is reasonable and defensible; a jump above 7–8% risks a vacancy you won't fill quickly in January.

**3. What lease term?** A 12-month renewal gives you the most control over next year's renewal timing. A month-to-month conversion gives flexibility but raises risk — either party can end it on 30 days' notice. For properties with Fort Gordon tenants, a 12-month renewal with an SCRA early-termination clause is typically the right structure.

For properties where the tenant is a service member, note that Basic Allowance for Housing rates reset each January 1. Pricing a renewal that takes effect January 1, 2027 means your rent aligns with what the tenant's new BAH will be — which affects what they can comfortably pay. See the [Fort Gordon BAH rental pricing guide](/blog/fort-gordon-bah-rental-pricing-csra-2026/) for 2026 rate context.

## Building Your 2027 Property Budget

A written budget — even a simple one — is what separates landlords who are surprised by their rental's performance from landlords who are actively managing it. Build yours from your actual 2026 numbers, not national benchmarks.

**2027 rental property budget (single-family CSRA):**

| Budget Line | CSRA Benchmark | Your 2026 Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rent (annual) | Lease rate × 12 | |
| Vacancy allowance | 5–8% of gross rent | |
| **Effective gross income** | Gross − vacancy | |
| Property management fee | 8–10% of effective gross | |
| Landlord insurance | $900–$1,500/year | |
| Property taxes | Actual assessment | |
| HOA dues (if applicable) | Actual | |
| Routine maintenance/repairs | 5–10% of gross rent | |
| CapEx reserve contribution | 2–5% of gross rent | |
| Mortgage (P+I) | Actual | |
| **Net annual cash flow (pre-tax)** | | |

The [Operating Expenses Worksheet](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Operating-Expenses-Worksheet.pdf) has CSRA-specific benchmarks for each line and walks you through the full calculation — it's a free download from McBride PM.

One figure that surprises landlords building their first budget: the total of management, insurance, taxes, maintenance reserve, CapEx, and vacancy typically runs 40–55% of gross rent on a typical CSRA single-family rental. If you underwrote the property assuming a 25–30% expense ratio, you need to revisit that model before you decide what 2027 looks like.

## Setting Your 2027 Portfolio Goals

The year-end review isn't only backward-looking. It's the foundation for decisions about what 2027 looks like for your portfolio.

Three questions worth writing down before year-end:

**Hold, improve, or sell?** For each property: given its 2026 performance, its remaining useful life, and your personal goals, is 2027 a year to hold as-is, invest in improvements to increase rent or value, or position it for a sale? If you're considering selling a rental you've owned for three or more years, the [Section 121 exclusion rules](/blog/section-121-military-exception-fort-gordon-pcs-landlords/) and [depreciation recapture considerations when selling a Georgia rental](/blog/depreciation-recapture-selling-rental-property-georgia/) are worth reviewing with your CPA now, not in February when urgency sets in.

**Acquisition in 2027?** If you're planning to add a property, the [step-by-step guide to buying your first CSRA rental](/blog/first-rental-property-csra-step-by-step-guide/) and the [cash flow analysis for Augusta-area rentals](/blog/csra-rental-property-cash-flow-analysis-augusta-ga/) are good starting references. CSRA cap rates in late 2026 are running in the 6–8% range depending on submarket and condition. The Columbia County submarkets of Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez continue to outperform Augusta proper on vacancy stability and rent trajectory, driven by the ongoing employment growth around Fort Gordon's cyber mission expansion, the opening of Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center, and continued residential development in the Highway 1 corridor.

**Management structure:** If you're currently self-managing and spending significant time on repairs, tenant calls, renewals, and accounting, the year-end review is a natural moment to evaluate whether outsourcing makes sense. The [true cost of self-managing a CSRA rental property](/blog/true-cost-self-managing-rental-property-csra/) breaks down the math. For many owners with one to three properties, the management fee (8–10% of gross rent) is often less than what their own time costs — particularly if any of their time is billable professional time being displaced.

If you're considering hiring a property manager, the [guide to onboarding with a CSRA property manager](/blog/hiring-property-manager-augusta-ga-onboarding-guide/) walks through what the transition looks like in practice.
