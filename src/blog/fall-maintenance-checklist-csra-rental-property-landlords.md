**What does a fall maintenance checklist for a CSRA rental property include?**
A complete fall checklist for Evans, Grovetown, and Augusta rentals covers HVAC service (schedule by mid-September), two rounds of gutter cleaning (mid-October and mid-November), plumbing exposure checks, roof inspection, pest exclusion, and exterior weatherproofing. Under Georgia's Safe at Home Act, landlords must maintain any heating system they provide—making HVAC service a legal obligation, not a suggestion.

</div>

The third week of September is when Augusta's summer heat finally starts to break. Overnight temperatures fall below 70°F for the first time in months, tenants flip the thermostat from "cool" to "heat"—and the calls start coming in.

If you own one to three rental properties in Columbia or Richmond County and you're reading this now, you have a narrow window to get ahead of those calls. HVAC contractors fill their fall schedules from mid-September through October. Roofers get busy after the first hard rain. Plumbers see a surge of frozen-pipe calls in January from the same landlords who skipped the $35 hose-bib insulation job in October.

This checklist organizes fall maintenance into timing windows that match how the CSRA calendar actually works—not generic national advice calibrated for Minnesota winters. The Augusta area gets mild winters by national standards, but the combination of high humidity, occasional hard freezes (Richmond County averages roughly 15 nights per year at or below 32°F), and homes built with minimal pipe insulation creates a specific set of vulnerabilities. This post covers each of them, along with the legal backdrop that makes some of this non-optional.

## Why Georgia's Safe at Home Act Makes Fall HVAC Service Non-Negotiable

Georgia's Safe at Home Act—HB 404, effective July 1, 2024—is directly relevant to how you approach fall maintenance. The law made the warranty of habitability non-waivable and explicitly added cooling *and* heating to the list of utilities landlords must maintain when provided.

The statute is [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-13/). If your rental has a heat pump, gas pack, or furnace, you are legally required to keep it in working condition. A tenant who goes without heat for more than a reasonable repair window has grounds for a habitability complaint, and the [Safe at Home Act bench card](https://gaappleseed.org/wp-content/uploads/SafeAtHome_BenchCard.pdf) published by Georgia Appleseed summarizes the remedies available to tenants—including rent escrow and termination.

The nuance—which [Georgia Public Broadcasting clarified when the law took effect](https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/09/23/do-landlords-have-provide-ac-and-heat-for-renters-heres-what-ga-law-says)—is that landlords aren't required to *install* heating or cooling in a property that never had it. But once the equipment is there, maintaining it is your obligation. A system that fails in January because it hadn't been serviced in three years is a much harder conversation with both a tenant and, potentially, a magistrate court judge.

*This is general guidance from a property manager, not legal or tax advice. Talk to a Georgia attorney for your specific situation.*

The practical takeaway: a fall HVAC tune-up isn't just smart property management—it's how you document good-faith compliance with a statutory duty. Schedule it before mid-September.

## September: The High-Priority Window

These three tasks should be done or firmly scheduled before the end of September.

### 1. HVAC Preventive Maintenance

For a gas pack or heat pump—the two most common unit types in Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez single-family rentals—a fall service call includes:

- Inspect and clean the heat exchanger (gas units) or test the reversing valve and refrigerant charge (heat pumps)
- Test electrical connections, capacitors, and contactors
- Verify thermostat sequence and confirm heating mode operates correctly
- Replace air filter if not on a quarterly tenant rotation
- Clear condensate drain lines
- Inspect ductwork for visible gaps or separated sections

Cost in the Augusta metro: **$125–$200** for a standard tune-up. Emergency repairs in December for the same system run **$300–$600** for the service call alone, plus parts.

If you have a lease clause requiring tenants to change air filters, confirm they're actually doing it. A clogged filter is the single most common cause of HVAC performance degradation and premature failure, and it's entirely preventable. For more on the legal framework around HVAC responsibility, see our [complete guide to HVAC obligations for CSRA landlords](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/hvac-csra-rental-property-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/).

### 2. Roof Visual Inspection

A licensed roofer will spend 20–45 minutes walking your roof looking for:

- Missing, lifted, or cracked shingles (common after Augusta's summer storm season)
- Cracked or separated flashing around chimney bases, pipe boots, and skylight frames
- Debris accumulation in roof valleys
- Dark staining or soft spots that suggest active moisture intrusion

Columbia County properties built in the 1990s and early 2000s—much of the Evans and Martinez rental stock—often have 25-year architectural shingles now approaching or past their designed lifespan. A fall inspection tells you whether you're looking at routine maintenance or an impending capital replacement that should go into your [CapEx planning](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/capex-planning-csra-rental-properties/).

Cost: **$150–$300** for inspection. Catching one cracked flashing detail in October prevents **$2,000–$6,000** in interior water damage repairs over the winter.

### 3. Exterior Door, Window, and Penetration Sealing

Walk the exterior with a flashlight on a bright day. Check weatherstripping on all exterior doors, caulking around window frames, and threshold seals. Focus especially on garage doors (common in Columbia County single-family rentals), French doors, and sliding glass doors.

Air infiltration is both a comfort issue and a maintenance cost driver—gaps accelerate HVAC wear, raise tenant utility bills, and create humidity pathways that can lead to mold formation along exterior walls. The [Georgia rental mold framework under the Safe at Home Act](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/mold-moisture-rental-property-augusta-ga-landlord-guide/) makes this a pre-emptive step worth taking seriously.


## October: After the Heat, Before the Rain

October in the CSRA is the peak shoulder season—temperatures are comfortable, most of the year's heaviest storms have passed, and conditions are ideal for exterior work. These tasks belong in October.

### 4. Gutter Cleaning: First Pass

Columbia County's mix of mature oaks, sweet gums, and hardwoods means gutters fill in two waves: early fall (pine needles and early-drop species) and late fall (main hardwood leaf drop). A mid-October cleaning clears the first wave and lets you confirm gutters are properly sloped and downspouts are discharging at least four feet from the foundation.

Clogged gutters cause ice dam formation at eaves during hard freezes, fascia rot, soffit damage, and water infiltration at the foundation—any of which runs from a few hundred dollars to several thousand in repairs.

Cost: **$100–$175** for a single-story home in Evans or Grovetown. Two-story properties with extensive runs: **$150–$250**.

### 5. Outdoor Plumbing Winterization

This is the step most Augusta-area landlords skip because "it doesn't really freeze here." It does—briefly. And a burst pipe in a property you manage remotely doesn't care that the freeze only lasted 18 hours.

Specific tasks:

- **Disconnect and drain garden hoses from all outdoor bibs.** A connected hose traps water in the fitting, which can freeze and crack the valve inside the wall. This is the most consistently skipped $15 preventive measure in CSRA rental management. The [U.S. Department of Energy's weatherization guidance](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/winter-home-maintenance-tips) flags outdoor faucet draining as a top-priority freeze-prevention step.
- **Install foam hose-bib covers on all outdoor faucets.** Available at any hardware store; five minutes per bib.
- **Locate and document the main water shutoff.** Your tenants should know where it is before an emergency. If your property is managed through McBride PM's [AppFolio owner portal](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/), confirm the shutoff location is noted in the property file.
- **Check exposed pipes in crawl spaces or unheated garage areas.** Wrap any pipe closer than six inches to an exterior wall with foam pipe insulation. Cost: under **$40** for most properties.
- **Test the water heater's temperature and pressure relief valve.** Water heaters in the CSRA typically run 8–12 years before replacement becomes cost-effective. If yours is in that range, note it for [capital expenditure planning](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/capex-planning-csra-rental-properties/).

### 6. Pest Exclusion

Fall is when rodents, cockroaches, and other pests move indoors seeking warmth. Under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13—as reinforced by the Safe at Home Act's habitability standards—pest infestations affecting health and habitability are a [landlord responsibility](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/pest-control-rental-property-georgia-landlord-guide/).

Preventive steps:

- Seal any gap larger than ¼ inch around pipe penetrations, dryer vents, and cable entries using steel wool followed by expanding foam
- Apply door sweep gaskets to all exterior doors with visible threshold gaps
- Clear debris, firewood stacks, and leaf piles from within three feet of the foundation

A preventive pest treatment by a licensed exterminator in October: **$80–$150**. A rodent infestation requiring remediation and exclusion work in February: **$400–$900** or more—and that's before any habitability complaint or tenant request for rent reduction.

## Landlord vs. Tenant Responsibility: Fall Maintenance Edition

One of the most consistent friction points in CSRA rental relationships is a disagreement about who handles what seasonal task. Georgia law and a well-drafted lease resolve most of this before it becomes a dispute.

| Task | Landlord Responsibility | Tenant Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC system service and repair | Yes — always | Replace filters if specified in lease |
| Gutter cleaning and repair | Yes — structural | Report visible clogs or overflow |
| Roof inspection and repair | Yes | Report visible ceiling stains immediately |
| Outdoor hose bib insulation | Yes | Disconnect and store garden hoses |
| Window and door weatherstripping | Yes | Report drafts; do not remove |
| Pest exclusion (structural sealing) | Yes | Keep unit clean; report infestations promptly |
| Water heater maintenance | Yes | Report performance issues |
| Smoke and CO detector batteries | Varies by lease | Often assigned to tenant in writing |
| Furnace filter replacement | Both (lease-dependent) | If explicitly stated in lease |

If your lease doesn't address filter changes, Georgia courts generally treat it as a shared obligation—the landlord provides a functioning system, and routine consumable tasks can be assigned to tenants in writing. ## November: Second Gutter Pass and Year-End Interior Check

By mid-to-late November, the main hardwood leaf drop in Columbia and Richmond County is complete. This is the right time for the second gutter pass and a final exterior walk before December rains arrive.

### Final Gutter Cleaning

The November cleaning removes the bulk of the year's leaf accumulation. A clogged gutter going into December—when Columbia County averages 4–5 inches of rainfall—is a liability waiting to happen. After cleaning, run water from a garden hose through each downspout to confirm free flow. If you spot any fascia boards with soft spots or paint peeling, flag them for repair before spring.

### Interior Walk-Through

With proper 48-hour advance written notice to your tenant (as required by [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-12](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-12/)), walk through the interior and confirm:

- Smoke detectors and CO detectors function (test each one; replace batteries as needed)
- HVAC filter is clean; note if the tenant is behind on their change schedule
- Water heater pilot light is lit (gas units) and temperature is set to 120°F
- No moisture staining visible on ceilings or walls from fall rains
- Attic access hatch is insulated and closed

For owners managing remotely from another duty station or another state, this walk-through is worth combining with the scheduled [annual property inspection](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/annual-property-inspection-csra-rental-landlord-guide/) that produces a dated photo record. ## The Cost Math: Fall Maintenance vs. Reactive Repairs

The ROI case for fall maintenance is clear. Here's a realistic cost comparison for a single-family rental in Evans or Grovetown:

| Preventive Task | Typical Cost | If Skipped: Reactive Cost |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC fall tune-up | $125–$200 | $600–$2,500 (emergency repair + lost heat) |
| Gutter cleaning (x2) | $200–$350 | $800–$4,000 (fascia rot, foundation seepage) |
| Roof inspection | $150–$300 | $2,000–$8,000 (water intrusion from cracked flashing) |
| Hose bib insulation | $30–$50 | $3,000–$8,000 (burst pipe + water damage) |
| Pest exclusion | $80–$150 | $400–$900 (rodent infestation + remediation) |
| **Total preventive** | **$585–$1,050** | **$6,800–$23,400 (expected exposure)** |

A complete fall maintenance cycle costs roughly **$700–$800** for most Evans or Grovetown single-family rentals. The reactive exposure from skipping it isn't hypothetical—every CSRA property management firm, including McBride PM, sees burst pipes, failed HVAC calls, and gutter-related water damage every winter from properties that went into the season without this work done.
