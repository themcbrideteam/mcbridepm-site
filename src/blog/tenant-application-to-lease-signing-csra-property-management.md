Your Evans rental has been on the market for ten days. You're getting inquiries through Zillow, forwarding them to a friend to show when available. Someone finally applied. Now what?

If you're self-managing — or if you handed a property off to a manager and you're not sure what they're supposed to be doing — the stretch from first inquiry to signed lease is both shorter than most landlords expect and more legally sensitive than any other part of the tenancy. The Federal Fair Credit Reporting Act governs what happens when you pull a credit report. Fair Housing law governs how you evaluate what you find. Georgia statutes govern how you hold the deposit once it's collected. And the lease itself needs clauses that most homemade lease forms don't include.

Here's the full pipeline. This is exactly how McBride PM runs the process — step by step, from the first phone call to the security deposit sitting in a Georgia-required trust account.

The national median time to lease a single-family rental home has held in the three-to-four week range, depending on season and market. In the CSRA, it moves faster. A well-priced home in Evans or Grovetown typically receives its first qualified application within seven to 14 days of listing. Augusta proper runs 10 to 21 days depending on the submarket. First, qualified applicants — especially Fort Eisenhower service members working against PCS timelines — will accept another property while you're waiting on paperwork. Second, a process that isn't consistently documented creates Fair Housing exposure. The legal standard isn't whether you treated any single applicant fairly — it's whether your process, applied consistently across all applicants, would hold up to a federal audit.

## Step 1 — Inquiry and Pre-Screening

The process starts before anyone fills out an application. Pre-screening is a quick filter — a one-page questionnaire or a two-minute phone conversation — that confirms an applicant meets the basic parameters before you invest in a showing.

Pre-screening covers: Does the target rent fit their budget? What's the requested move-in date? How many people will occupy the unit? Any pets? What is their current employment status?

This isn't a screening decision. You're not evaluating creditworthiness here, and you're not making any determination that could trigger Fair Housing concerns. You're simply confirming that the basic logistics fit before scheduling a showing. If someone is looking for a 2-bedroom and your property is a 4-bedroom at twice their stated budget, you haven't screened them out for a protected characteristic — you've matched the wrong inventory to the wrong prospect.

Document every pre-screen interaction in your property management system. Under McBride PM's AppFolio workflow, online inquiries flow into a lead-tracking queue, and each one is logged with the date, the contact's name, and the outcome (showing scheduled, redirected to a more appropriate listing, or non-responsive).

## Step 2 — The Showing

For vacant properties, McBride PM uses self-showing lockbox technology through AppFolio — the applicant schedules a one-hour window online, receives a time-limited door code, and completes the showing independently. This removes scheduling bottlenecks without compromising access control, and it creates an automatic timestamped record of who toured the property and when.

For occupied properties or homes with complex access requirements, a McBride PM agent accompanies the showing.

After the showing, every prospective tenant who expressed serious interest receives an application link. Sending the link to everyone who toured — not just those you've pre-approved in your head — is a Fair Housing best practice. It documents that the application opportunity was equally available to everyone who showed genuine interest.

## Step 3 — The Formal Application

The formal application is submitted through AppFolio's secure online portal. For McBride PM rentals, the application collects:

- Full legal name, date of birth, and Social Security Number (for screening purposes only)
- Current and previous addresses for the past three years
- Current employer, supervisor contact, and income documentation (pay stub, offer letter, bank statements for self-employed, or LES for military)
- Rental history references for the past three years, including landlord contact information
- All occupant names and ages
- Pet information (species, breed, weight), if applicable
- Explicit, written authorization for a consumer credit report, criminal background check, and eviction history report under the Federal Fair Credit Reporting Act

That last item — the FCRA consent — must be a separate, explicit authorization. It cannot be buried in the lease or in boilerplate rental terms. The [FTC's guide on using consumer reports for housing decisions](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know) is unambiguous: the consent must be standalone, specific to the purpose, and clearly state what reports will be pulled.

The application fee covers the cost of running the screening reports and is non-refundable once reports have been ordered. Our [published tenant screening standards](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Tenant-Screening-Standards.pdf) are available to any applicant on request before they pay the fee.

## Step 4 — The Screening Report

Once the application is complete and the fee collected, McBride PM orders a comprehensive screening package through AppFolio's integrated screening service:

**Credit report**: A full credit report that shows trade lines, payment history, utilization, derogatory marks, and collections. The overall picture matters more than a single score, but we use a minimum score threshold that is published in our screening criteria and applied consistently to every applicant.

**Criminal background check**: A national multi-jurisdictional criminal history search. McBride PM follows HUD's 2016 guidance on criminal history and does not apply blanket disqualifications based on arrest record alone. Felony convictions involving crimes against persons or property, drug manufacturing, or sex offenses are assessed against the nature of the crime, time elapsed, and evidence of rehabilitation — individually, in writing, for every applicant.

**Eviction history**: A search of eviction filings in jurisdictions where the applicant has resided. A prior judgment for eviction is a significant negative factor; an eviction filing that was dismissed or settled is noted in context.

**Income verification**: We verify that gross household income meets the 3× monthly rent threshold. For a $1,850 home in Evans, that means $5,550/month gross verifiable income. Military applicants can use their LES; self-employed applicants provide two years of tax returns or three months of bank statements. The full criteria breakdown is covered in our [tenant screening criteria guide](/blog/tenant-screening-criteria-income-credit-csra-landlords/).

The full review is documented in AppFolio with a disposition for each criterion. This documentation is what allows you to explain any approval or denial decision months — or years — later if challenged.

## Step 5 — The Approval Decision

This is the step most landlords who switch to professional management ask about first: *do I still decide who lives in my house?*

Yes. The property owner makes the final approval or denial decision. What the property manager does is complete the screening, evaluate the results against published criteria, and present a recommendation. The owner then has 24 to 48 hours to approve, deny, or ask for additional information before the applicant is notified.

Here's exactly how the responsibility divides:

| Decision | Owner | Property Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Approve the applicant | ✓ | Recommends based on criteria |
| Deny based on screening results | ✓ | Delivers screening summary and recommendation |
| Set screening criteria | ✓ (at start of management) | Applies consistently per Fair Housing |
| Issue the adverse action notice | | ✓ (required; see Step 6) |
| Negotiate lease terms within policy | ✓ | Facilitates |
| Waive pet deposit for a specific applicant | With PM guidance | Documents the exception |
| Apply different criteria to a specific applicant mid-cycle | No — creates Fair Housing liability | Flags and escalates |

That final row matters. Once you have published screening criteria and an active applicant pool, you cannot apply different standards to different applicants in the same cycle. You can update your criteria between tenancy cycles, before the property goes back on the market. But during an active applicant pool, the criteria are fixed for that cycle. If you want to waive the income requirement for someone you know personally, that's the kind of exception that creates Fair Housing exposure — both for the waived applicant and for every applicant you held to the full standard.

## Step 6 — When an Application Is Denied

When a denial is based in whole or in part on information from a consumer reporting agency — the credit report, background check, or eviction history — the FCRA requires the landlord to send an adverse action notice.

The notice must include:

- The name, address, and phone number of the consumer reporting agency that supplied the report
- A statement that the reporting agency didn't make the decision and can't explain why the application was denied
- Notice of the applicant's right to a free copy of the report from the agency within 60 days of the notice
- Notice of the applicant's right to dispute inaccurate or incomplete information with the agency

This isn't optional, and there's no size exemption for individual landlords. The [CFPB and HUD jointly encourage landlords to send written adverse action notices](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/federal-housing-agencies-strongly-encourage-landlords-to-provide-tenants-written-notice-of-their-rights/) even when a denial is based on non-consumer-report factors. As a matter of practice, McBride PM sends a written notice for every denial, regardless of basis. The cost is minimal; the exposure for not sending one isn't.

Self-managing landlords who deny applications without sending the FCRA notice — often because they simply don't know the requirement exists — are creating liability on every denial. The applicant doesn't need to understand their rights to file a complaint; the CFPB can initiate a review based on a referral.

## Step 7 — Lease Preparation and Signing

On owner approval, the lease goes into preparation. McBride PM builds leases in AppFolio using the Georgia Association of REALTORS® residential lease as the base document, with the following required modifications:

**Landlord identity disclosure** (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-3): The lease must identify the owner or the owner's authorized agent by name and address for service of process. If McBride PM is the authorized agent, both the owner's identity and McBride PM's contact information appear in the lease. This is a statutory requirement, not a boilerplate preference.

**Safe at Home Act warranty** (Georgia HB 404, effective July 1, 2024): For any lease signed or renewed on or after July 1, 2024, Georgia law implies a non-waivable warranty of habitability. The lease cannot purport to waive this warranty — any such clause is void. McBride PM's lease form includes an explicit Safe at Home Act acknowledgment and links to the statutory maintenance request procedures.

**SCRA military clause**: Any property that might rent to a service member includes the standard SCRA lease-termination clause, which allows early termination with 30 days' written notice if the servicemember receives PCS orders or is deployed overseas for more than 90 days. This applies to Fort Eisenhower-area rentals in particular. The [SCRA lease-termination guide](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/) covers what the proper exercise of this clause looks like and how to document it.

**Pet addendum**: Where pets are approved, a separate addendum specifies the approved animals by name and description, the pet deposit amount, any monthly pet fee, and the tenant's responsibility for pet-related damage beyond normal wear and tear.

The completed lease package is sent through AppFolio's e-signature system. All required parties — including co-applicants and all adult occupants — receive the execution package. Standard execution time is 24 to 48 hours. The tenancy does not begin until all parties have executed and the security deposit has been received in cleared funds.

## Step 8 — Security Deposit and Move-In Funds

Before the tenant takes possession, McBride PM collects:

**Security deposit**: Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-30.1) caps the security deposit at two months' rent for unfurnished residential properties and three months' rent for furnished properties. The specific amount is set at the property level and disclosed in the listing. Under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-30, the deposit must be held in a trust account separate from the property manager's operating funds, and the tenant must be informed in writing of where the deposit is held within 30 days of receipt.

**First month's rent** (if not prepaid at application): If the lease start date isn't the first of the month, prorated rent for the remaining days of the first calendar month is collected at this time along with the full second month's rent.

**Pet deposit and/or monthly pet fee**: If applicable per the pet addendum.

All funds are collected via AppFolio's secure payment portal before possession is granted. No keys transfer until all required funds have cleared. After funds clear, McBride PM schedules the move-in inspection — a separate process covered in detail in our [move-in inspection documentation guide](/blog/move-in-move-out-inspection-rental-property-georgia-landlords/).
