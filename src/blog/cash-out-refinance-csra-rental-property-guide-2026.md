## Rate-and-Term vs. Cash-Out: Know Which Refinance You're Doing

These are fundamentally different transactions, and lenders treat them differently.

A **rate-and-term refinance** replaces your existing mortgage with a new one at a different rate or term length. No cash comes out at closing beyond minor cost adjustments. Because the risk profile is lower, the LTV limit is slightly more generous — typically 80% on a single-unit rental — and pricing is better. If your current rate is materially above market, this is the cleaner move.

A **cash-out refinance** replaces the existing loan with a larger one and puts the difference in your pocket. The new loan is bigger, your monthly payment usually rises (even if the rate drops), and lenders impose stricter limits:

| Feature | Rate-and-Term Refi | Cash-Out Refi |
|---|---|---|
| Max LTV, 1-unit rental | 80% | 75% |
| Max LTV, 2–4 unit | 75% | 70% |
| Rate vs. primary home | +0.25–0.50% | +0.50–0.75% |
| Seasoning on existing note | None required | 12 months (Fannie Mae) |
| Minimum PITIA reserves | 2–4 months | 6 months |
| Use of proceeds | No restriction (n/a) | No restriction |

The 12-month seasoning requirement on the existing note — meaning the note date, not your ownership date — is the most common surprise. If you bought the home with a purchase mortgage less than a year ago, you can't do a conventional cash-out yet. If you bought it with cash, the delayed-financing exception may apply, but it requires specific documentation and lender approval.

For a full breakdown of purchase financing options before you get to the refinance stage, see our guide to [conventional, DSCR, and portfolio loans for CSRA rental investors](/blog/rental-property-financing-csra-conventional-dscr-portfolio-loans/).

## What Lenders Actually Require for an Investment Property Cash-Out Refi

Conventional (Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac) is the default path for most owner-occupants. For investment properties, the rules are stricter across the board.

**LTV limits.** Fannie Mae's [Selling Guide (B2-1.3-03)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.3-03/cash-out-refinance-transactions) allows a maximum of 75% LTV on a single-unit investment property and 70% on two- to four-unit properties for a cash-out transaction. That 75% limit means if your home appraises at $280,000, the maximum new loan is $210,000. If you owe $175,000 today, the most you can pull out before costs is $35,000.

**Credit score.** The minimum for an investment-property conventional loan is typically 680. Below that, you lose access to conventional cash-out entirely and would need to explore non-QM options.

**Reserves after closing.** The [Fannie Mae Selling Guide (B3-4.1-01)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.1-01/minimum-reserve-requirements) requires at least six months of PITIA — principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues — held in liquid assets after the loan closes. On a $210,000 loan at 7.00%, that's roughly $1,400 a month × 6 = $8,400 that must remain in your account after all closing costs are paid. If you own multiple financed properties, supplemental reserves stack.

**Rate premium.** Investment property pricing runs about 0.50% to 0.75% above a comparable primary-residence loan. Georgia 30-year fixed rates were running roughly 6.50% to 7.00% in mid-2026 (per Bankrate's Georgia rate data); investment-property pricing sits at the higher end of that band or above it. A 7.00% investment-property cash-out versus a 6.50% primary-home refi represents real carrying cost on a $200,000+ loan.

**FHA and VA are not options here.** Both programs are limited to owner-occupied properties. If you're a Fort Gordon service member refinancing a home you no longer occupy, the VA occupancy requirement applies — your property management team can confirm what's on file.

## DSCR Refinance: The No-Tax-Return Option

</figure>

If your personal income picture is complicated — a 1099 year with heavy deductions, a year of depreciation writeoffs, or simply three rentals generating good cashflow that your W-2 doesn't reflect — a DSCR refinance is worth comparing against the conventional path.

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loans are non-QM products originated by portfolio lenders and private capital sources. The qualification metric is simple: does the property's gross rent divided by the proposed monthly PITIA meet the lender's minimum ratio?

**DSCR formula:**
```
DSCR = Monthly Gross Rent ÷ Monthly PITIA
```

A ratio of 1.00 means rent exactly covers debt service. Most DSCR lenders set a minimum ratio of 1.00; some price more favorably at 1.10 or 1.25. A handful will originate below 1.00 at a premium rate — but that path is expensive and means the property's rent does not cover its debt payments by definition.

| DSCR Level | Typical Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1.25+ | Best pricing tier; most lenders approve |
| 1.10–1.24 | Standard approval; some rate premium |
| 1.00–1.09 | Approved but higher rate and/or LTV reduction |
| Below 1.00 | Lender-specific; often requires compensating factors |

For a cash-out DSCR refinance, most lenders cap at 75% LTV on a single-unit — same as conventional — and require 640 to 680 credit. Rates were running roughly 6.875% to 8.75% for DSCR products in mid-2026, depending on the ratio, credit, and lender, per [The Mortgage Reports' DSCR rate data](https://themortgagereports.com/136050/dscr-loan-rates-requirements).

The trade-off: DSCR loans typically carry slightly higher rates than conventional, but they close faster and don't require two years of tax returns showing rental income. For a P3 investor with two or three rentals generating solid cashflow but significant depreciation deductions, the DSCR path may be the cleaner underwrite. For more on DSCR loan mechanics before the refinance step, see our [DSCR loan guide for CSRA investors](/blog/dscr-loans-csra-rental-investors-augusta-ga/).

## The Cash-Out Math for a Typical CSRA Rental

Let's run a realistic example for a Columbia County single-family rental.

**Property facts:**
- Purchase price: $265,000 (2021)
- Current appraised value: $310,000 (2026)
- Current mortgage balance: $218,000 at 3.50% (30-year fixed)
- Current monthly payment (P&I): ~$979

**Cash-out scenario at 75% LTV:**
- Maximum new loan: $310,000 × 0.75 = $232,500
- Less current payoff: $218,000
- Gross equity available: $14,500
- Less closing costs (est. 2.5% of new loan): ~$5,800
- **Net cash at close: ~$8,700**

That's a small number — and importantly, the new rate of approximately 7.25% on the $232,500 loan produces a monthly P&I of roughly $1,588, up $609 from today's payment. The interest cost on $8,700 of extracted equity at that premium is steep.

Now run it again with more equity:

**Scenario 2 — higher equity, same LTV cap:**
- Current mortgage balance: $148,000
- Maximum new loan at 75% LTV: $232,500
- Less current payoff: $148,000
- Gross equity available: $84,500
- Less closing costs: ~$5,800
- **Net cash at close: ~$78,700**

If you deploy that $78,700 as a 25% down payment on a $315,000 Evans rental, you've used one property's equity to acquire a second. As of November 23, 2026, the three-bedroom homes we have listed in Evans are asking $1,850 to $2,400 a month — the rent on that second property could well cover the incremental debt service on both loans if you buy correctly.

## The Seasoning Rule That Trips Up CSRA Investors

The 12-month seasoning requirement in Fannie Mae's Selling Guide applies to the **existing note date** — the original date of the loan being paid off — not how long you've owned the property.

This catches investors in two specific scenarios:

1. **You refinanced recently.** If you did a rate-and-term refinance 10 months ago to lower your rate, and now want to cash out, you're still in the seasoning window on the new note. The clock restarted at that refinance closing.

2. **BRRRR strategy.** The classic BRRRR sequence is: buy distressed → rehab → refinance → rent → repeat. If you purchased with an acquisition mortgage, began the rehab, and want to do a cash-out refinance in month nine after achieving market value, you can't — the note is still under 12 months old. You'd need to wait until month 12. See how [the BRRRR strategy works in the CSRA](/blog/brrrr-strategy-csra-augusta-ga-rental-investor-guide/) before mapping your exit timing.

**The delayed-financing exception** is the workaround for cash buyers. If you bought the CSRA property with all cash — no mortgage at close — you can refinance within the first six months under Fannie Mae's delayed-financing guidelines, without waiting for the standard seasoning period. Requirements include documentation that the original purchase was truly cash, clean title with no liens, and that the new loan does not exceed the documented acquisition cost plus eligible closing costs and capital improvements. It's not a loophole — it's a specific underwriting path, and not every lender offers it.

## When a Cash-Out Refi Makes Sense — and When It Doesn't

There's a rough framework for evaluating this decision. The question isn't just "can I do it" — it's "should I, given what I'll pay for the capital and what I'll do with it."

**Strong case for cash-out refinance:**

- **Large equity spread with a clear deployment.** You have $80,000+ to pull out, and a specific second-rental acquisition or major capital improvement in mind that will generate measurable return.
- **Your existing rate is at or above today's market.** If you bought in mid-2023 at 7.5% and today's investment-property rate is 7.0%, the refinance lowers your existing rate AND allows you to access equity — the incremental cost of the equity is effectively only the gap in LTV, not a full rate premium.
- **DSCR qualifies you for a cash-out at a competitive rate.** Your property's rent-to-PITIA ratio is 1.15 or better, allowing a DSCR product to close cleanly without digging through three years of Schedule E income.

**Weak case for cash-out refinance:**

- **Your existing rate is well below today's market.** If you locked a 3.5% rate in 2021 and cash-out rates today are 7.25%, you're paying an enormous ongoing premium for whatever capital you extract. The effective cost of that equity is very high.
- **You're close to loan payoff.** The interest-to-principal ratio in later loan years is favorable; refinancing resets the amortization and extends your time to payoff.
- **The extracted capital has no specific deployment.** Pulling equity and parking it in a money market at 4–5% while paying 7%+ on the new loan is a losing spread.
- **Closing costs eat the purpose.** At 2–3% of the new loan, closing a $230,000 refinance costs $5,750 to $6,900. If you're extracting $20,000 and paying $6,000 in costs, you've netted $14,000 at a very high all-in rate. Run the numbers in the [cash flow analysis for CSRA rentals](/blog/csra-rental-property-cash-flow-analysis-augusta-ga/) before committing.

A useful checklist before proceeding:

- [ ] Does my current note date clear the 12-month seasoning window?
- [ ] Does 75% LTV produce meaningful extractable equity after payoff and costs?
- [ ] Can I document 6 months of PITIA reserves after closing on liquid assets?
- [ ] Is my credit score 680 or above, or have I modeled the DSCR path?
- [ ] Is the destination for the equity identified and analyzed to generate returns above my all-in cost of capital?
- [ ] Have I spoken with a CPA about the deductibility of interest on the cash-out portion?

The mortgage interest deduction on the refinanced portion depends on how the proceeds are used — the [IRS Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p936.pdf) rules apply, and a CPA familiar with rental real estate will have the definitive answer for your situation.

The Federal Reserve's rate path heading into 2027 matters for two reasons. First, the investment-property rate premium over primary-home pricing could narrow or widen depending on secondary-market appetite for rental loans. Second, if rates ease, the case for cash-out refinancing improves for the large cohort of CSRA investors who locked purchase mortgages in 2022–2023 at 6.5–7.5% and are currently sitting on the edge of a worthwhile refinance.

The CSRA's underlying rental demand story hasn't changed: Fort Gordon's expanding cyber mission, Savannah River Site contract activity, and the ongoing residential growth in Columbia County continue to generate consistent tenant demand.
