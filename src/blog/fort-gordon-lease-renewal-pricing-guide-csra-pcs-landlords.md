## Why the November–December Window Is Your Critical Planning Period

Lease renewal decisions for January and February expirations should be made no later than mid-December. Here is why timing matters so acutely for military landlords right now:

**The BAH 2027 announcement is coming.** The Department of Defense has followed a consistent pattern: the 2026 rates were released December 11, 2025; the 2025 rates were released December 13, 2024. Rates for the following year typically appear in the second or third week of December. Once your Fort Gordon tenant sees the new BAH number, their housing math changes. If you've already locked in a signed renewal before that date, you've removed the variable. If you haven't, your tenant may reconsider.

**Military lease expirations cluster in winter.** Fort Gordon assignment cycles mean many military tenants PCS between November and March. November–December is when active-duty service members receive or confirm their next-assignment orders. If your tenant is leaving, you need that information now, not in January, to give yourself 60 days of marketing runway. A lease that expires February 1 with no renewal signed by December 1 is a lease that may start leaking vacancy costs.

**Winter is a thinner leasing market in the CSRA.** While Fort Gordon's BAH demand provides year-round floor support, January and February are genuinely slower for new tenant move-ins than March through July. A property that sits empty in January costs more in lost rent than a modest rate concession to keep a good tenant in place.

## BAH 2027: What to Expect and What to Do Before the Announcement

As of November 2026, DoD has not published the 2027 BAH rates for the Fort Gordon area. Based on the [FY 2027 defense budget request analyzed by the Military Officers Association of America (MOAA)](https://www.moaa.org/content/publications-and-media/news-articles/2026-news-articles/advocacy/rebuilding-the-housing-benefit-moaas-work-toward-100-bah-continues/), the administration requested a 6.2% BAH increase for FY 2027. That figure is a congressional request, not a final DoD rate — the actual rate depends on the annual local rental survey DoD conducts, and it varies by Military Housing Area.

For planning purposes, consider a range: if BAH 2027 at Fort Gordon comes in between 4% and 6% higher than 2026, the E-5 with-dependents rate would move from roughly $1,890 to approximately $1,966–$2,003 per month.

**What this means for your renewal decision:**

If you're planning to raise rent at renewal, you have two sensible approaches:

1. **Lock the renewal before mid-December** at a price you're confident in based on current market data. You avoid the uncertainty of the announcement, and your tenant signs before they can benchmark against the new BAH.
2. **Build a one-time adjustment clause** that ties the first month of a new lease term to a fixed date — but verify any such clause with your Georgia-licensed attorney before including it. Georgia law does not automatically permit mid-lease rent changes without tenant consent.

The worst position: waiting until January, when the BAH number is out, your tenant has had three weeks to look at the market, and you're negotiating on their timeline instead of yours.

## Renewal Decision Framework: Five Questions Before You Set the Price

Before your property manager sends the renewal offer, work through these five questions:

1. **Has the tenant indicated PCS intentions?** A Fort Gordon tenant receiving orders must notify you under SCRA, but that notification may come at any time — including after you've made renewal plans. Ask your property manager to inquire directly at the 90-day mark.

2. **What is the current market rent for a comparable property?** Don't guess. As of November 25, 2026, the homes McBride Property Management has listed in Evans are asking $1,850 to $2,400 for three-bedroom homes and $2,295 to $2,600 for four-bedroom homes. In Augusta, three-bedroom homes are listed at $1,295 to $2,050. These are the homes competing for your next tenant if this tenant doesn't renew.

3. **What is the cost of one month of vacancy?** If your property rents for $1,900 per month, one vacant month costs you $1,900 plus make-ready expenses (typically $500–$1,500 for a mid-tenancy turnover). A 5% renewal increase adds $95 per month. The break-even on that increase requires your tenant staying roughly 15–20 months after the renewal. A good long-term tenant often warrants a below-market renewal to protect against that vacancy math.

4. **Is the tenant using BAH?** Most Fort Gordon military tenants use their full BAH toward housing. This means their housing budget is pegged to a government formula, not to their personal income. A rent increase within BAH is effectively invisible to them financially. A rent increase above BAH requires them to cover the difference from base pay — and many will start looking elsewhere.

5. **What lease term serves you?** A 12-month renewal locks in stability. A 6-month renewal gives you flexibility if you plan to sell or move back in — but it creates another renewal decision in mid-2027 and may deter a tenant who doesn't want to move again in six months.

## How to Structure the Renewal Offer

A well-structured renewal offer for a Fort Gordon military tenant includes:

**Renewal checklist for CSRA PCS landlord properties:**
- [ ] Confirm current lease expiration date in writing
- [ ] Request tenant's PCS status confirmation (orders, likelihood of orders) through the property manager
- [ ] Pull current comparable rents for your property type and city
- [ ] Check your property's current condition report (from last inspection) for any items that need addressing before renewal
- [ ] Set the renewal rent based on market comparables and BAH tier
- [ ] Decide on lease term (12-month preferred for stability; 6-month only if you have specific plans)
- [ ] Send the written offer with a response deadline of 14–21 days
- [ ] Confirm whether you need to disclose any coming changes (management, ownership, insurance)
- [ ] Once signed, update your property manager's records with the new term and rate

The written offer should include the new monthly rent, the new lease term, the response deadline, and a brief note about what has not changed (management, payment procedures, maintenance process). Military tenants appreciate operational clarity — they deal in orders, timelines, and confirmation of continuity.

## If Your Military Tenant Is PCSing Out

Under the [Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. § 3955)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title50-section3955&num=0&edition=prelim), a service member who receives qualifying military orders can terminate a lease by providing written notice and a copy of the orders. The termination is effective 30 days after the next rent payment due date following that notice.

This means a tenant who provides SCRA notice and a copy of orders on December 1 would owe rent through the end of January (30 days after the December payment due date). Your effective vacancy start would be February 1.

What you need to know:
- **SCRA protection applies to renewed leases.** A tenant who signs a renewal and then receives PCS orders still has SCRA protection. The renewal does not waive it.
- **You cannot charge an early-termination fee for a valid SCRA termination.** Any lease clause that penalizes proper SCRA notice is void.
- **BAH continues until the service member departs.** A departing tenant using BAH has the housing budget until they PCS out, which means rent payments typically remain stable through the SCRA notice period.
- **Document everything.** When you receive SCRA notice and orders, verify the orders through the [Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) SCRA verification tool](https://scra.dmdc.osd.mil/) before making any re-leasing decisions based on them.

For a full review of SCRA notice procedures, see our [guide to SCRA lease termination for Fort Gordon landlords](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/).

If your tenant is PCSing, the December–January period is when you should activate your re-leasing marketing. Fort Gordon generates a consistent supply of incoming service members year-round, but the spring PCS surge (March–June) is when you'll find the deepest qualified applicant pool. A property available February 15 that hasn't been listed until February 1 misses four weeks of marketing. A property listed in January 15 is in front of service members confirming orders right now.

## A Note on Month-to-Month Risk

If you don't send a renewal offer and the lease expires without a signed renewal, most Georgia leases convert to a month-to-month tenancy by default. Under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-7, a month-to-month tenancy requires 30 days' written notice from either party to terminate.

Month-to-month status is not ideal for a PCS landlord who is out of state. For the full rules on Georgia month-to-month tenancies, see our [Georgia month-to-month lease guide](/blog/georgia-month-to-month-lease-csra-landlords/).
- Your tenant can leave on 30 days' notice — no advance planning window for you
- You cannot increase rent without the tenant's consent or by giving proper notice for the next rental period
- Tenants in month-to-month status are more likely to begin looking at other options when personal circumstances shift

A signed 12-month renewal locks in your income, your tenant quality, and your peace of mind through 2027. That is worth the effort of sending the offer now.
