Your Evans or Grovetown rental went live on Zillow three days ago. You've gotten eight inquiries. One person no-showed the scheduled tour. Two asked questions that made you uncomfortable. The two who actually walked through said they'd think about it — and then went quiet.

This is what a leasing funnel looks like when it isn't running well. A vacant property costs you real money — at the CSRA's average rent of roughly $1,200–$1,400 per month for a single-family home, every week of vacancy is $300–$350 gone. And poor showing execution is one of the most common, most fixable reasons a home sits vacant longer than it should.

The good news: showings aren't complicated when you have a consistent system. The bad news: most self-managing owners treat them as one-off events rather than a repeatable process. This guide gives you that system — from pre-showing prep through lease execution — along with the Georgia-specific legal rules that protect you along the way.

## Get the property ready before the first prospect walks in

The showing decision is usually made before the prospect reaches the front door. First impressions form in roughly 30 seconds, and the most common feedback from prospects who decline to apply is some version of "it just didn't feel right" — which almost always points to something visibly off on arrival.

**Your pre-showing prep checklist:**

- [ ] Exterior: gutters clean, no debris on walkway or driveway, lawn trimmed, entry light functioning
- [ ] Interior: scuffed walls touched up, no odors (pet, smoke, or mildew), all light bulbs working
- [ ] Mechanical: HVAC filter changed, water heater operating, any in-progress repair with a clear completion date
- [ ] Utilities: power and water on (essential for demonstrating HVAC and plumbing)
- [ ] Clean to a hotel standard — neutral, lightly scented or unscented throughout
- [ ] Vacant homes: interior temperature no higher than 78°F; a stuffy, overheated vacant home in October is a conversion problem, even in the CSRA's mild fall weather
- [ ] Confirm access method works before scheduling the first tour

This checklist applies whether the unit is fully vacant or you're doing a transition with a current tenant still in place. A home that reads "move-in ready" attracts applicants who respect the property — and [McBride Property Management's Pre-Rental Property Prep Checklist](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Pre-Rental-Property-Prep-Checklist.pdf) walks through the full scope. Even a small deferred-maintenance item — a dripping faucet, a broken door latch — signals to a careful prospect that the ownership is inattentive.

## Self-show, owner-guided, or PM-guided: comparing your three options

The right showing method depends on your volume, bandwidth, and risk tolerance. All three work; all three have real trade-offs.

| | Self-Showing (Lockbox) | Owner-Guided | PM-Guided |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Vacant units, high inquiry volume, out-of-state owners | Low volume, high-value units, owner preference for relationship leasing | Full portfolios, Fair Housing risk mitigation, ongoing management |
| **Cost** | $0–$30/month for smart lockbox; ~$300 installed for a smart lock | Your time | Included in leasing fee (typically 50–100% of one month's rent) |
| **Speed** | Fastest — prospects tour on their own schedule | Limited by your availability | Managed on business-day schedule with PM staff |
| **Fair Housing risk** | Low (no in-person verbal exchange) | Moderate — in-person scripts must be consistent | Low (trained staff follows written protocol) |
| **Security** | Requires ID verification + time-limited codes | Direct control of every entry | PM verifies all prospects before access |

**On self-showings:** for a vacant Columbia County single-family home generating more than a few inquiries per week, a smart lockbox pays for itself in recovered vacancy days. The key discipline is never releasing a code without verifying identity — at minimum, collect a valid photo ID before issuing access. Systems like Rently require a government ID upload and geo-confirm the prospect is physically at the property before showing the code. A mechanical combination lockbox works for lower-traffic situations but lacks the entry log you'd want if anything goes missing during a self-show. [Buildium's guide to self-showings](https://www.buildium.com/blog/self-showings-for-rental-properties/) covers the practical security trade-offs in detail.

**On owner-guided showings:** you're the property's best advocate, and a short conversation can help you make a better leasing decision than a credit report alone. But your personal availability caps your showing capacity, and in-person scripts must be airtight from a Fair Housing standpoint — more on that below.

## Building the funnel: from online inquiry to scheduled showing

The showing starts with the first message or call, not the doorbell. Most self-managing owners treat every inquiry as a warm lead and schedule immediately. That fills the calendar with unqualified prospects who no-show or decline after the tour — wasting your time and, if the unit is occupied, your tenant's patience.

**Pre-screen before you schedule.** A two-minute intake call or a short intake form eliminates tire-kickers without any Fair Housing risk, provided your questions stick to objective criteria. The questions you can legally ask before scheduling a showing:

- What's your target move-in date?
- How many people will be living in the home?
- Do you have pets? (Confirm your pet policy immediately)
- What's your approximate monthly gross income? (You're looking for roughly 3× rent as a general qualifying floor)
- Have you reviewed the monthly rent and security deposit amount?
- Is smoking permitted in the home a concern? (State your no-smoking policy)

Questions you cannot ask — before, during, or after a showing — include anything touching race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, or familial status under [the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. § 3604)](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_overview). "Is it just you?" reads as familial status probing. "Where are you from originally?" is national origin territory. Stick to income, move-in date, pets, lease term, and deposit logistics.

Once pre-qualified, schedule with a digital link (Calendly, AppFolio ShowingSync, or a simple text) and send a confirmation with the address, showing time, and what to bring: valid photo ID. Confirm again the day before. A reminder confirmation cuts no-show rates significantly in a market where prospects are often touring three or four properties in the same weekend.

Our [fair housing compliance guide for CSRA landlords](/blog/fair-housing-compliance-csra-landlords-2026/) covers what to do when a prospect raises a concern that touches a protected class — worth reading before you take your first inquiry call on a new vacancy.

## Entry rules during showings in Georgia — occupied vs. vacant

For a **vacant property**, there is no tenant and therefore no entry rules to navigate. You own the home; you can schedule showings at will. The only constraints are any HOA rules about signage or visitor traffic on the property — and if your property is in an Evans or Grovetown HOA, a quick read of the governing documents before you list saves a fine later.

For an **occupied property**, Georgia's framework is different from most states. Georgia has no statute that establishes a required advance-notice period for landlord entry. As the [Georgia DCA Landlord-Tenant Handbook](https://dca.ga.gov/sites/default/files/landlord_tenant_handbook.pdf) and O.C.G.A. § 44-7-1 make clear, the tenant's right to quiet enjoyment of the premises is protected, and the lease governs what entry looks like during the tenancy.

Most well-drafted Georgia leases require 24 hours' written or electronic notice before entry for non-emergency purposes. Some require 48 hours. If your lease is silent on entry notice, you're in murky territory: O.C.G.A. § 44-7-1 protects the tenant's quiet enjoyment, and repeated entries without asking can be treated as a breach of that right.

**Best practice for occupied showings:**

1. Give written notice (text or email qualifies) at least 24 hours before the showing — even if your lease doesn't strictly require it
2. Schedule between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m.; contact outside those hours strains the relationship
3. Tell the tenant who is coming and why (prospective tenant tours)
4. If the tenant offers an alternative time slot, accommodate it; don't force access to override a reasonable objection
5. Don't book back-to-back tours through an occupied home without the tenant's agreement — it can feel like a stream of strangers and will poison the transition

A tenant who feels respected during the notice period is far more likely to leave the unit in good condition, cooperate with move-out timing, and serve as a de facto advocate for the property when showing it in place.

## Fair Housing compliance during showings

This is the section most self-managing owners skip — and the one that generates the most legal exposure.

Fair Housing rules apply at every stage of the leasing process: the listing language, the inquiry intake, the showing, the application review, and the approval or denial decision. During an in-person showing, exposure is highest because verbal exchanges are unscripted and hard to document.

**The consistent-treatment rule.** Every prospect who meets your minimum financial pre-screening criteria must receive the same tour, the same information, the same follow-up timeline, and the same application link. Any deviation based on the prospect's appearance, accent, perceived national origin, family composition, or disability creates a Fair Housing complaint pathway. Georgia's Commission on Equal Opportunity updated its enforcement rule in February 2026 under Georgia Rule 186-2-.03, and HUD's April 2024 guidance on screening emphasizes that housing providers are responsible for the screening practices used in their name — including any default criteria that produce disparate impact outcomes.

**What you can and cannot say during a showing:**

| You CAN say... | You CANNOT say... |
|---|---|
| "The lease term is 12 months with a possible renewal." | "Families with young kids sometimes struggle with the HOA noise rules." |
| "The HOA doesn't allow commercial vehicles in the driveway." | "The neighborhood attracts a lot of [any demographic description]." |
| "We require renters insurance with at least $100,000 in liability coverage." | "Are you planning on having anyone else move in later?" |
| "Monthly rent is $X. Security deposit is equal to one month's rent." | "We've had issues with certain types of tenants before." |

The practical solution is a **showing script** — a short set of talking points used consistently for every tour. Our team uses standardized showing scripts when working with prospective residents; it keeps every showing conversation fully documented and defensible. Our [publicly available Tenant Screening Standards (PDF)](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Tenant-Screening-Standards.pdf) outlines the criteria applied in every application review, so owners and applicants alike know what to expect.

## The applicant-flow timeline: showing to signed lease in the CSRA

Speed is one of the most underrated leasing tools in a market where serious, qualified applicants in Evans and Grovetown are often touring multiple properties in a single weekend. The properties that lease fastest aren't always the cheapest or the best condition — they're the ones whose owners make decisions quickly.

**Target timeline — showing to signed lease:**

1. **Day 0 (Showing day):** Tour completed. Application link sent to the prospect immediately — same-day follow-up while interest is at its peak.

2. **Day 1 (Application day):** Application received. Initiate credit check, criminal background, rental history verification, and income confirmation the same day. Do not let an application sit overnight without initiating screening.

3. **Days 1–2 (Review day):** Compare results against your written qualifying criteria — minimum credit score, income multiple, rental history standards. See our [tenant screening guide](/blog/tenant-screening-process-augusta-ga-landlords/) for what criteria to document and how to apply them consistently.

4. **Day 2 (Decision day):** Approved: send an offer letter with deposit instructions. Denied: issue an adverse action notice as required by the [Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 1681 et seq.)](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/fair-credit-reporting-act) if a consumer report factored in the decision.

5. **Day 2–3 (Lease execution):** Send the lease for electronic signature. E-signatures are valid under O.C.G.A. § 10-12-1 (Georgia Electronic Records and Signatures Act). Platforms like AppFolio manage this workflow automatically.

6. **Day 3–5 (Move-in):** Security deposit and first month's rent collected digitally before move-in. Keys released only after funds clear and all signatures are complete.

This is a 3–5 business day window from showing to executed lease. Most self-managing owners take 7–14 days — not because the steps are slower but because decisions aren't being made daily. Setting a decision date upfront ("we'll have a decision by Wednesday") and communicating it to the applicant keeps serious prospects engaged instead of accepting the next property on their list.

## When showings aren't converting: diagnosing the problem

If you're getting 10 or more showings and no applications, one of four things is wrong — and almost none of them are fixed by running more advertising.

**1. Price.** The most common root cause. If you're above the current market rate for your submarket and condition level, prospects are walking through and immediately applying elsewhere at a better value. Check the [current lease-renewal pricing benchmarks for Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez](/blog/lease-renewal-pricing-csra-rental-property-owners/) — Q3/Q4 2026 in the CSRA has seen modest softening from the 2025 peak, and pricing that was right in June may be $75/month high today.

**2. Condition.** Something the photos didn't capture — a worn carpet, a dated kitchen, a smaller-than-expected bedroom — is disqualifying the home after the tour. If you're not getting feedback, ask directly after every showing: "What would need to change for this to feel like a fit?" Even three or four answers will identify a pattern.

**3. Competition.** Your Columbia County neighbors have the same Columbia County School District address and the same Grovetown zip code. When supply tightens in a specific submarket, how your listing compares on photography, amenities, and price matters more than usual. The [marketing guide for CSRA rentals](/blog/marketing-csra-rental-property-lease-fast-augusta-ga/) covers how to make your listing the most compelling one on any given weekend.

**4. Process friction.** A paper application, a delayed decision, or a confusing deposit requirement will cost you your best applicants — specifically the ones who have options and won't wait around. The [online application guide for CSRA properties](/blog/online-rental-applications-property-management-csra-guide/) shows how a streamlined digital process cuts days off your vacancy window without adding work.

If you've run more than 10 showings and haven't received a single acceptable application, it's almost certainly price or condition — not a showing process problem. Cutting the rent by $50–$75 or addressing a specific condition item will outperform any showing technique.
