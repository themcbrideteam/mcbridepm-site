**What counts as a maintenance emergency at a CSRA rental property?**
Emergency maintenance is any condition that threatens the health, safety, or legal habitability of the home and cannot wait until the next business day. Under Georgia's [Safe at Home Act](https://gaappleseed.org/safe-at-home-act/) (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13(b)), landlords must keep heating, cooling, plumbing, and electrical systems functional — meaning any total failure of those systems is a legal emergency, not just an inconvenience.

## Georgia's Legal Floor: What the Safe at Home Act Requires

Georgia's habitability standard got clearer in 2024. The Safe at Home Act (HB 404, signed April 2024, effective July 1, 2024) added an express warranty of habitability to O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13(b) — prior to that law, [Georgia was the only state in the country without a statutory habitability warrant](https://gaappleseed.org/safe-at-home-act/). The law also expanded the statutory definition of "utilities" under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-14.1(a) to explicitly include cooling alongside heat, light, and water.

What that means practically: if a rental includes air conditioning and it fails during a Georgia summer, the landlord's repair obligation is now written into statute — not just implied by case law. As [GPB News reported in 2024](https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/09/23/do-landlords-have-provide-ac-and-heat-for-renters-heres-what-ga-law-says), the law doesn't require landlords to install HVAC if none exists, but landlords must repair a system that came with the unit.

What the law doesn't specify: a repair timeline. Georgia sets no "must fix within 24 hours" rule. What it creates is liability exposure for owners who know about a habitability failure and fail to act within a reasonable time. *This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice; consult a Georgia real estate attorney for your situation.*

The practical standard at McBride PM: emergency repairs are dispatched the same day they're reported, with the goal of resolution within 24 hours for single-trade emergencies — a heat source, a water supply, an electrical panel issue. Multi-trade or complex repairs get a temporary fix — a space heater, a water shutoff, a boarded entry — while the full repair is scheduled.

## Emergency, Urgent, Routine: How We Classify Every Repair Request

Not every 10 p.m. call is an emergency. The single most common source of after-hours cost overruns is responding to routine issues at emergency rates — because no one defined the difference in the management agreement.

Here's how McBride PM classifies repair requests:

| Classification | Definition | Response Time Target | Authorization |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency** | Threatens safety, habitability, or structural integrity; cannot wait until business hours | Dispatch within 2 hours | Act immediately, notify owner same day |
| **Urgent** | Serious functional impairment, not an immediate safety risk | Schedule within 24–48 hours | Standard approval workflow |
| **Routine** | Non-urgent repair or cosmetic issue; no safety impact | Next available slot | Standard approval workflow |

**Emergency: act within two hours**

- Active water leak, burst pipe, or interior flooding
- Total loss of heat when outside temperatures are below 40°F or forecast overnight low
- Total loss of cooling during a heat advisory (Georgia DCA guideline: sustained 95°F+)
- Gas smell or suspected gas leak anywhere in the unit
- Sewage backup entering the living area
- Electrical hazard — burning smell, sparking outlet, panel trip that won't reset
- Compromised entry point — break-in, kicked door, window that can't be secured

**Urgent: schedule today or tomorrow**

- HVAC running but clearly failing: not reaching set temperature, unusual noise, ice forming
- Water heater producing inadequate hot water or showing signs of early failure
- Single-fixture plumbing (one toilet, one faucet) that doesn't affect other fixtures
- Minor electrical problem isolated to one non-essential room or circuit
- Roof leak during active rain — tarp and schedule full repair

**Routine: next available slot**

- Dripping faucet that isn't worsening
- Broken blinds or interior hardware not affecting safety
- Minor cosmetic damage (scuff, small hole, trim gap)
- Appliance malfunction for appliances not listed in the lease
- Garage door sensor misalignment with no safety hazard

This framework is the same one your management agreement should reflect. When tenants, vendors, and the McBride PM team all operate from the same definitions, after-hours calls get triaged correctly — not just dispatched because a tenant is persistent.

## The Spending Authorization Threshold: What McBride Can Do Without Calling You

Every McBride PM management agreement includes two authorization tiers that govern repair spending.

**Standard repair threshold:** The dollar amount per work order below which McBride PM can approve and commission repairs without contacting the owner first. Typically set at $200–$300 per incident. It covers the majority of routine repairs — filters, minor plumbing, appliance adjustments — that don't need owner input.

**Emergency threshold:** A separate, higher authorization amount that applies when a repair meets the emergency definition above. Typically set at $500–$750 per incident. When a true emergency hits, McBride PM dispatches immediately and notifies the owner afterward, rather than waiting for approval that could take hours to obtain.

These numbers matter most to PCS landlords. If you're deployed or overseas when a Georgia emergency hits at 3 a.m. your time, the emergency threshold is the mechanism that lets your property manager act without a 2 a.m. call. It isn't a blank check — every emergency repair is documented with photos, contractor invoices, and a line item in your AppFolio owner statement within 24 hours. It's a pre-authorized spending ceiling that puts action before approval in situations where waiting creates liability.

**How to set your threshold well:**

The number should cover one single-trade emergency service call — HVAC dispatch, emergency plumber, emergency electrician — including after-hours rates. In the Augusta, Evans, and Grovetown markets, a typical after-hours HVAC service call runs $250–$500 depending on scope; emergency plumbing calls for a burst-pipe shutoff and basic repair range from $350 to $600. A $500 emergency threshold covers most single-trade scenarios. A $750 threshold covers larger single-trade events with parts.

What it does NOT cover: multi-trade emergencies, structural events (major roof failure, foundation), or any repair requiring a licensed contractor to pull permits. Those always involve owner contact regardless of threshold.

## What Happens When a True Emergency Hits

Here's the step-by-step when a tenant reports a true emergency at a McBride PM property:

1. **Tenant contacts the after-hours line.** Tenants have a 24/7 emergency number for situations that meet the definition above. After-hours calls are triaged by our on-call coordinator.

2. **Triage and classification.** The coordinator confirms the issue meets the emergency definition, asks the tenant to describe the problem in detail, and confirms whether the situation is currently safe — is anyone in immediate danger, can they stay in the unit tonight.

3. **Vendor dispatch.** For confirmed emergencies, McBride PM dispatches from our established vendor roster — HVAC technicians, plumbers, and electricians in Evans, Grovetown, Martinez, and Augusta who have confirmed after-hours availability.

4. **Temporary mitigation where needed.** If a full repair can't be completed overnight — parts on order, multi-day job — we arrange a temporary fix: space heater for heat loss, water shutoff with bottled water for certain plumbing situations, board-up for a compromised entry.

5. **Owner notification.** Within 24 hours of dispatch, the owner receives a notification through AppFolio with a description of the issue, photos from the property, a summary of work performed or in progress, and the invoice when available. If the repair exceeded the emergency threshold, we contact the owner directly before commissioning additional work beyond the initial stabilization.

6. **Full invoice and documentation.** Every emergency repair is posted to the owner's AppFolio statement with contractor invoices attached. Nothing is absorbed into the management fee — costs flow through as documented expenses.

If you're a Fort Gordon PCS landlord — or any owner who may be unreachable during U.S. business hours — this process is designed so you don't need to be in the loop for every step. You need to be notified; you don't need to be the bottleneck. ## What McBride Does NOT Consider an Emergency

After-hours calls drive after-hours costs. Managing a rental well includes managing tenant expectations about what qualifies as an emergency — so after-hours resources are reserved for real ones.

The following are not emergencies in McBride PM's operational framework, even when reported after hours:

- A single toilet that runs or flushes slowly (if other toilets in the unit function)
- A dishwasher that's not working (dishwashers are typically not covered by the habitability warranty)
- An appliance not listed in the lease that malfunctions
- A broken interior door handle or cabinet hinge
- A light bulb out or a single non-essential circuit
- Minor pest sightings (one ant or roach in a clean unit — not an active infestation)
- Cosmetic damage that occurred mid-tenancy
- HVAC not reaching the exact desired temperature when ambient temperature is moderate

This list is communicated to tenants at move-in through our [Maintenance Request Guide](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Maintenance-Request-Guide.pdf), so the first time a tenant hears it isn't at 10 p.m. when they're frustrated about a running toilet.

The test we apply: would a reasonable person consider this situation unsafe or unlivable *right now*? If yes, it's an emergency. If it's inconvenient but the tenant can safely stay in the unit tonight, it's urgent or routine.

## After-Hours Costs: Why Emergency Repairs Run Higher

This is important context for any owner setting their emergency spending authorization. After-hours service calls cost more than scheduled daytime repairs — not as a penalty, but because the vendors performing them face real costs: after-hours labor rates, overtime pay, and premium-pricing on emergency parts procurement.

In the CSRA market, owner-facing ranges as of fall 2026:

| Repair Type | Standard Business Hours | After-Hours Emergency |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC service call (diagnostic + labor) | $150–$300 | $300–$500 |
| Emergency plumbing (burst pipe, shutoff) | $200–$350 | $400–$650 |
| Emergency electrician (safety call) | $200–$400 | $350–$550 |
| Emergency locksmith / re-key | $100–$200 | $175–$325 |

These are CSRA market ranges as of fall 2026. Specific quotes vary by vendor, scope, and parts availability. The point: a $500 emergency threshold is calibrated to these real costs and covers most single-trade after-hours calls.

This is also why proactive maintenance matters. Catching a failing water heater during an annual inspection — a $150–$300 service visit during business hours — is far better than the same water heater failing at midnight and flooding the subfloor. As [Buildium's property management maintenance guide](https://www.buildium.com/blog/property-management-maintenance-emergency-guide/) notes, emergency maintenance costs typically run two to three times the standard repair cost when after-hours rates and rush parts are factored in.

## Setting Up Before Your PCS: Five Things to Do Now

If you're a [Fort Gordon](/blog/pcs-fort-gordon-rent-home-landlord-timeline/) landlord with PCS orders coming — or you're already remote and reviewing your setup — here are the five things that matter most for emergency maintenance coverage.

**1. Confirm your emergency spending authorization in writing.**
Make sure your management agreement specifies both a routine repair threshold and a separate emergency threshold. If it isn't written down, it doesn't exist. Ask McBride PM to confirm yours before you leave the CSRA.

**2. Verify your AppFolio notification settings.**
Log into your [owner portal](https://www.appfolio.com/) and confirm email notifications are active for work orders, invoices, and maintenance updates. Use an email address you can check from overseas — not a work account behind VPN you won't have access to.

**3. Name a backup contact.**
If a repair comes in over your emergency threshold, McBride PM needs someone stateside who can authorize. Provide a phone number and email in writing before departure: a family member, Power of Attorney holder, or trusted contact who understands the scope.

**4. Clear known deferred maintenance before you leave.**
Don't leave items for a tenant to discover in January. Ask us for a pre-PCS walkthrough — it catches slow-draining sinks, aging water heaters, and weatherstripping gaps before they become 2 a.m. emergency calls from Evans.

**5. Download and review the PCS Quick-Start Guide.**
Our [PCS Landlord Quick-Start Guide](/pdfs/McBride-PM_PCS-Landlord-Quick-Start-Guide.pdf) covers the full owner setup: emergency contacts, AppFolio configuration, lease clause review, and the insurance checklist every PCS owner should complete before handing over keys.

## Preventive Maintenance as Emergency Prevention

The best emergency story is the one that never happened.

In October and November, McBride PM proactively schedules HVAC pre-season service for managed properties — filter change, coil cleaning, capacitor check — so heat is reliable before the first cold snap in Columbia County. A failing capacitor caught during a $100 pre-season tune-up beats a $450 after-hours call in January when it finally quits.

The fall maintenance window is also when we address:

**Gutter clearing and downspout inspection.** Blocked gutters in a CSRA fall and winter contribute to fascia rot, ice damming in hard freezes, and eventually interior water intrusion. Our [fall maintenance checklist for CSRA landlords](/blog/fall-maintenance-checklist-csra-rental-property-landlords/) covers the full inspection sequence.

**Water heater assessment.** Any unit over 8 years old gets a visual inspection for corrosion, expansion tank condition, and sediment. A water heater showing signs of failure in October is a manageable replacement; one that fails during a January cold snap is an emergency plus a flooded utility room.

**Weatherstripping and door seals.** Drafts in Evans and [Grovetown](/grovetown/) homes drive tenant comfort calls through the winter. Addressing them proactively prevents habitability questions if temperatures drop hard.

**Smoke and carbon monoxide detector testing.** Required under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-18) and most homeowner's insurance policies. We test all detectors during the fall walkthrough.

Our [HVAC guide for CSRA rental owners](/blog/hvac-csra-rental-property-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/) covers the full seasonal HVAC decision tree — when to service, when to replace, and what the Safe at Home Act's new utility definition means for HVAC failure specifically.
