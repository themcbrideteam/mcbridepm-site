## The Moment PCS Orders Arrive, the Clock Starts — and the HOA Is in the Picture

You get your assignment notification — Fort Gordon to Fort Campbell, or Fort Gordon to Joint Base Lewis-McChord, or anywhere else with a 90-day out-date — and the checklist starts. Sell versus rent. Mortgage servicer notification. Property manager. Get it listed before you're gone. Most owners in Evans 30809 or Grovetown 30813 run straight to rental pricing before they've ever called their HOA management company.

That's the mistake that stalls placements. A rental cap that's full, an owner-occupancy requirement you didn't know existed, or a tenant approval process that takes two weeks can sit between a signed lease and your out-date. The gap costs real money — a month of carrying costs on a vacant property while you're already at your new duty station adds up fast.

This guide walks through what to check, in what order, when Fort Gordon PCS orders land. Most Columbia County HOA communities do allow renting. But knowing your community's specific rules before you list puts you in control of the timeline instead of chasing approvals at the worst moment.

## Why Evans and Grovetown Communities Have Higher HOA Density

Evans and Grovetown have some of the highest HOA density in the CSRA. Columbia County's rapid residential growth from roughly 2010 through 2024 produced subdivision after subdivision governed by homeowners associations: Summerfield, Columbia Lakes, River Island Estates, Riverview, Cranbrook, Moss Creek, Laurel Creek, and dozens of smaller planned communities throughout 30809 and 30813.

These aren't all identical in structure or authority. Some are informal voluntary associations with limited powers. Others are fully governed under [Georgia's Property Owners' Association Act (POAA)](https://www.fsresidential.com/georgia/news-events/articles/georgia-property-owners-association-act/), which grants them statutory enforcement tools including lien rights, fines, and in extreme cases, foreclosure. Condominium communities follow a separate Georgia Condominium Act entirely. And some older subdivisions operate under covenant-only frameworks with no statutory underpinning at all.

Why does this matter? Because the power your HOA has over your rental decision — and the legal protections available to you as a landlord — depend entirely on which framework governs your community. A POAA community can potentially amend its documents to restrict rentals with a two-thirds owner vote. A common-law covenant community typically cannot impose a new restriction that wasn't in the original documents you agreed to at closing. The Declaration you received at closing tells you which world you're in.

## The Three Restrictions Your HOA Might Impose — and How to Check Each One

Before you list on any platform or sign with a property manager, call your HOA's management company and ask specifically about each of these three restrictions.

### 1. Rental Caps

A rental cap limits the share of homes that can be rented at any one time — typically 15–25% of total units. If your community has 200 homes and a 20% cap, only 40 units may have non-owner occupants simultaneously. When that cap is full, additional owners who want to rent must join a waitlist.

**How to check:** Call the management company and ask three questions: What is the cap? How many units are currently rented? Is there a waitlist, and what is the current estimated wait?

Fort Gordon PCS timelines are usually 60–120 days from orders to departure. If the waitlist runs six months, you're facing extended vacancy, a second mortgage without rental income, or a decision to sell instead.

### 2. Owner-Occupancy Requirements

Some communities require an owner to live in the home for a set period — often 12 to 24 months — before the property qualifies for rental. If you bought your Evans or Grovetown home and lived there for only eight months before PCS orders arrived, you may not meet this threshold.

Georgia law doesn't override this restriction for service members. The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act protects against certain financial harms related to PCS orders (covered in detail at [SCRA lease termination rights for Fort Gordon landlords](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/)), but an HOA owner-occupancy requirement is a separate community-governance matter. Some communities offer a waiver process; others don't. Ask directly.

### 3. Minimum Lease Terms

Most residential HOAs in Evans and Grovetown allow standard 12-month leases without issue. But some explicitly prohibit short-term or month-to-month arrangements in the governing documents. If your declaration requires a 12-month minimum, a furnished short-term arrangement or a flexible 6-month lease to bridge a tenant transition would be a rule violation.

For most PCS landlords targeting long-term military or civilian tenants, this isn't a problem. But confirm the minimum term in writing before you set your leasing timeline.

## The HOA Tenant Approval Process — and Why You Can't Skip It

Most Evans and Grovetown HOAs with rental rules require the landlord to submit a tenant application to the HOA management company before the tenant moves in. This is separate from your own screening process. You can approve a tenant — they've passed your credit check, income verification, and background review — and the HOA approval process can still take 5–15 business days.

If you allow a tenant to move in before written HOA approval arrives and the HOA later discovers the violation, fines come to you. The HOA has no contract with your tenant — you are its only contact, and you are responsible for bringing the property into compliance.

**The typical HOA approval sequence:**

1. Obtain the HOA's rental application package from the management company (usually available on their online portal or by email request).
2. Complete the owner section: unit address, your contact information, proposed lease start date, forwarding address where you'll receive HOA communications.
3. Have the prospective tenant complete the tenant section: full legal names of all adults who will occupy the property, contact information, vehicle details.
4. Submit the package with the required fee — typically $50–$150 depending on the management company — to the HOA.
5. Wait for written approval. Do not allow the tenant to move in until it's in hand.
6. Keep the written approval in your file. If a dispute arises later about whether proper process was followed, that document is your evidence.

Building this timeline into your leasing schedule is critical for PCS landlords. If you have a 90-day window from orders to departure and you find a qualified tenant in week six, the HOA approval process needs to be running in week six — not week nine when you're finalizing the lease.

## Georgia O.C.G.A. § 44-3-226(a): The Grandfathering Protection and Its Limits

Since January 2021, Georgia law has included a specific protection for existing rental owners in POAA communities. Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-3-226(a)](https://coultersierra.com/2020/09/change-to-the-georgia-property-owners-association-act/), a Property Owners' Association cannot enforce a newly adopted leasing restriction against owners who were already renting their lots when the amendment was recorded.

**What this means in practice:** If you've been renting your Evans home since 2023, and the HOA passed a rental cap amendment in 2024 that would now prohibit new rentals, that cap cannot be applied to your unit while you continue to rent.

**What the grandfathering does NOT protect:**

- Owners who were not actively renting at the time the restriction was adopted — if your home was owner-occupied when the cap passed, you're not grandfathered
- Owners in common-law covenant communities — the statute applies only to POAA-governed associations; a community without that designation doesn't fall under this rule
- Condominium associations — governed by the separate Georgia Condominium Act
- Short-term and vacation rentals — Georgia courts have upheld HOA restrictions against these even when the owner was an active long-term landlord

There is also a genuine gray area that Georgia case law has not resolved cleanly: what happens after your current tenant vacates and you re-lease to a new tenant? Some legal commentators read O.C.G.A. § 44-3-226(a) as a continuous protection as long as you maintain an active rental use of the lot. Others argue the protection attaches to the specific active lease and expires when that tenancy ends. The answer may depend on the specific language in your community's governing documents.

This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice; talk to a Georgia real estate attorney familiar with POAA communities for your specific situation before relying on grandfathering protection.

## What Georgia SB 406 Changes — and When It Matters

Governor Kemp signed the [Georgia Property Owners' Bill of Rights Act (SB 406)](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/georgia-property-owners-bill-of-rights-6426556/) on May 12, 2026. Most provisions take effect January 1, 2027.

The law's most significant change for landlords isn't a direct rental protection — it's about HOA enforcement power. Starting January 2027, every residential Property Owners' Association must register annually with the Georgia Secretary of State. Associations that fail to register lose the ability to:

- Collect fines or fees from owners
- File or record liens against a property
- Initiate foreclosure proceedings for unpaid assessments

In practical terms: if your tenant generates HOA violations and the association wants to collect fines from you, it can only do so if it's properly registered. An unregistered HOA has no statutory enforcement tools after January 2027. The [Georgia Secretary of State's Property Owners' Associations Division](https://sos.ga.gov/page/georgia-property-owners-associations-division-faq) will administer the registration portal — that page is the authoritative source for updates as the 2027 deadline approaches.

SB 406 also doubled the minimum in unpaid dues required before an HOA can initiate foreclosure proceedings — now $4,000 in dues (not fines). And an attorney's fee provision took effect July 1, 2026, applying to HOA disputes filed on or after that date.

For a PCS landlord reading this in October 2026, the practical impact is this: the SB 406 registration deadline is 75 days away as of this writing. Before signing a lease that will take you into 2027, adding "Is this HOA currently registered under SB 406?" to your management company checklist costs nothing and may clarify the association's enforcement posture.

## Your Pre-Listing HOA Checklist

Complete this checklist before your property goes on any platform:

**Step 1 — Locate your governing documents.** Pull your Declaration (CC&Rs) and any recorded amendments. These were in your closing packet. If you no longer have them, request them from your HOA management company or search the Columbia County Clerk of Superior Court's records, where declarations are recorded.

**Step 2 — Identify which act governs your community.** Look in the Declaration for a reference to "Property Owners' Association Act" or "O.C.G.A. Title 44, Chapter 3." If it's there, you're in a POAA community. If not, you may be in a common-law covenant community — the grandfathering rule in § 44-3-226(a) won't apply.

**Step 3 — Call the HOA management company.** Ask the eight questions from the FAQ above: cap status, occupancy requirements, minimum lease term, approval process, pet restrictions, open violations on your unit, assessment due date, and SB 406 registration status. Get answers in writing or by email — verbal guidance from a management company employee doesn't protect you if the information turns out to be wrong.

**Step 4 — Check for open violations.** If there's a landscaping violation, a fence that needs repair, or any open notice on your unit, clear it before a tenant moves in. A violation that exists at move-in creates ambiguity about who caused it — and puts you in a dispute with both the HOA and potentially the tenant.

**Step 5 — Include HOA compliance in your lease.** Attach the community's governing documents as a lease exhibit. Make HOA compliance a lease condition (a material violation of HOA rules equals a lease breach). Prohibit the tenant from making any exterior modifications, landscaping changes, or structural alterations without written HOA approval. If the HOA provides a rules-acknowledgment form, have your tenant sign it.

**Step 6 — Notify your property manager of all HOA timelines.** Your property manager needs to know: the management company contact, the approval timeline, the annual assessment date, and any community-specific rules about notice for lease renewals. This is the infrastructure that makes remote ownership workable from another duty station.

## When You're Not Local: Why HOA-Governed Properties Need Professional Management

A non-HOA rental and an HOA-governed rental are not the same management problem when you're stationed 1,000 miles away. With an HOA, the ongoing obligations include:

- Annual assessment payments (typically due in January, sometimes quarterly)
- Violation response windows — most HOAs require a response within 15–30 days of a written notice
- Tenant rule violations that need immediate landlord action to avoid fine escalation
- Annual meeting documentation and any assessment increase or special assessment notices
- HOA approval process for lease renewals, new tenants, or any permitted changes to the unit

None of these are complicated individually. Combined with a full-time duty assignment, they're a recipe for a missed deadline. A fine that escalated to a lien because a violation notice went to your old address at Fort Gordon is a real scenario that comes up more than once a year.

## The Three HOA Conversations Worth Having Before You Leave

Before your departure date, schedule three direct conversations — either yourself or through your property manager — about HOA-specific items that often get missed in the PCS transition rush.

**Conversation 1: Current violation status.** Ask the management company to confirm in writing that your unit has no open violations. A violation that exists at tenant move-in creates a compliance problem on day one that's harder to resolve from 800 miles away.

**Conversation 2: Annual assessment payment process.** When is the next assessment due, and can your property manager pay it automatically from your reserve account? A missed assessment in January when you're focused on settling into a new duty station can generate late fees, and a late fee from an HOA that was already at the foreclosure-threshold edge is a problem worth preventing with a calendar reminder.

**Conversation 3: Lease renewal notification.** When your tenant renews after year one, does the HOA require re-notification or re-approval? Some communities do. Knowing this before the renewal window opens means your property manager can initiate the process on time rather than scrambling when the renewal date arrives.
