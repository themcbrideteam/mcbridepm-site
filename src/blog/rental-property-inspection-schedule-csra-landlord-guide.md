## The Four Inspection Types Every CSRA Landlord Should Expect

A serious inspection program for a single-family rental runs on four distinct event types, each with a different scope, trigger, and documentation output. The [annual property inspection](/blog/annual-property-inspection-csra-rental-landlord-guide/) is the most thorough — but it's one data point per year. The program's value comes from the full cadence.

| Inspection type | Typical frequency | Entry required? | Documentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual interior walkthrough | Once per lease year | Yes (advance notice required) | Full written report + photos in AppFolio |
| Tenant turnover (move-in / move-out) | At every tenant change | Yes | Condition report + photos in AppFolio |
| Drive-by exterior check | Quarterly | No | Notes; photos if issue found |
| Condition-triggered interior visit | As-needed | Yes (advance notice required) | Full report + photos in AppFolio |

Each type answers a different question. The annual walkthrough answers: is the property being maintained as contracted? The turnover inspection answers: what changed between tenants, and who is responsible? The drive-by answers: is anything visibly wrong right now? The condition-triggered visit answers: is what we suspected actually as serious as it looked from the street?

Together, they build a continuous monitoring record — not an annual snapshot.

## What a Quarterly Drive-By Actually Checks

A drive-by inspection takes 10 minutes and requires no tenant coordination. Amber McBride, McBride PM's operations manager, has the property management team check the following during every scheduled drive-by.

**Exterior indicators:**
- Lawn and landscaping condition — overgrown grass is often the first sign of either abandonment or a tenant who has stopped caring about the property
- Vehicle count and type — more vehicles than the lease authorizes signals potential undisclosed occupants
- Exterior condition: visible damage, broken windows, unsecured entry points, missing or damaged screens
- Trash and debris: accumulation in the yard, items stored outside that suggest interior overflow
- Unauthorized signage: "For Rent" signs, commercial advertising, satellite dishes mounted to fascia (common lease violations)
- Evidence of unauthorized modifications: new fencing, concrete poured in the yard, structural alterations

**Fall additions (September through November):**
- Gutters: visible leaf accumulation that can funnel water into fascia and foundation
- HVAC outdoor unit: vegetation clearance, unusual sounds when the heat pump cycles
- Storm envelope: any visible roofing or siding damage from late-season convective storms — the Atlantic hurricane season runs through November 30, and the CSRA sees its share of late-season systems

The drive-by is an early-warning system, not a replacement for the annual walkthrough. If a drive-by in [Evans](/evans/) or [Grovetown](/grovetown/) turns up an RV parked in the backyard, an overgrown lawn in October, or a tarp covering a section of roof — that's what triggers an unscheduled interior visit.

## Georgia's Notice Requirements and the Safe at Home Act

Georgia's [Safe at Home Act](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/) (originally passed as HB 404 and codified under O.C.G.A. Title 44, Chapter 7) created the state's first statutory warranty of habitability. It also set a clear expectation around landlord entry: the tenant's home is their home, and access requires advance notice except in genuine emergencies.

The statute doesn't specify a hard minimum notice period for non-emergency inspections. McBride PM's default — and the standard in most professionally drafted CSRA leases — is 24 hours' written notice before any interior entry. In practice that means AppFolio message plus text to the tenant, with their acknowledgment before the visit.

Drive-by inspections require no notice. You're observing the exterior from outside the property line, the same as any neighbor or passerby.

Showing up for an unannounced interior inspection — except in a genuine emergency like a gas leak or flooding — creates legal exposure and, more practically, damages the tenant relationship. Tenants who feel their privacy is respected tend to stay longer and take better care of the property. That's not just ethics; it's a retention calculation.

## Why Fall Preventive Inspections Are a Habitability Requirement

The Safe at Home Act's warranty of habitability requires landlords to maintain rental properties that are fit to live in: functioning heating and cooling, working plumbing, a structurally sound envelope. A landlord who learns about a material habitability defect and fails to address it is in breach of that warranty — regardless of whether the tenant ever filed a formal complaint.

This is where the preventive inspection cadence has a direct legal dimension. If your property manager schedules a fall preventive visit and documents that the HVAC heat exchanger shows signs of failure — and you authorize the repair before winter — you've met your habitability obligation proactively. If no one runs that visit and your tenant calls code enforcement in January because the heat pump won't switch to heat mode, you're in a reactive, legally exposed position.

## What Triggers an Unscheduled Interior Visit

Beyond the annual calendar, specific events automatically trigger a full interior inspection with appropriate tenant notice:

**Neighbor or HOA reports.** If an HOA compliance officer or a neighbor contacts McBride PM about a visible condition issue at the property — broken exterior, water running from the foundation, noise complaints — we open a work order and schedule a formal interior check. Columbia County's HOA-dense neighborhoods produce reliable early-warning signals for this reason.

**Maintenance requests that flag a larger problem.** A tenant who submits a maintenance request for "a small drip under the kitchen sink" often reveals, on inspection, that the subfloor has been wet for weeks and the particleboard has softened. Maintenance requests are audit triggers, not isolated tickets. Every request gets a physical review, not just a vendor dispatch.

**Sustained tenant non-responsiveness.** If a tenant goes more than five business days without responding to an AppFolio message or a maintenance communication, we conduct a welfare check and exterior drive-by, escalating to a formal interior visit with proper notice if warranted.

**Pre-renewal condition assessment.** Before offering a lease renewal, McBride PM runs a condition check to confirm the property warrants renewal at its current rate. If the tenant has produced wear beyond normal, that's a conversation to have before the lease is extended — not after.

**Post-storm assessment.** After any severe weather event — CSRA convective storms, tropical systems — McBride PM drives all managed properties to check the envelope before tenants can obscure new damage with temporary repairs.

## Checklist: Questions to Ask Any CSRA Property Manager About Inspections

If you're evaluating property managers in the Augusta area, or auditing whether your current PM has a real monitoring program, these are the questions worth asking:

**Program structure:**
- [ ] How many distinct inspection types do you conduct per lease year?
- [ ] Do you run drive-by checks between annual walkthroughs? How often?
- [ ] What specifically is documented during each type?

**Documentation:**
- [ ] Are all inspections documented with photos?
- [ ] How quickly after an inspection does the owner receive the report?
- [ ] Can I see a sample report before I sign the management agreement?

**Notice and compliance:**
- [ ] What notice do you give tenants before interior inspections?
- [ ] How is that notice delivered — email, written letter, AppFolio message?
- [ ] Do you confirm tenant acknowledgment before entering?

**Response protocol:**
- [ ] If a drive-by turns up a lease violation, what's the escalation path?
- [ ] What constitutes a genuine emergency that warrants immediate entry without advance notice?
- [ ] Who at your company makes the call to escalate a drive-by finding to a full interior visit?

**Remote owner communication:**
- [ ] How are inspection results communicated to owners who are deployed or out of state?
- [ ] Can I set an approval threshold so routine repairs proceed without my sign-off each time?

If a property manager can't answer the documentation and response questions specifically — or says "we do an annual walkthrough and call you if something comes up" — that's a rent-collection operation, not a monitoring program. ## What Inspections Catch That Rent Collection Misses

[NARPM research](https://www.narpm.org/) on owner retention consistently identifies two factors that predict whether an owner stays with a property manager: transparent reporting and proactive maintenance. The inspection cadence is the engine behind both.

Properties under active monitoring programs tend to produce lower turnover costs because lease violations and maintenance deferrals are caught while they're inexpensive. The [connection between responsive maintenance and tenant retention](/blog/how-maintenance-requests-affect-tenant-retention/) is well-documented — tenants who live in a property where problems are addressed promptly renew at higher rates. A quarterly drive-by that catches a deferred issue before lease-renewal time is a direct input to that retention math.

Columbia County's vacancy rate sits in the 11–12% range for the CSRA rental market overall, per [HUD's FY2026 Fair Market Rent data for the Augusta metro](https://fairmarketrentmap.com/blog/augusta-fair-market-rent-2026/). Maintaining and monitoring a property well keeps you out of that vacancy number.

## Seasonal Inspection Priorities for Fall 2026

For CSRA rental owners, the October through November window carries specific inspection priorities that sit at the intersection of habitability law and practical expense management:

1. **Heat function tested.** Before the first sub-45°F night (typically late October in the Columbia County suburbs), confirm the heat pump switches to heat mode. This is a direct Safe at Home Act habitability requirement. Test it in October — not in response to a tenant call in November.

2. **Gutters and drainage.** Columbia County averages roughly 49 inches of rainfall annually. Gutters clogged with October leaf fall push water into fascia, soffits, and eventually foundation. A gutter cleanout in October is $100–$180. Fascia replacement runs $600–$1,200 per section.

3. **Water heater condition.** Water heaters between 7 and 12 years old in CSRA properties often show anode rod depletion accelerated by the area's mineral-heavy well and municipal water supply. A fall anode check costs $50–$80 and extends heater life by 3–5 years. A failed water heater at tenant notice is an emergency replacement at weekend rates.

4. **Pest exclusion sweep.** Fall is when field mice and other rodents seek warm interior entry points. A drive-by or fall inspection that catches missing door sweeps, open foundation gaps, or compromised garage seals avoids a January pest remediation that runs $300–$800 and often requires a lease-violation conversation about sanitation.
