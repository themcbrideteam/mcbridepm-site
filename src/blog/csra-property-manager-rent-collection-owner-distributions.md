Your lease is signed. Your tenant moved in on the 1st. Rent is due by the 5th, and you're in Colorado, at a new duty station, or across town at a job that doesn't leave much time for property management. You have one practical question: when does the money actually land in your bank account, and how do you know if something goes wrong before it gets there?

It's one of the first questions new owners ask McBride PM before handing over keys — and it's a good one. The answer isn't complicated, but understanding the full payment cycle changes how you plan cash flow, set up your finances, and manage the relationship with your property manager.

Here's the full picture, from the tenant's phone to your bank account.

## How Tenants Pay Rent Through AppFolio

McBride Property Management runs all rent payments through [AppFolio Property Management](https://www.appfolio.com/) — a cloud-based platform with a dedicated resident portal for tenants and an owner portal for property owners. There are no checks mailed to an office, no cash handling, and no informal side arrangements. Every payment goes through the portal and generates a timestamped record visible to both the tenant and the management team.

Tenants have three ways to pay inside the portal:

| Payment Method | Typical Clearing Time | Fee to Tenant | Fee to Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACH bank transfer | 3–5 business days | None | None |
| Debit card | 1–2 business days | Processor fee (~$2–3) | None |
| Credit card | 1–2 business days | Processor fee (~2.9%) | None |

ACH bank transfer is the default and by far the most common. It costs the tenant nothing extra and is the most reliable method for on-time payments. Debit and credit card options are available but carry a processor fee charged to the tenant, not the owner.

### Autopay: What McBride PM Encourages for Every Tenant

When a new lease is signed, tenants are enrolled in autopay as part of the standard onboarding process. Autopay drafts the ACH transfer automatically on the rent due date — the tenant doesn't have to log in each month and doesn't risk forgetting. For owners, autopay tenants post significantly fewer late payments than manual-pay tenants, which means a more consistent distribution schedule for you.

Tenants can review payment options and manage their autopay settings through the resident portal. The [resident FAQs at McBride PM](/resident-faqs/) cover the most common questions about how payments work from the tenant side.


## The ACH Clearing Window: Why Rent Collected on the 1st Doesn't Arrive Instantly

ACH (Automated Clearing House) is the payment rail that moves money between U.S. bank accounts. Under the [NACHA Operating Rules](https://www.nacha.org/content/ach-network-rules) — the standards governing the ACH Network — standard ACH debits settle within 1–2 business days after origination. When you add AppFolio's payment verification window, the realistic timeline from tenant payment to confirmed cleared funds is 3–5 business days.

This isn't a McBride PM policy. It's the infrastructure the entire U.S. banking system runs on.

Two things this matters for:

**NSF rejections.** If a tenant's bank account has insufficient funds, the ACH transfer will appear to process but then reverse — sometimes 3–4 days after the payment looked like it went through. McBride PM monitors every payment for NSF returns and notifies owners immediately when a reversal occurs. Repeated NSF returns are treated as a delinquency event under the lease terms.

**Cash flow timing.** Knowing the clearing window helps you plan. A tenant paying on the 1st → confirmed cleared funds by approximately the 6th–8th → owner distribution initiated by the 15th → funds in your account by the 17th at the latest. The cycle is predictable once you understand it.

This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice; consult a Georgia attorney or CPA for your specific financial situation.

## The Distribution Cycle: How McBride PM Calculates What You Receive

Once rent is confirmed cleared, McBride PM calculates your owner distribution using a straightforward formula:

**Gross rent collected**
minus Management fee (percentage of collected rent per your agreement)
minus Maintenance and vendor invoices approved and paid from your account this period
minus Leasing fee (only in months when a new tenant was placed)
minus Lease renewal fee (only when a lease was renewed this period)
equals **Net owner distribution**

Every deduction appears as a line item on your owner statement with the date, vendor, and amount. Nothing is aggregated or hidden. The full fee structure is in your management agreement and is covered in detail in our [property management fees guide for Augusta-area owners](/blog/property-management-fees-augusta-ga-2026/).

McBride PM initiates owner distributions between the **10th and 15th of each month** for the prior month's collected rent. If that window falls on a federal holiday or weekend, distributions go out on the next business day. ACH transfers to domestic U.S. accounts settle within 1–2 business days.

**Example timeline for a Fort Gordon PCS landlord:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| November 1 | Tenant's autopay initiates ACH transfer |
| November 4–6 | Payment confirmed cleared; NSF window closes |
| November 1–8 | Any November vendor invoices posted to owner account |
| November 10–15 | McBride PM initiates owner ACH distribution |
| November 12–17 | Funds appear in your U.S. bank account |
| November 10–15 | Owner statement published in AppFolio portal |

From tenant payment to your account: approximately 12–17 calendar days for a tenant paying on the 1st. If you're stationed at Fort Gordon or managing remotely from another state, that timeline is consistent every month — and you can verify every step in the owner portal without making a single phone call.

## Reading Your Owner Statement and the Distribution Notification

Every distribution triggers an email notification and a new owner statement published simultaneously in the [AppFolio owner portal](/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/). You'll see the statement the moment the distribution is initiated.

A standard owner statement shows:

- **Opening balance:** reserve funds on hand at the start of the period
- **Rent received:** by property, by month, with payment date
- **Deductions:** management fee, maintenance invoices, leasing fees — each itemized with date and amount
- **Net distribution:** the amount sent, the bank account it went to, and the initiation date
- **Closing balance:** reserve on hand after the distribution

If a line item is unfamiliar, click through in AppFolio to see the underlying work order, vendor invoice, or repair photo — without contacting the office. ## What Happens When a Tenant Pays Late

Georgia has no statutory grace period for rent — rent is due on the date stated in the lease, and late fees can apply immediately thereafter under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-13/). McBride PM leases include a late fee provision that applies after a short contractual grace period (typically 3–5 days), consistent with industry standards and the Georgia DCA Landlord-Tenant Handbook.

The delinquency sequence:

1. **Day 1–5 after due date:** Autopay tenants receive a portal alert; manual-pay tenants receive a reminder notification. The owner portal shows the balance due in real time.
2. **Day 5:** Late fee charges in AppFolio. You see it immediately in your portal.
3. **Day 7–10:** McBride PM makes direct contact with the tenant.
4. **Day 14–15:** If rent remains unpaid, Noah McBride is notified and a formal 3-day demand notice is prepared under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-50/).
5. **Distribution timing:** If rent is collected after the standard clearing window for that month (roughly the 8th), it carries to the following distribution cycle. You won't receive a partial distribution and a second deposit — the amount is held and included in the next cycle, with a clear notation on the statement.

Nothing escalates beyond the demand notice without owner involvement. You're informed at each step through the portal and by email. The full delinquency process — from the first late fee through dispossessory — is covered in our [tenant late rent guide for Georgia landlords](/blog/georgia-tenant-late-rent-landlord-guide/).

## PCS Landlords: How to Receive Distributions While Stationed Overseas

If your Fort Gordon PCS takes you overseas, rental income still needs to reach you — but there's one technical limitation worth knowing before you leave: **ACH transfers in the United States cannot go directly to international bank accounts.** The ACH Network, governed by [NACHA](https://www.nacha.org/content/ach-network-rules), is a U.S.-only payment rail. International wire transfers operate differently and require a separate setup.

Your practical options:

| Option | How It Works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Keep a U.S. personal checking account open | ACH distributions land in your U.S. account; you transfer internationally as needed | Most common; cost-effective, no changes required at McBride PM |
| Joint account with a stateside spouse or family member | Shared U.S. account works seamlessly | Simple if applicable to your situation |
| U.S. brokerage or investment account | Many accept ACH deposits; deploy rental income into investments automatically | Useful for growing a portfolio while abroad |
| International wire by special arrangement | McBride PM can initiate a wire transfer instead of ACH; wire fees apply | Requires advance setup in the management agreement |

The standard recommendation from Noah McBride to every active-duty owner: keep a U.S. bank account open and active before your PCS departure. Set it as the distribution destination. Transfer funds internationally on your own cadence using your bank or a service like Wise. This approach requires no changes to your AppFolio setup and keeps the distribution cycle clean.

For the full picture of managing your CSRA rental after PCS, the [remote management guide for PCS landlords](/blog/csra-rental-remote-management-pcs-landlord/) and the [Fort Gordon PCS emergency playbook](/blog/csra-rental-emergency-playbook-pcs-landlords/) cover the complete operational setup. ## Maintenance Reserves and Their Effect on Your Distribution

First-time owners are often surprised when a distribution comes in lower than expected. Usually it's because of a maintenance charge. Understanding the reserve prevents the confusion.

McBride PM holds a **maintenance reserve** — a buffer kept in your owner account (not McBride PM's operating account) to fund routine repairs without requiring an owner transfer for each job. The reserve amount is specified in your management agreement and is typically equivalent to one month's management fee.

When McBride PM pays a vendor for a repair at your Evans or Grovetown property:

1. The invoice is posted to your AppFolio account
2. Funds draw from your reserve balance first
3. If the repair exceeds the reserve, the shortfall deducts from that month's rent distribution
4. If the reserve drops below the minimum, the distribution includes a replenishment line to bring it back up

In a month with a significant repair — an HVAC service call, a plumber visit, a water heater repair — your distribution will be lower by the repair amount. The statement shows every line item. Your reserve balance is always visible in the owner portal as your "owner balance."

The [property management fees and cost structure guide](/blog/property-management-fees-augusta-ga-2026/) details how the reserve interacts with your monthly distribution. The [Operating Expenses Worksheet PDF](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Operating-Expenses-Worksheet.pdf) lets you model realistic monthly distributions against actual CSRA maintenance cost benchmarks before you commit to a management agreement.

## Setting Up Your Distribution Account

When McBride PM onboards a new owner, the bank account setup is one of the first three items on the checklist. What you'll need:

- Nine-digit ABA routing number (found on a check or in online banking)
- Bank account number (checking or savings — either works for ACH)
- Account type: personal, joint, or business/LLC
- Account holder name matching the ownership entity on the management agreement

If your property is in an LLC, the distribution account should be the LLC's business checking account. Distributions to an LLC are treated as rental income to the entity. [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527) covers how rental income is reported whether received personally or through a pass-through entity — your CPA should confirm the setup that fits your ownership structure.

Account changes require a secure verification process through AppFolio. McBride PM cannot update distribution bank details over a phone call without documentation — this is a standard fraud-prevention control that protects every owner on the platform.

## The Checklist: What to Confirm Before Your First Distribution

Before your first tenant moves in and your first distribution cycle runs, confirm these five items are in place:

- [ ] Bank account registered in AppFolio owner portal (routing + account number verified)
- [ ] Distribution account holder name matches the management agreement
- [ ] Maintenance reserve funded to minimum per your agreement
- [ ] Tenant enrolled in autopay for ACH payments
- [ ] Email address for distribution notifications is current in AppFolio

If you're a PCS landlord: also confirm the account will remain active and accessible during your deployment, and that someone in the United States can receive statements on your behalf if needed.
