You looked at Augusta, Evans, and Grovetown. Then someone mentioned Aiken — the SC side of the river — and you started pulling numbers. What you found is that Aiken, SC is producing cash flow that the Georgia side of the CSRA hasn't consistently delivered in three years, at purchase prices still under $310,000 for a solid single-family rental.

The reason most out-of-state investors overlook Aiken is that it doesn't fit the usual story lines. It's not a high-growth Sun Belt boomtown. It's not a trendy secondary market that just appeared on every investor newsletter. It's a mid-sized South Carolina city anchored by a federal nuclear-weapons complex, sustained by generations of manufacturers, and — right now — sitting in the middle of a documented housing shortage that is straining its workforce pipeline.

That combination — stable federal employment anchor, diversifying private-sector expansion, insufficient housing supply — is exactly the kind of structural rental demand that supports long-term occupancy. If you're running a portfolio from out of state, Aiken deserves a serious look.

## Why Out-of-State Investors Are Paying Attention to Aiken Right Now

The Savannah River Site (SRS) is the single most important fact about the Aiken rental market. Located on Aiken and Barnwell County land about 25 miles southeast of the city of Aiken, SRS is a Department of Energy facility that processes and stores the nation's nuclear materials stockpile. It employs [13,387 workers as of September 2024](https://www.energy.gov/nnsa/savannah-river-site-contract), the most recent official Department of Energy count.

That number is not static. In April 2025, SRNS CEO Dennis Carr stated publicly that the SRS workforce could grow from roughly 12,000 to [as many as 18,000 workers](https://www.postandcourier.com/aikenstandard/aikenstandard/news/savannah-river-site/savannah-river-site-workforce-growth/article_bf9f65d9-16bb-4bc4-9010-c12ad18a5f3d.html) as the Savannah River Plutonium Processing Facility (SRPPF) comes online. The NNSA [extended the existing M&O contract through September 30, 2027](https://wgac.com/2026/08/13/savannah-river-site-contract-extended-through-september-2027-as-bid-review-continues/) at a value of approximately $2.3 billion — a sign of operational continuity during the rebid period.

Alongside SRS, Aiken County's private-sector base has been expanding. According to the [Western SC Economic Development Partnership's FY2025-26 annual report](https://www.postandcourier.com/aikenstandard/news/local/aiken-western-sc-economic-development-partnership-annual-meeting/article_bd4d110b-019c-427c-a809-c54241ba7e21.html), three major employer expansions were announced during the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026 — including AGY (glass fiber manufacturing, $12.1 million expansion, 80 new jobs on Wagener Road), Rolls-Royce Solutions America, and AmbioPharm — representing nearly $290 million in combined investment and 342 new jobs. The [SC Department of Employment and Workforce community profile for Aiken County](https://lmi.sc.gov/_docs/Community-Profiles/04000003_Aiken.pdf) (updated May 2026) counts approximately 76,460 employed workers and documents 14.4% job growth since 2020.

The housing side of this equation is the opportunity: a regional housing study cited by the Aiken Chamber of Commerce projects the county will fall more than 1,300 units short of the roughly 4,000 new units needed by 2030. The Chamber has flagged this shortfall as a threat to the workforce needed for SRS and the manufacturing sector. That undersupply is a direct structural driver of rental demand.

## What the Aiken Housing Market Looks Like Right Now

The Aiken Association of REALTORS reported August 2026 market data showing a median closed-sale price of **$307,950** — up 4.7% year-over-year from $295,865 at the same point in 2025. Year-to-date through August, the median stands at $309,900. Active inventory was 1,401 homes at 4.9 months of supply, which is roughly balanced — neither distressed nor overheated.

ZIP code matters for investors sizing up acquisition cost:

| ZIP Code | Area | Approx. Median Closed Price (last 6 months) |
|----------|------|----------------------------------------------|
| 29801    | City of Aiken core | ~$278,000 |
| 29803    | South Aiken (Woodside, Cedar Creek area) | ~$341,000 |
| 29805    | North/East Aiken, Belvedere | ~$240,000–$265,000 |

The 29801 and 29805 ZIPs tend to produce better cap rates for investors. South Aiken (29803) commands a premium on purchase price that can compress yields unless rents in that submarket support the premium.

Aiken is not a hot flipping market. Price appreciation has been steady (3.4–4.7% annually depending on source and period), not explosive. That's actually what a long-hold cash-flow investor wants: predictable appreciation, not spike-and-crash cycles.

## What Single-Family Rentals Actually Rent For in Aiken

Apartment data and single-family rental data diverge sharply in Aiken. RentCafe (which tracks apartment complexes of 50+ units) reports an average of $1,365/month as of August 2026, up 4.91% year-over-year. That number significantly underrepresents what you'll collect on a single-family home.

For houses specifically:

- **Homes.com** reports a median single-family rental of approximately **$1,950/month**
- **Apartments.com** (house listings only) shows an average of **$2,016/month** based on current Aiken listings
- **Zumper** (July 2026) reports a 3-bedroom median of **$1,950/month** and a 4-bedroom median of **$2,260/month**

For modeling purposes, a 3-bedroom single-family home in Aiken in a well-maintained condition and a market-appropriate ZIP code should rent in the **$1,950–$2,050/month range**. A 4-bedroom at $2,200–$2,400/month is achievable in south Aiken, though those purchase prices are higher.

The gap between apartment rents and house rents matters for investor modeling. The families SRS attracts — many with dual incomes, children, and long-term intent — are renting houses, not apartments. The competition set for your 3BR/2BA single-family rental is other similar houses, not apartment complexes.

## What South Carolina Landlord Law Means for Your Investment

If you're coming from a Georgia rental portfolio, the most important thing to know is that Aiken operates under [South Carolina's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (SCRLTA), Title 27, Chapter 40 of the SC Code of Laws](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php). It's a well-structured landlord-tenant statute but it has meaningful differences from Georgia's O.C.G.A. Title 44.

Key points for investors:

**Security deposits:** South Carolina has no statutory maximum deposit amount. The landlord must return the deposit (minus documented deductions) with an itemized written statement within **30 days** after the later of: termination of tenancy or tenant's delivery of possession and written demand, provided the tenant has given a forwarding address in writing. Wrongful withholding exposes the landlord to **3x the amount withheld plus attorney fees** under S.C. Code § 27-40-410.

**Non-payment of rent:** A **5-day notice to pay or quit** is required before filing for ejectment (the South Carolina equivalent of dispossessory). The eviction process goes through Magistrate's Court.

**Month-to-month termination:** Either party must give **30 days' written notice** to terminate a month-to-month tenancy under § 27-40-770.

**Habitability standard:** Like Georgia, South Carolina requires landlords to maintain the property in a fit and habitable condition. The SCRLTA gives tenants the right to withhold rent or repair-and-deduct under specific conditions.

## How Out-of-State Investors Make the Aiken Buy Work

Buying a rental in Aiken from out of state follows the same framework as any remote CSRA acquisition, with one wrinkle: South Carolina does not have Georgia's HB 399 requirement that out-of-state owners maintain a licensed in-state agent of record. That said, the practical reasons for having local professional management in place before closing are identical.

A working checklist for first-time Aiken buyers:

1. **Define your ZIP code target** — 29801 and 29805 for cash flow, 29803 for a higher-quality tenant profile at lower yield
2. **Run a full expense model** before making an offer — use real SC insurance quotes and verified Aiken County millage rates
3. **Order a licensed home inspection** from a South Carolina ASHI- or InterNACHI-certified inspector; do not skip the inspection contingency on a remote buy
4. **Verify the property is not HOA-restricted from renting** — Aiken has both HOA and non-HOA neighborhoods
5. **Get a CMA from a local agent** using actual closed comps from the Aiken MLS, not AVM estimates alone
6. **Engage a property manager before closing** — not after. The management agreement, lease, and tenant-placement process start at closing
7. **Confirm your SC landlord insurance quote** before close — not all carriers write non-owner-occupied policies in Aiken County
