**Do Augusta-area rental property owners need a business license?**
In Augusta-Richmond County: yes. Every person or entity operating a rental property within the county must obtain a Business Tax Certificate and pay the annual Occupation Tax, regardless of how many units they own. Columbia County has a parallel requirement. You didn't plan to become a landlord. Maybe your parent left you the house on Wrightsboro Road. Maybe your employer moved you to Dallas and you couldn't get your asking price in time. Whatever brought you here, you now collect rent — and that makes you a business operator in the eyes of Augusta-Richmond County and Columbia County.

Most accidental landlords know they need insurance. Many know they need a lease. Very few know they need a Business Tax Certificate before they hand over keys. That gap can cost you: in late penalties, in fines, and — in the worst case — in a magistrate court proceeding where the tenant's attorney notices you're not registered.

Here's what each jurisdiction in the CSRA actually requires, and what to do before December 31.

## Does Georgia State Law Require a Landlord License?

Georgia does not require a statewide landlord license. O.C.G.A. Title 44 Chapter 7, which governs the landlord-tenant relationship, says nothing about registration or licensing. You do not need a license from the Georgia Secretary of State's office simply to rent a house.

What Georgia does require is compliance with its building and habitability standards under the Safe at Home Act (HB 404), proper lease documentation, correct security deposit handling, and — for out-of-state owners — compliance with HB 399 (signed May 2025). That last item we'll cover below.

The licensing question is purely local. It falls under each county or city's occupation tax ordinance, which is authorized by Georgia's Occupation Tax statute at O.C.G.A. § 48-13-9 through § 48-13-26. What those ordinances say varies — and in the CSRA, both Augusta-Richmond County and Columbia County have clear requirements that apply to landlords.

## Augusta-Richmond County: The Business Tax Certificate

Every person or entity engaged in any business, trade, profession, or occupation within Augusta-Richmond County (excluding the City of Hephzibah and the Town of Blythe) must register with the License and Inspection Department and obtain a [Business Tax Certificate](https://www.augustaga.gov/207/Occupation-Tax). This includes residential rental property owners.

**How the tax is calculated.** The Occupation Tax is based on a percentage of estimated gross receipts for the year. The fee tier depends on which of the county's defined "professions and occupations" categories your activity falls under. Rental income from residential property generally falls under real estate-related activity. The exact schedule is published on augustaga.gov and can change with each budget cycle — verify the current rate when you register.

**The certificate expires December 31 every year.** No grace period after the calendar year ends; late renewals carry interest and penalties. If you are a landlord in Augusta-Richmond County who has not yet renewed for 2026, the deadline is December 31, 2026.

**Where to apply.** Augusta-Richmond County accepts Business Tax Return filings online through the Augusta CityView Portal. You'll need:

- The property address
- Proof of ownership (deed or current property tax bill)
- Your EIN or SSN
- Estimated gross receipts for the year

**The certificate must be displayed.** Augusta-Richmond County code requires the Business Tax Certificate to be displayed conspicuously at the business premises. For a rental property, the practical equivalent is keeping the certificate in your lease file and providing a copy to your property manager. If a tenant or their attorney requests documentation of your license status, you want to be able to produce it.

**LLC-owned properties.** If your rental is held in an LLC, the LLC must register and pay the Occupation Tax in the entity's name. The LLC protection and the licensing requirement are independent — one doesn't replace the other. ## Columbia County: Evans, Martinez, Grovetown, and Unincorporated Areas

Columbia County requires all businesses operating in unincorporated portions of the county — which includes most of Evans and Martinez — to obtain a business license and pay an occupational tax. The Columbia County Licensing Department handles applications; the Business License and Occupational Tax Application is available at [columbiacountyga.gov](https://www.columbiacountyga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/917/Business-License-and-Occupational-Tax-Application-PDF).

The structure mirrors Augusta-Richmond County: annual fee based on gross receipts, required renewal, and a certificate covering the calendar year.

**Incorporated cities within Columbia County — Grovetown and Harlem — maintain their own business license requirements.** If your rental is inside Grovetown city limits, confirm directly with the City of Grovetown about its business license process. A property in unincorporated Columbia County near Evans or Martinez falls under the county's requirements.

**Why this matters for accidental landlords.** If you inherited a home in Evans or converted your Martinez home to a rental before relocating, the clock on your business license obligation started the moment you began collecting rent — not the moment you decided to register. Columbia County's occupational tax ordinance, like Augusta-Richmond County's, does not have a startup exemption for new or accidental landlords.

A good property manager will flag this during onboarding. If you self-managed through the transition period, check your registration status before year-end. ## South Carolina: Aiken, North Augusta, and Aiken County

South Carolina takes a different statutory approach. SC Code §§ 6-1-820 through 6-1-990 authorize local governments to levy business license taxes based on gross income earned within their jurisdiction. Every municipality in Aiken County that has adopted a local business license ordinance can require landlords operating within their city limits to obtain a license and pay the annual fee.

**City of Aiken.** The City of Aiken requires registration and accommodation tax remittance for short-term rentals (vacation rentals and Airbnb-type properties). For long-term residential leases, confirm current requirements directly with the City of Aiken Business Licensing Office at (803) 642-7642. Requirements can change with each budget cycle.

**North Augusta.** The City of North Augusta has its own business license structure. Landlords who own property within North Augusta city limits should contact the City of North Augusta Licensing Office for current requirements. Properties in the Riverside Village area or other city-limit developments need to confirm this directly before placing a tenant.

**Unincorporated Aiken County.** Aiken County may require an annual business license based on gross revenue for businesses operating in unincorporated areas. Contact the Aiken County Business License Office at (803) 642-1508 to verify current requirements and fee schedules.

**Key difference from Georgia:** South Carolina does not have the same preemption on rental registration that Georgia does (see below). Some South Carolina counties and cities have moved toward landlord registration programs in recent years. Check the current status in your specific municipality before assuming no registration is required.

## Georgia's Rental Registration Ban — What It Does and Doesn't Cover

Here's a source of genuine confusion: Georgia has prohibited local governments from requiring landlords to maintain a rental registry since 2003. HB 399, signed into law in May 2025 by Governor Kemp, reinforced that prohibition. The statute is clear: in no event may a Georgia local government require the registration of residential rental property.

This **does not** mean Georgia landlords are exempt from business licensing. The Business Tax Certificate and Occupation Tax are not "rental registration" — they are taxes on business activity that apply to all businesses, including rental businesses. The distinction:

| Requirement | What it is | Allowed in Georgia? |
|---|---|---|
| Rental registry | A government database tracking which properties are rented, tenant information, condition reports | **Banned** under O.C.G.A. § 36-60-25 and HB 399 |
| Business Tax Certificate / Occupation Tax | A tax on business income, applicable to all businesses | **Required** in Augusta-Richmond County, Columbia County, and others |
| Rental inspection program without probable cause | Mandatory inspections of rental properties absent a complaint | **Banned** under HB 399 |

So: no county in Georgia can require you to register your rental property in a rental database. But every county that has adopted an occupation tax ordinance can — and does — require you to pay that tax if you operate any business, including a rental business, within its jurisdiction.

## The HB 399 Overlay: Out-of-State Owners Face Additional Rules

If you own rental property in Georgia but live outside Georgia, HB 399 adds a requirement on top of local business licensing. Since May 2025, out-of-state owners of single-family homes and duplexes must either:

1. **Hire a Georgia-licensed property manager** to serve as the management contact, or
2. **Designate a local contact** — a person with a Georgia address who can accept legal service, respond to tenant maintenance concerns, and be reached within a reasonable time.

Violation of HB 399 can create legal exposure in tenant disputes and potentially affect your ability to pursue a dispossessory. The law was designed to ensure CSRA tenants — including Fort Gordon military families — have a reachable point of contact when something goes wrong with the property.

## What Happens If You Don't Register?

The risks fall into three categories:

**Direct financial penalties.** Both Augusta-Richmond County and Columbia County assess late fees and interest on unpaid occupation taxes. If you've been operating without a certificate, the back taxes plus penalties can add up — especially for multi-year gaps.

**Complication in legal proceedings.** If a tenant files in magistrate court or you need to pursue a dispossessory, an opposing attorney may raise your unlicensed status. While Georgia courts haven't established a bright-line rule that unlicensed landlords lose their claims, it's a distraction you don't want, and judges have discretion. Being current on all licensing obligations removes that angle entirely.

**Audit exposure.** Business license compliance can intersect with local tax audits. If the county identifies rental income on a property where no Business Tax Certificate is on file, it can assess back taxes, fees, and interest going back multiple years.

The fix is straightforward. Register, pay what's owed, and stay current. Most Augusta-area accidental landlords who discover this gap are able to get current with minimal penalty by acting before year-end.

## How to Get Compliant Before December 31

Year-end is the right time to act. The Augusta-Richmond County Business Tax Certificate expires December 31. Here's a quick compliance checklist:

**For Augusta-Richmond County properties:**
- [ ] Confirm whether a current Business Tax Certificate is on file in the entity or owner name that matches your lease
- [ ] If missing or expired, complete the Business Tax Return via the Augusta CityView Portal at augustaga.gov
- [ ] Pay the Occupation Tax based on your estimated 2026 gross rental receipts
- [ ] File by December 31, 2026 to avoid late charges

**For Columbia County properties:**
- [ ] Download the Business License and Occupational Tax Application from columbiacountyga.gov
- [ ] Submit to the Columbia County Licensing Department with your rental income estimate
- [ ] Confirm whether your property is inside Grovetown or Harlem city limits, which require separate city license filings

**For South Carolina properties:**
- [ ] Contact the City of Aiken Business Licensing Office (803-642-7642) or the Aiken County Business License Office (803-642-1508) to confirm current requirements
- [ ] For North Augusta properties, confirm with the City of North Augusta Licensing Office

**If your rental is in an LLC:**
- [ ] Register the LLC for the Occupation Tax in each jurisdiction where it operates
- [ ] Make sure the certificate is in the LLC's name, not just the property owner's personal name
