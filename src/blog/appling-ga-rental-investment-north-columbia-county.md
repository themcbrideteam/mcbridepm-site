## What Is Appling, GA — and Why Does the County Matter?

[Appling](/appling/) (ZIP 30802) is the county seat of Columbia County, located approximately 20 miles northwest of downtown Augusta along the Highway 221 corridor north of I-20. The unincorporated community has a semi-rural character — larger lots, lower density than Evans or Grovetown, a mix of brick ranches, newer construction subdivisions, and pastoral acreage tracts.

Two clarifications that matter for investors:

First, **do not confuse Appling, GA with Appling County, GA**. Appling County is a separate jurisdiction 180 miles southeast, with Baxley as its county seat. The Appling that belongs to Columbia County — ZIP 30802 — is what this guide covers.

Second, the county jurisdiction matters as much as the city name. Columbia County has:
- Lower millage rates than Richmond County (which covers Augusta proper)
- The Columbia County School District, which covers Appling, Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez
- Its own planning and zoning authority, which has been actively approving new residential developments to handle projected population growth

Properties in the 30802 ZIP feed directly into [Columbia County's rental market ecosystem](/evans/) — the same demand drivers, the same applicant pool, and the same legal framework as the higher-volume submarkets. What's different is the price point and the Google story.

## The Google Data Center Campus: What It Means for Appling Landlords

On July 23, 2026, Columbia County and its development authority confirmed what real estate watchers had been speculating about for months: [Google, LLC, is the sole end-user and operator of the data center campus in Appling](https://theaugustapress.com/google-revealed-as-end-user-of-appling-data-center/). The site sits off I-20 and spans more than 1,900 acres — some reports put the broader footprint at more than 3,300 acres across the Appling and Harlem areas.

The numbers are significant. [Columbia County's development authority projects the campus will generate $4 billion for the county over 35 years](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/07/28/columbia-county-leaders-discuss-google-data-centers-proposed-tax-relief/). County leaders have proposed using the resulting tax revenue to eventually eliminate homestead property taxes — voters will decide on the underlying authorization in a November 2026 referendum.

For landlords, the immediate relevance is workforce housing demand. Data center campuses generate employment in layers:

1. **Construction phase (now through approximately 2028–2029):** Site preparation, foundation work, building construction, and utility infrastructure each require thousands of workers. Those workers need housing near the site — and the Appling/Harlem corridor is the logical commute radius.

2. **Operational phase:** Hyperscale data centers require relatively few operations staff per square foot, but they require specialized technical roles — network engineers, facilities technicians, security personnel — that tend to be well-compensated and to lease or buy near the site for years at a time.

3. **Induced employment:** Restaurants, retail, and services follow workforce concentration. The Highway 221 corridor near the campus is already seeing accelerated commercial activity.

This layered demand pattern doesn't produce an overnight rent spike. It produces a sustained, multi-year trend line that well-positioned landlords capture over a 5–10 year hold. Investors who wait until the campus is fully operational to buy will pay significantly more for the same properties.

## Who Will Rent in Appling? The Tenant Profile

Understanding the applicant pool is as important as understanding the rent numbers. Appling's tenant mix differs from Evans or Grovetown in a few ways that affect leasing strategy.

**Construction and technical workers:** The Google campus will draw workers from across the region and beyond. Some will commute from Augusta or Evans; many will rent near the site. This group tends to be higher-earning, often relocating without a family, and willing to pay a premium for a clean, well-maintained single-family home over an apartment.

**Fort Gordon service members and civilians:** Fort Gordon generates roughly 36,000 military and civilian personnel across the CSRA. The base is 25–30 miles from Appling — farther than Evans or Grovetown — but [2026 BAH rates for Fort Gordon](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/fort-gordon-bah-rental-pricing-csra-2026/) give [E-5 personnel with dependents $1,890/month and O-3s with dependents $2,250/month](https://vetcalc.org/bah/georgia/fort-eisenhower-fort-gordon/). Military tenants at those BAH tiers can afford quality single-family rentals in Appling. Some prefer the larger lots and lower density compared to newer subdivisions closer to the gate.

**Savannah River Site workforce:** The Savannah River Site (SRS), located in Aiken County, South Carolina, employs approximately 11,000 workers. Much of the SRS commute shed runs through Appling and the Highway 25 and Highway 1 corridors. For workers living on the Columbia County side, Appling is a legitimate commute for SRS employment.

**Families seeking Columbia County school access:** Families who prioritize the Columbia County School District but find Evans and Grovetown prices out of range increasingly look to Appling and Harlem as comparable alternatives with more affordable rents.

## The Investment Math: Cap Rate and Cash Flow Modeling

This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice. Talk to a CPA familiar with Georgia rental real estate for your specific situation.

A typical Appling acquisition might look like this in 2026:

**Purchase price:** $290,000  
**Gross monthly rent:** $1,575  
**Annual gross rent:** $18,900  

Less operating expenses:
- Vacancy (6%): –$1,134
- Property management (9%): –$1,701
- Property taxes (1.05% of assessed value, roughly 40% of market value in Columbia County): ~$1,218
- Insurance (landlord policy): ~$1,500
- Maintenance reserve ($100/month): –$1,200
- Total expenses: ~$6,753

**Net Operating Income (NOI):** $12,147  
**Cap rate:** 4.2% (NOI / purchase price)

That headline cap rate looks low. Here's the caveat: if Google-driven demand lifts rents to $1,800/month within three years (a 14% increase), the same property at the same purchase price returns a NOI of $14,700 and a 5.1% yield on cost. On a stabilized $1,800/month property acquired today at $290,000, you're buying future yield at a discount.

For investors using DSCR financing, a 25% down payment on a $290,000 purchase is $72,500. At current rates, a 30-year DSCR loan at approximately 7.25% on $217,500 produces monthly debt service near $1,485. Against $1,575 gross rent and $563/month in operating expenses (management + vacancy), monthly net cash flow is roughly $–475 to breakeven in year one — tightening to positive as rents grow and the loan balance amortizes.

## What to Look For — and Watch Out For — When Buying in Appling

Appling's semi-rural character creates specific due diligence considerations that differ from buying in a developed Evans subdivision.

**Well and septic:** Many Appling properties are on private well and septic rather than municipal water and sewer. Inspect both before closing. Columbia County has ordinances specifically prohibiting the Google data center from drawing on private wells — which speaks to the county's awareness of water resource constraints in the northern part of the county. Know what utilities your property uses, what their condition is, and whether county water and sewer extension is planned for the immediate area.

**Lot size and zoning:** Larger lots in Appling can be appealing to tenants, but verify zoning classification through the Columbia County GIS portal. Some agricultural or estate-zoned parcels may have restrictions on rental use or require specific licensing. Confirm before acquiring.

**HOA coverage:** Evans and Grovetown have dense HOA coverage in planned subdivisions — which creates compliance risk for landlords (see the [HOA and rental restrictions guide](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/georgia-hoa-rental-restrictions-csra-landlords/) for what to check). Appling has thinner HOA coverage, which reduces that compliance burden, but always verify deed restrictions before assuming no HOA applies.

**Commute and tenant pool depth:** The thinner applicant pool in Appling compared to Evans or Grovetown means vacancy periods can run longer if pricing is off. Plan for 8–12% vacancy in your initial underwriting — tighter once the Google campus workforce ramps up, but conservative today.

**Property condition and age:** Much of Appling's existing housing stock consists of older brick ranches (1960s–1980s) and newer construction (2005–2015). For older properties, budget for a thorough pre-purchase inspection covering HVAC, roof, electrical, and plumbing. Georgia's [Safe at Home Act (HB 404)](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/georgia-safe-at-home-act-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/) places affirmative habitability obligations on landlords — systems that work at closing need to continue working throughout the tenancy.

## Appling vs. Evans and Grovetown: A Practical Comparison

Investors regularly ask how Appling stacks up against the CSRA's more established rental submarkets. Here's an honest side-by-side:

**Choose Evans (30809) if:** You want the deepest applicant pool, the shortest Fort Gordon commute, the most active comparable sales, and you're comfortable paying for the market's premium pricing. [The Evans rental market guide](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/evans-ga-rental-market-investor-guide-2026/) covers this in depth.

**Choose Grovetown (30813) if:** You want a balance of affordability and demand — lower entry price than Evans, similar Fort Gordon commute, and the I-20 corridor's growth momentum. The [fall 2026 market update](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/csra-rental-market-fall-2026-update-investors/) includes Grovetown submarket data.

**Choose Appling (30802) if:** You want to buy at a lower price-to-rent ratio in a county with strong fundamentals, you have a 5–10 year investment horizon, and you're specifically positioning for the Google campus workforce housing demand. Lower competition, lower entry price, and a demand catalyst that has not yet been fully priced into acquisition costs.

This isn't a binary choice — many investors in the CSRA hold properties in multiple submarkets. The [CSRA investment comparison across Evans, Grovetown, and Augusta](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/evans-grovetown-martinez-augusta-csra-rental-investment-guide/) gives a fuller framework.
