## What Your Property Manager Sends You at Year-End — and When

Your PM is the source of three critical documents. All three should arrive by January 31. If any are late, contact your property manager immediately — the IRS deadline for 1099s issued to you is January 31, and your CPA will need them to file.

| Document | What It Shows | Deadline | Where It Goes on Your Return |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1099-MISC** | Gross rent your PM collected on your behalf | Jan 31 | Schedule E, Line 3 (Rents received) |
| **Annual Owner Statement** | All income, expenses, fees, and disbursements for the year | Jan 15–31 | Every expense line on Schedule E |
| **Maintenance Invoice Report** | Itemized repair and maintenance costs with vendor invoices | Jan 15–31 | Schedule E, Line 14 (Repairs) |
| **Security Deposit Activity Summary** | Deposits held, applied, or returned | Jan 31 | Not a deduction — but required if a deposit was applied to damages (becomes income) |

If you haven't logged in recently, this is a good time to confirm your email address is current so the year-end package reaches you. Our team coordinates the annual distribution; if anything looks off on your statement, call our office at (706) 339-2874 or email [robert@mcbride-pm.com](mailto:robert@mcbride-pm.com).

One point that trips up remote owners: the 1099-MISC reports **gross rent** — the total collected before management fees, repairs, and any other expenses are deducted. That's not your taxable income. Your Schedule E is what converts gross rent into net rental income (or a loss). The gross number on the 1099-MISC and the expenses on your owner statement reconcile on Schedule E. Your [owner statement line items](/blog/reading-owner-statement-property-management-augusta-ga/) are your deduction documentation.

Your lender also sends a document: **Form 1098**, mailed by January 31. This reports mortgage interest paid in 2026. Mortgage interest is one of the largest Schedule E deductions most CSRA landlords have. Cross-reference it against the loan payment breakdown on your owner statement.

## The Six Deductions CSRA Landlords Consistently Miss

You probably know about mortgage interest and property taxes. These six get missed more often:

**1. Depreciation.** The IRS lets you recover the cost of the building (not land) over 27.5 years under the Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System (MACRS). On a $250,000 home with $50,000 allocated to land, the annual depreciation deduction is roughly $7,273 — every year, whether or not you spent a dollar on the property. This is calculated on Form 4562 and flows to Schedule E. Many first-year landlords don't realize their CPA needs the original **Closing Disclosure** from the purchase to establish the cost basis. Find it now, not in February.

Per [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527), residential rental property is depreciated over 27.5 years starting the day it's placed in service (the day it's ready and available to rent, not the day the tenant moves in).

**2. Property management fees.** Your management fee — typically a percentage of collected rent — is fully deductible on Schedule E, Line 10 (Other). It's already itemized on your AppFolio owner statement. No separate documentation needed; the statement is the record.

**3. Landlord insurance premium.** Your insurance invoice is deductible in the year paid. If you pay annually in advance, it may straddle two tax years — your CPA needs the invoice showing the coverage period and amount paid.

**4. Property taxes.** Columbia County and Richmond County property taxes are deductible in the year paid on Schedule E. This is separate from the State and Local Tax (SALT) deduction on Schedule A. The rental property portion goes on Schedule E, not Schedule A.

**5. Travel to the property.** If you flew from your duty station to Augusta to inspect the property or oversee work, those travel costs are deductible. For Fort Gordon PCS landlords, this often means a trip home during leave or TDY. Mileage at the 2026 standard rate is also deductible for driving to the property from the Augusta area. Keep a mileage log.

**6. Repairs and maintenance (versus improvements).** Repairs — fixing a leaking faucet, patching drywall, replacing a broken window — are deductible in the year paid. Capital improvements — a new HVAC system, a roof replacement, new flooring throughout — must be capitalized and depreciated. The line between repair and improvement isn't always obvious. Your [maintenance invoice report](/blog/property-management-maintenance-vendors-csra-augusta-ga/) from AppFolio gives you the itemized list; your CPA makes the call on each item.

## How Georgia State Taxes Work for Out-of-State Rental Owners

If you're a Fort Gordon service member now stationed elsewhere, or you relocated after PCS, you still owe Georgia income tax on income earned from Georgia sources — including rent from a property in Evans, Martinez, or Grovetown.

Georgia uses your **federal adjusted gross income** as the starting point for the state return. Your net rental income or loss from Schedule E carries into your Georgia Form 500. Out-of-state residents file as nonresidents and report only Georgia-source income.

The [Georgia Department of Revenue Form 500 instructions](https://dor.georgia.gov/) walk through the Schedule 3 nonresident computation. In plain terms: your federal Schedule E result (net rental income from your Georgia property) gets reported on your Georgia return and taxed at Georgia's flat rate. Your CPA will handle this, but you need to tell them explicitly that the property is in Georgia and provide your Georgia address (your property address or your accountant's address works as the filing address if you have no Georgia residence).

One nuance for PCS landlords: your **military pay is not taxed by Georgia** if you're a nonresident service member stationed elsewhere — but **rental income from a Georgia property is**. These are two different income streams with different rules under [O.C.G.A. § 48-7-1](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/). Make sure your CPA knows this distinction. The scenario where a service member forgets to file Georgia because "I don't live there anymore" and then gets a notice is one we see frequently.

## The Schedule E Map: What Goes on Which Line

[Schedule E (IRS Form 1040, Part I)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se) is the form where rental income and expenses live. Here's how the CSRA landlord's documents map to it:

| Schedule E Line | Expense Category | Your Source Document |
|---|---|---|
| Line 3 | Rents received | 1099-MISC from PM; Annual Owner Statement |
| Line 5 | Advertising | Owner Statement (vacancy marketing fees) |
| Line 6 | Auto and travel | Mileage log; flight/hotel receipts |
| Line 9 | Insurance | Landlord insurance invoice |
| Line 10 | Legal and professional fees | PM management fees; attorney invoices if any |
| Line 11 | Management fees | Owner Statement (management fee line) |
| Line 12 | Mortgage interest | Form 1098 from lender |
| Line 14 | Repairs | Owner Statement / maintenance invoice report |
| Line 16 | Taxes | County property tax bill (paid amount) |
| Line 18 | Other | Misc. deductible expenses with receipts |
| Line 20 | Depreciation | Form 4562 (your CPA calculates this) |

Line 20 (depreciation) requires your CPA to have your original cost basis from closing and any prior-year depreciation schedules if this isn't the first year. If you've owned the property for multiple years and are switching CPAs or picking up the return after doing it yourself, finding those prior-year schedules before December 31 saves a lot of back-and-forth in February.

## Year-End Timing: Actions That Must Happen Before December 31

Not every deduction is passive — some require action before December 31 to count in the 2026 tax year.

**Prepay January's mortgage payment.** If your January 2027 payment is due January 1, making it on December 31 moves the interest into the 2026 tax year. This is a common strategy for landlords with positive rental income who want to increase their 2026 deductions. Confirm the exact timing with your mortgage servicer — some servicers allocate interest based on receipt date, others on due date.

**Pay outstanding repair invoices.** If your property manager has authorized a repair that isn't invoiced yet, follow up and confirm it's invoiced and paid before December 31. Repairs paid in 2026 are deductible on the 2026 return; repairs paid in January 2027 push to next year.

**Review vacancy days.** If the property was vacant for part of the year, different rules apply for the personal-use calculation. As long as the property was available to rent at fair market value and you weren't using it personally, you can generally deduct full expenses and depreciation even during vacancy periods. Tell your CPA how many days, if any, the property sat vacant.

**Check your 1099 obligations.** If you self-managed part of the year or paid any contractors directly (not through McBride PM), you may have a 1099-NEC obligation for unincorporated vendors paid $600 or more. The deadline to send 1099-NECs to recipients is January 31, 2027, and e-filing is required for most landlords. Talk to your CPA about whether you have any filing obligations.

## Building Your CPA Package: The Complete Year-End Checklist

When you hand your CPA a complete package, you reduce their time on your return — which usually means a lower bill and fewer back-and-forth requests. Here's the complete list for a CSRA rental owner:

**From your property manager:**
1. Annual Owner Statement (full year, all transactions) — from AppFolio portal
2. 1099-MISC from McBride PM — arrives by January 31
3. Maintenance and repair invoice report (full year) — from AppFolio portal
4. Security deposit activity report — if any deposits were applied or returned

**From your lender:**
5. Form 1098 (mortgage interest statement) — arrives by January 31

**From the county:**
6. Paid property tax bill for 2026 — Columbia County or Richmond County assessor website

**From your insurance carrier:**
7. Paid landlord insurance invoice for 2026

**From your own records:**
8. Original Closing Disclosure from purchase (establishes cost basis for depreciation)
9. Prior-year depreciation schedule (Form 4562 from last year's return)
10. Mileage log for property-related travel
11. Receipts for any capital improvements (HVAC, roof, appliances) paid in 2026
12. Any loan payoff statements if you refinanced in 2026

**The one-page property summary your CPA will thank you for:**
- Property address and ZIP code
- Date the property was first placed in service as a rental
- Original purchase price and land value allocation
- Any periods of personal use (usually zero for a managed rental)
- Periods of vacancy in 2026 (if any)
