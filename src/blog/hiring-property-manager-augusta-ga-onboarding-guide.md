## Step 1 — Signing the Management Agreement

Once you decide to move forward, the first formal document is the [property management agreement](/services/).

Georgia law requires that anyone collecting rent or managing property on behalf of another hold an active real estate broker's license. This is codified in [O.C.G.A. § 43-40-1](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-43/chapter-40/section-43-40-1/). Noah McBride is the licensed broker of record for McBride Property Management, holding active licensure in both Georgia and South Carolina, which is why we can manage properties on both sides of the river. Before signing with any property manager, ask for their license number and verify it at the [Georgia Real Estate Commission's public license lookup](https://verify.gsccca.org/). It takes two minutes.

The agreement itself defines:

- **Management fee** — typically expressed as a percentage of monthly rent collected (not gross rent on the lease, but actual rent received)
- **Leasing fee** — a one-time fee for placing a new tenant, usually equivalent to a portion of one month's rent
- **Renewal fee** — a smaller fee when an existing tenant renews their lease
- **Maintenance authorization threshold** — the dollar amount the manager can spend on repairs without contacting you for approval; above that amount, they call first
- **Security deposit handling** — where the deposit is held, who controls it, and the process for deductions
- **Termination terms** — how either party can exit the agreement and under what conditions

Georgia law does not cap management fees, so all of these are negotiable before signing. The [property management fees post](/blog/property-management-fees-augusta-ga-2026/) covers what typical CSRA fee ranges look like and what you should watch for in the math.

At the same time you sign the agreement, you'll also provide:

- A signed **W-9** (required for 1099 issuance each January)
- **Bank routing and account number** for direct deposit distributions
- **Proof of current landlord insurance** (a dwelling or landlord fire policy, not homeowners — a standard homeowners policy does not cover tenant-occupied property)
- Copies of any **existing tenant lease and security deposit records** if a tenant is already in place

## Step 2 — The Baseline Onboarding Inspection

Within the first few days after signing, we schedule a thorough inspection of the property. This is not a casual walkthrough. It is a dated, photographic, written record of the condition of every room, every system, and every surface in the home — the legal baseline against which the tenant's eventual move-out will be measured.

**Why this step is non-negotiable.** If a tenant damages something during their tenancy and you want to make a legitimate deduction from their security deposit, you need evidence that the damage was not there before they moved in. Without a documented baseline, courts routinely side with tenants on disputed deductions, because the burden of proof is on the landlord. The [move-in and move-out inspection guide](/blog/move-in-move-out-inspection-rental-property-georgia-landlords/) goes deeper into the legal requirements under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33 and how courts in Columbia County and Richmond County have handled disputed deposit cases.

The inspection also serves a capital-planning function. A well-documented baseline tells us which systems are aging — a water heater that's eight years old, a roof showing early granule loss, an HVAC unit entering its final years — so we can alert you before an emergency happens rather than after.

**Owner documents to have ready before the onboarding inspection:**

- Copy of the property deed or most recent property tax statement (proof of ownership)
- Current landlord insurance declaration page with your name and the property address
- Any existing home warranty documentation
- Appliance manuals and serial numbers if available
- HOA governing documents, fee schedule, and contact information (if the property is in an HOA)
- Keys: front door, back door, mailbox, any outbuilding, and garage door openers
- Access codes: keypad entry, alarm system, gate codes
- Utility account numbers for any utilities currently in the owner's name

Once the inspection is complete and the documents are processed, we activate your access to the AppFolio owner portal.

AppFolio is the property management software McBride PM uses to track everything — rent collection, maintenance work orders, vendor invoices, owner statements, and lease documents. As the property owner, your portal gives you 24/7 read access to all of it. The [AppFolio owner portal walkthrough post](/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/) covers exactly what you'll see there and what each section means.

During onboarding, Amber walks every new owner through the portal on a brief call or screen share. By the time your first tenant moves in, you should know:

- Where to find your monthly owner statement
- How to see a maintenance work order from open to closed
- Where to access uploaded lease documents and inspection reports
- How to download your year-end 1099 each January

We also establish the **maintenance reserve** at this step — typically $250–$500 per property held in your owner account to cover routine repairs without requiring a wire transfer from you every time a faucet needs a new washer. The reserve amount is specified in your management agreement. It is your money; it shows up in your owner statement and can be refunded when the agreement ends.

The bank account you provided at signing is linked to your owner profile, and your distributions will go there by direct deposit. At McBride PM, distributions go out by the 10th of each month.

## Step 5 — Lease Execution and Move-In

Once an applicant is approved, we move quickly. Delays between approval and lease signing create opportunities for good tenants to accept other offers.

The lease is executed digitally through AppFolio's e-signature workflow. Under [O.C.G.A. § 10-12-1 (Georgia's Electronic Records and Signatures Act)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-10/chapter-12/), electronic signatures are legally binding on residential leases. The tenant pays the first month's rent and the security deposit at signing.

**Security deposit handling.** Georgia law under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-31 requires that security deposits be held in a separate escrow account, not commingled with operating funds. At McBride PM, security deposits are held in a dedicated trust account separate from all other accounts. This protects both you and the tenant, and it means we can produce documentation if a deposit dispute ever ends up in magistrate court.

Move-in day includes a thorough move-in inspection with the incoming tenant — a room-by-room walkthrough with photos and written notes, signed by the tenant. This signed document is what the tenant's security deposit claim is measured against at move-out. [Our Move-In Inspection Checklist PDF](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Move-In-Inspection-Checklist.pdf) mirrors the form used during this process.

## Your First Owner Statement: What You'll See

At the end of the first full rental month, your AppFolio owner statement is generated and available in the portal. It is a complete accounting of everything that happened that month.

A typical first-month statement for a vacant property that was just leased will show:

| Line Item | Example Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross rent collected | $1,850.00 |
| Management fee (9% example) | –$166.50 |
| Leasing fee (one-time) | –$925.00 |
| Pre-listing repair: paint touch-up | –$185.00 |
| Maintenance reserve funded | –$300.00 |
| **Net distribution to owner** | **$273.50** |

The first month often shows a lower net than subsequent months because the leasing fee and any pre-listing repairs are charged when they occur. Starting with month two, absent unusual maintenance, the statement simplifies to: rent minus management fee minus any repairs that came up.

If you've never read an owner statement before, the [owner statement walkthrough post](/blog/reading-owner-statement-property-management-augusta-ga/) explains each line in detail. ## What Good Onboarding Looks Like vs. Red Flags

Not every property manager runs this process the same way. Here's what to look for — and what to be cautious about.

| What good onboarding includes | Red flags to watch for |
|---|---|
| Written management agreement before any work begins | Verbal commitments with paperwork "coming later" |
| Documented baseline inspection with dated photos | No inspection, or inspection skipped to "save time" |
| Clear fee schedule with no ambiguous line items | Vague fees, percentage-of-gross structures that inflate billing |
| Separate escrow account for security deposits | Deposit funds held in the operating account |
| Owner portal access before first tenant moves in | No portal, or "we'll set it up eventually" |
| Licensed broker of record verifiable on state database | License number not provided, or not verifiable |
| Maintenance reserve amount specified in writing | No reserve discussed until after first repair invoice |
| Defined maintenance authorization threshold | No threshold — manager spends without limit |
| Tenant screening criteria documented and applied consistently | Screening criteria described verbally only, not in writing |

Georgia's [NARPM chapter members](https://www.narpm.org/) typically follow standardized onboarding practices because they're held to a code of ethics and professional standards. Membership is not mandatory, but it's a signal that a manager has invested in professional development in the field.

The [CSRA landlord field guide](/pdfs/McBride-PM_CSRA-Landlord-Field-Guide.pdf) has a section on evaluating a property management agreement that walks through these points in more detail. Download it for free at the resources page — it's a useful reference whether you work with us or someone else.

## Your First 90 Days as an Owner: What to Expect

Onboarding itself takes a week to two weeks. But the relationship settles into its steady rhythm over the first 90 days, and that's the period where most owners form their opinion of whether they made the right choice.

Here's what typically happens in the first 90 days:

- **Weeks 1–2:** Agreement signed, documents collected, baseline inspection completed
- **Weeks 2–4:** Any pre-listing repairs, professional photography, listing goes live
- **Weeks 4–6:** Tenant placement for a rent-ready property; longer if repairs are needed
- **Month 2:** First full month of rental activity, first owner statement generated and distributed
- **Month 3:** Rhythm established — you're getting monthly statements, any maintenance is being handled, the tenant is paying rent on time or the manager is addressing it per your agreement

By the 90-day mark, you should feel like the hardest part is behind you. The property is producing income, you understand what the owner statement shows, and you know how to reach Amber McBride at ops@mcbride-pm.com if something needs your attention.

For owners coming to us from self-management, the biggest adjustment is usually the transition from doing everything yourself to getting an update when something happens. The [true cost of self-managing post](/blog/true-cost-self-managing-rental-property-csra/) walks through what that work actually costs in time and liability exposure before you hand it over — useful reading even after you've made the decision.
