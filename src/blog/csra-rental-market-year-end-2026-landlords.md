**How did the CSRA rental market perform in 2026, and what should small landlords plan for in 2027?**
Rents across the Augusta area ended 2026 roughly flat to modestly up, with Columbia County outperforming Richmond County and single-family homes outperforming apartments. Demand anchors — Fort Gordon, the Savannah River Site, Augusta University Medical Center, and the new Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center — kept absorption healthy. Small investors entering 2027 should focus on submarket-level pricing rather than metro-wide averages.

If you own one or two rental homes in Evans, Grovetown, or the broader CSRA and you're preparing to price lease renewals, evaluate your reserve account, and decide whether to hold or sell, there's a gap between what national rent headlines say and what your actual property is experiencing. Rent data tends to flatten the CSRA into one market. It isn't one market.

This is the year-end picture: what actually happened with rents and demand in the Central Savannah River Area in 2026, what the demand drivers look like heading into 2027, and what that means for specific decisions you're about to make.

## Where CSRA Rents Actually Landed in 2026

Rents in the Augusta metro grew modestly, but the word "modestly" covers a wide range depending on what you count and where you look.

Apartment-focused platforms tell the flattest story. [RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ga/augusta/) tracked the average Augusta area asking rent at $1,253 as of late August 2026, up about 1.6% year over year. That figure includes a heavy weight of apartment-unit inventory, which has seen the most supply pressure nationally and tends to drag the headline lower than what a single-family owner experiences.

[Zumper's June 2026 research](https://www.zumper.com/rent-research/augusta-ga) puts the median closer to $1,350 for all bedroom counts, with houses averaging $1,495. [SFR Analytics](https://sfranalytics.com/rental-yields/ga/augusta-richmond-county-ga-sc) reported a metro-wide median asking rent of $1,700 for Augusta-Richmond County as of July 2026, covering larger single-family units. The spread reflects genuine variation across unit types and submarkets, not just measurement noise.

The number that matters most for voucher-eligible applicants in your tenant pool is HUD's Fair Market Rent. For FY2026, [HUD set the Augusta-Richmond County FMR](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr.html) at **$1,261 for a two-bedroom** and **$1,627 for a three-bedroom**. These figures define the payment ceiling for Housing Choice Voucher holders in this metro and affect the lower end of your applicant pool regardless of your asking price.

For specific, real-time context: as of November 21, 2026, the homes McBride Property Management has listed for rent in Evans include three-bedroom homes asking between $1,850 and $2,400 per month. Those prices reflect the Columbia County premium — location near Fort Gordon's Gate 1, the Columbia County School District, and the Wellstar corridor — and sit well above both the metro-wide apartment average and the HUD FMR. That's normal for well-maintained Columbia County homes.

What the data says in plain language: if you own a three-bedroom single-family home in [Evans](/evans/) or [Grovetown](/grovetown/) in good condition, your achievable rent is likely meaningfully above what a metro-wide average suggests. If you own older stock in Richmond County, the market is more competitive and your pricing needs to be sharper. Neither is a problem — they're different submarkets, and they perform accordingly.

## The Four Demand Drivers That Held the Floor

The CSRA's rental market in 2026 didn't grow spectacularly, but it didn't retreat either. Four institutional anchors drove that stability.

**Fort Gordon.** The installation employs approximately 31,155 military and civilian personnel and contributes more than [$7.5 billion annually to the regional economy](https://www.fortgordonalliance.com/economic-impact/), according to the Alliance for Fort Gordon. The Army Cyber Center of Excellence drew a continuing stream of technical military and civilian employees, many of whom arrive with Basic Allowance for Housing and compete directly for the same homes as working professionals. Augusta was ranked [LinkedIn's No. 1 "City on the Rise" for 2026](https://wrdw.com/2026/07/23/augusta-tops-new-linkedin-list-cities-rise/), driven partly by the installation's tech employment draw.

**Savannah River Site.** The SRS is the second major anchor on the South Carolina side of the river, drawing employees to Aiken, North Augusta, and the suburban Columbia County corridors. SRS construction and cleanup contracts bring high-income technical workers who want stable longer-term rentals within commuting distance of the Graniteville area.

**Augusta University Medical Center and Wellstar MCG.** Healthcare is a major and growing employer in the CSRA. The Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center — the first full-service hospital in Columbia County — opened in 2026 and immediately added both healthcare jobs and healthcare workers looking for rentals in Evans and Martinez. For landlords in Columbia County specifically, this is a new and significant demand layer that wasn't present in prior years.

**USC Aiken and Augusta University student population.** Both institutions contribute a less price-elastic tenant pool — students, faculty, and medical residents who need to be within a specific commute radius and are willing to pay for proximity to their campus or clinical site.

None of these drivers disappeared in 2026. All four will continue into 2027 at approximately the same scale or higher. That's the structural floor under CSRA rents, and it's the reason the market held even as national rent growth slowed.

For the broader market context from earlier this year, the [Fall 2026 CSRA rental market update](/blog/csra-rental-market-fall-2026-update-investors/) covers the institutional dynamics in detail.


## Columbia County vs. Richmond County: Two Markets in One Metro

Owners who treat the CSRA as a single market tend to either overprice Richmond County properties or underprice Columbia County ones. The two submarkets have meaningfully different fundamentals.

|---|---|---|
| Typical SFR asking rent (3BR) | $1,850–$2,400 (Nov 2026 listings) | $1,295–$2,050 (Nov 2026 listings) |
| Primary demand drivers | Fort Gordon, Wellstar MCG, Columbia County School District | Augusta University Medical Center, downtown employment, price-access |
| Supply pipeline | Limited — Columbia County growth managed | Higher — older stock, more units competing for applicants |
| Tenant financial profile | Strong BAH alignment, high income-to-rent ratios | More variable; broader income band |
| Vacancy pressure | Low | Moderate in some submarkets |

## What Absorption and Activity Tell You

The CSRA doesn't have a public quarterly vacancy report the way a larger metro does. What you can observe from the owner side is absorption: how quickly well-priced, well-prepared homes lease versus how long overpriced or under-prepared ones sit.

The consistent pattern across 2026: homes that came to market at a price supported by comparables and in move-in-ready condition leased faster than the national average for comparable secondary markets. Homes that came to market overpriced, or that had deferred maintenance visible in listing photos, sat — and owners typically ended up accepting below their starting price anyway after burning weeks of potential rental income.

The honest answer to "how long will my property sit vacant?" in the CSRA in 2026: it depends almost entirely on two controllable factors — price and condition. The demand is there. The question is whether you're asking the market to pay for your property's future potential rather than its current state. The [owner FAQs](/owner-faqs/) address this question frequently.

Seasonal pattern to understand heading into 2027: the CSRA's strongest leasing months are February through July, driven by the military PCS cycle. Official PCS orders typically drop March through June, and military families act fast once orders are confirmed. The slowest months are November and December. If you have a vacancy opening in January, get the property market-ready by mid-December and price it to move — January applicants often include service members with hard start dates who will accept list price for the right home in the right location.


## What 2026 Owners Got Right — and What They Got Wrong

Three patterns emerged this year that are worth naming directly.

**What worked: leaning into renewal retention.** Turnover in the CSRA in 2026 cost between $1,500 and $3,500 all-in for a typical Columbia County home — make-ready labor, potential rent loss during the gap, and leasing costs. Owners who offered quality tenants a modest renewal increase below what the open market would bear kept their tenant, avoided turnover costs, and came out ahead financially. The [lease renewal pricing guide](/blog/lease-renewal-pricing-csra-rental-property-owners/) covers the math in detail.

**What worked: proactive maintenance.** Owners who addressed deferred maintenance between tenancies and handled minor issues quickly during the lease year had better inspection scores, fewer tenant complaints, and less turnover. The correlation between fast maintenance response and tenant retention is real, and it's why Noah McBride has built 24-hour maintenance coordination into the [McBride Property Management service model](/services/) — it's not a feature, it's a retention strategy.

**What didn't work: pricing to the mortgage payment.** A surprising number of owners listed a property at a rent based on what they needed to cover their monthly mortgage, rather than what the market would bear. When those homes sat, owners typically accepted lower rents than comparables anyway — after burning weeks of potential rental income. The correct starting point is active comparable listings in your submarket, not your bank statement.

**What didn't work: inconsistent tenant screening.** Owners who lacked a documented, objective screening process struggled to defend adverse action decisions and attracted higher-risk applicants. The [tenant screening criteria guide](/blog/tenant-screening-criteria-income-credit-csra-landlords/) is a practical resource for establishing a defensible process.

## Year-End Owner Checklist: What to Do Before December 31

Before the calendar turns, run through this list:

- [ ] **Confirm lease renewal decisions.** For any lease expiring January–March 2027, your 60-day notice window opens now. Decide: offer a renewal, non-renew, or convert to month-to-month.
- [ ] **Pull your year-end owner statement.** Your net operating income, maintenance spend, and reserve balance for 2026 are the foundation for your Schedule E. Access your statement through the [AppFolio owner portal](/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/).
- [ ] **Verify 1099-NEC obligations.** If you paid any contractor $600 or more this year (not through a property management company), you need to issue a 1099-NEC by January 31, 2027.
- [ ] **Assess your maintenance reserve.** Does the balance reflect what a realistic capital event in 2027 would cost? HVAC replacement in the CSRA runs $4,500–$7,500 for a residential unit; a full roof replacement on a 2,000 sq ft home is $8,000–$15,000.
- [ ] **Review your landlord insurance coverage.** Confirm your dwelling coverage reflects current replacement cost, not purchase price from several years ago.
- [ ] **Check your property tax assessment notice.** Columbia County and Richmond County assessors both processed reassessments this year. If your assessed value increased sharply, review the protest timeline with a local assessor.
- [ ] **Schedule any make-ready work for January vacancies.** Contractors book fast in January; get on a schedule in December.

## What to Watch in 2027

Three things will shape the CSRA rental market over the next twelve months.

**HUD FMR adjustments for FY2027.** HUD has issued its FY2027 Fair Market Rents with an updated methodology for utility cost inflation. For Augusta-Richmond County, official tables are posted at [HUD User](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr.html). The FMR directly affects the payment standard for Housing Choice Voucher holders — if it rises, it expands the pool of voucher holders who qualify for your property at your asking rent.

**BAH rates for 2027.** The Department of Defense typically announces the following year's Basic Allowance for Housing rates in December, effective January 1. If BAH for the Fort Gordon area increases, it creates upward pressure on rents military families are willing to pay in Columbia County — particularly in the $1,800–$2,400 range. Watch for the December announcement.

**The Wellstar MCG expansion's lag effect.** The Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center opened in 2026. The rental demand effect from new healthcare workers takes 6–18 months to fully materialize as hiring ramps, relocation decisions finalize, and leases turn over. The 2027 leasing season will be the first full cycle with the hospital at operating capacity. Columbia County landlords in Evans and Martinez should expect stronger-than-average competition for well-presented three-bedroom homes during the spring PCS and leasing surge.
