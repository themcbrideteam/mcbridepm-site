**Should a CSRA accidental landlord self-manage or hire a property manager?**
Most first-time owners should hire a property manager unless they live within 20 minutes of the property, have prior landlord experience, and can respond to maintenance within 24 hours. Georgia's Safe at Home Act and the complexity of CSRA tenant-screening law make self-management a higher-risk baseline than it was five years ago.

You didn't plan on becoming a landlord. A divorce, a job relocation, an inheritance, a home that wouldn't sell at the price you needed — something shifted, and now you have a residential property in Columbia County or Richmond County that needs a paying tenant. The question most people ask next is: "Can I manage this myself?"

It's a reasonable question. The management fee looks like a cost. Self-management looks like savings. But that framing misses most of what actually matters. The right way to think about it is as a risk-adjusted time-cost decision — and the CSRA has specific features that change the math compared to what you'd find in a generic national article.

[Zillow's March 2026 analysis](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-03-11-Accidental-landlords-rise-to-three-year-high-as-market-shifts) found that accidental landlords — owners who listed their home for sale, couldn't close a deal, and pivoted to renting — hit a three-year high this spring, with 2.3% of all homes listed for rent on Zillow having been recently listed for sale. Single-family homes were higher at 3.4%. The CSRA's combination of mortgage-rate lock-in and the Fort Gordon PCS pipeline means a meaningful number of those owners are right here in Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez. Most of them face the same decision you do, and most of them make it without enough information.

This post gives you the information.

## What "self-managing" actually means in the CSRA

Self-management is not a passive role. When you manage your own CSRA rental, you are personally responsible for every piece of the following:

- **Marketing and showings.** Writing the listing, posting it on Zillow, Facebook Marketplace, and AHRN, responding to inquiries within hours (not days — vacant days cost money), scheduling and conducting showings, and re-listing if prospects don't follow through.
- **Tenant screening.** Collecting applications, running credit and background checks (you must comply with the Fair Credit Reporting Act and use an approved consumer reporting agency), verifying income against CSRA norms, and issuing a written approval or denial decision that complies with [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-31](/blog/tenant-screening-process-augusta-ga-landlords/).
- **Lease execution.** Drafting a lease that complies with Georgia's Safe at Home Act (HB 404), the SCRA if you have military tenants near Fort Gordon, and your HOA's rules if the property sits in a planned community in Evans or Grovetown.
- **Maintenance.** Receiving requests, dispatching licensed vendors, overseeing the work, and verifying completion — all within timelines that O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13 and the Safe at Home Act imply. For habitability emergencies (heat, water, structural), that timeline is essentially 24–72 hours.
- **Rent collection and accounting.** Collecting payments, tracking ledgers, handling late fees correctly under Georgia law, issuing 3-day demand notices when rent is overdue, and issuing year-end 1099s.
- **Move-in and move-out inspections.** Documenting condition at both ends of a tenancy, getting tenant signatures, and processing the security deposit within Georgia's 30-day return window under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-34.

Each item above is a skill, a time commitment, and a legal exposure. Getting any one of them wrong can cost more than a full year of management fees.

## The five-factor test for CSRA accidental landlords

Before you decide anything, run through these five factors honestly. Each one matters.

### Factor 1: Distance from the property

If you live within 20 minutes of the rental, self-management is at least physically possible. If you've relocated out of Georgia — or moved more than 45 minutes away — the operational friction is significant. Emergency maintenance requires dispatching someone *now*. Showings require your presence or a licensed agent. Annual inspections require a visit. The [CSRA Landlord Field Guide](/pdfs/McBride-PM_CSRA-Landlord-Field-Guide.pdf) covers what active management actually requires week to week.

**Score yourself:** Less than 20 minutes away = 2 points. 20–45 minutes = 1 point. More than 45 minutes or out of state = 0 points.

### Factor 2: Prior landlord or property management experience

Have you managed a rental before? Do you know how to serve a Georgia 3-day demand notice, run a compliant tenant screening, or handle a Safe at Home Act habitability complaint? If not, your learning curve has real costs: vacancy extension while you figure out marketing, a missed lease clause that creates a dispute, an improperly documented move-in inspection that forfeits your deposit rights.

**Score yourself:** Prior landlord or PM experience with at least one Georgia rental = 2 points. General real-estate experience but no prior landlord role = 1 point. No prior experience = 0 points.

### Factor 3: Availability for a 24-hour maintenance response

Under Georgia law and the Safe at Home Act, landlords must maintain habitability. That means a broken furnace in December, a failed water heater, or significant pest infestation cannot wait until you're free next week. It needs a licensed HVAC technician or plumber dispatched within hours — and you need a trusted vendor roster in place before the tenant ever calls. Without that network, you're paying emergency-rate markups to whoever answers the phone.

**Score yourself:** You have local vendor relationships, a flexible schedule, and reliable availability = 2 points. Some capacity but no vendor network = 1 point. You travel regularly, have a demanding job, or are in a different time zone = 0 points.

### Factor 4: Risk tolerance for legal exposure

Georgia landlord-tenant law is not complex, but it has teeth. The Safe at Home Act (HB 404) raised habitability standards. O.C.G.A. § 44-7-35 allows courts to award tenants up to 3× the security deposit if the landlord withholds it in bad faith. The Fair Housing Act applies to every rental decision you make — from how you word an ad to which applicants you approve. If you'd rather pay for certainty than accept residual risk, that's a valid position, and it argues for professional management.

**Score yourself:** You have a legal background or high risk tolerance and would handle a magistrate court appearance without stress = 2 points. Some concern about legal risk = 1 point. The idea of a tenant dispute or court appearance is a dealbreaker = 0 points.

### Factor 5: Time budget

One fully-occupied rental in decent condition takes roughly 5–10 hours per month in a steady state. Turnover — the period between tenants — spikes that significantly: marketing, showings, make-ready coordination, and move-in onboarding can run 20–40 hours over 4–6 weeks. If your time is worth $50/hour to you professionally, a typical year of active self-management costs $4,000–$6,000 in opportunity cost before you count a single mistake.

**Score yourself:** You genuinely have 10–15 flexible hours per month available and find this kind of work tolerable = 2 points. You're stretched but could manage in a pinch = 1 point. You're at capacity with work and family obligations = 0 points.

### Reading the scorecard

| Total score | Decision |
|-------------|----------|
| 8–10 points | Self-management is a viable option — proceed with open eyes and read on |
| 5–7 points | Borderline — a hybrid approach (self-manage first year, then reassess) may be worth trying |
| 0–4 points | Hire a property manager. The risk-adjusted cost of self-managing is too high for your situation. |

## Where CSRA self-management gets legally complicated

Even landlords who score well on the five-factor test face some Georgia-specific legal realities worth understanding before they commit.

### The Safe at Home Act and habitability

Georgia's [Safe at Home Act (HB 404)](/blog/georgia-safe-at-home-act-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/) created an implied warranty of habitability in residential rental agreements. This is not an abstract concept — it creates specific timelines for the landlord to respond to habitability issues and specifies that failure to maintain livable conditions can allow a tenant to withhold rent or pursue damages. Self-managing landlords often don't realize this statutory standard exists until a dispute arrives.

### Georgia HB 399 and out-of-state owners

If you've relocated outside Georgia after listing your home as a rental, [Georgia HB 399](/blog/georgia-hb-399-csra-out-of-state-landlord-compliance/) (Act 315, signed May 2025) is relevant background. The enacted bill requires out-of-state owners of 25 or more Georgia single-family or duplex rentals to contract with a [Georgia-licensed real estate broker](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/65745). For the typical accidental landlord with one or two properties, there is no statutory bar to remote self-management under this law. However, the operational and logistical difficulty of remote self-management remains significant regardless of legal requirements: you still need a local vendor network, someone to conduct showings and inspections, and a legal presence if a magistrate court matter arises. The [PCS Landlord Quick-Start Guide](/pdfs/McBride-PM_PCS-Landlord-Quick-Start-Guide.pdf) covers what remote ownership looks like in practice for Fort Gordon families who have been through it.

### The move-in inspection is not optional

Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-33/), the landlord must provide the tenant with a written record of the property's condition at move-in and must get the tenant's signature on that document. Without a signed, itemized move-in inspection, you cannot legally charge the tenant for pre-existing damage at move-out. That single documentation failure is the most common and costly mistake McBride PM sees in accidental landlords who try self-managing and hand off the property mid-tenancy. The [move-in and move-out inspection guide](/blog/move-in-move-out-inspection-rental-property-georgia-landlords/) explains exactly what that document must include.

### Tenant screening under the FCRA

[Tenant screening criteria](/blog/tenant-screening-criteria-income-credit-csra-landlords/) must be applied consistently and documented. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 1681 et seq.) requires that you send a written adverse-action notice when you deny an applicant based on a consumer report. Missing this step exposes you to federal statutory damages per applicant, regardless of how fair your underlying decision was. Most first-time landlords don't know this requirement exists.

## The numbers: what property management actually costs — and what self-management actually costs

Here's where most self-management articles mislead you: they compare the management fee directly against zero, as if self-managing had no cost. It does. The table below shows what a typical first-year CSRA accidental landlord encounters with or without a property manager on a $1,500/month rental.

|-----------|--------------|------------------------|
| Annual management fee (10% of rent) | $0 | ~$1,800 |
| Leasing fee at tenant placement | $0 (you do it) | $750–$1,500 (one-time) |
| Extended vacancy (7 extra days vs. PM benchmark) | ~$350 | Near-zero |

| **Realistic first-year total** | **$6,000–$8,000+** | **$3,300–$3,800** |

This table uses illustrative estimates, not guaranteed figures. Your property's specific situation — rent level, tenant quality, maintenance needs — will change the math. But it shows why the management fee is not the number you're actually comparing.

## Red flags that say "hire a manager now"

The following are decision-clarifying signals, not edge cases. If any of these describe your situation, self-management is not the right call right now.

- You live outside Georgia, or more than 45 minutes from the property
- You don't have an existing vendor roster in the CSRA (licensed HVAC, plumber, general contractor)
- You have a demanding job and cannot reliably respond to a maintenance request by the next business day
- Your property sits in a planned community in Evans or Grovetown with an active HOA — [tenant-caused HOA violations become your liability](/blog/hoa-violation-tenant-rental-property-georgia-landlord/)
- Your property is currently occupied by an active-duty Fort Gordon military tenant — SCRA rights, BAH alignment, and PCS lease breaks require specialized knowledge; a misstep is expensive
- You've never read O.C.G.A. Title 44 Chapter 7 and have no plan to do so before your tenant moves in
- The property just underwent a major life event (inherited, divorce settlement, couldn't-sell pivot) and you're already stretched emotionally or logistically

Any one of these is enough. The goal is not to maximize your involvement — it's to protect the asset.

## The cases where self-management can realistically work

We're a property management company, so transparency here matters: some first-time landlords should absolutely try self-management. Here's when it makes sense.

**You're local and experienced.** You live in Evans, Grovetown, or Martinez, have managed a rental before or work in a related field (real estate, construction, law), and have a vendor network. You understand Georgia landlord-tenant law. For you, the management fee is a real cost, and the five-factor scorecard above shows you're a reasonable candidate to run it yourself.

**You have a known, long-term tenant.** If you're converting your home to a rental and your first tenant is someone you know personally, the screening risk is lower and the management complexity is reduced. This isn't a strategy for the long term — it creates its own complications — but it can make a 12-month self-managing run more viable while you evaluate.

**You have the time, the temperament, and the genuine interest.** Some intentional landlords have read up, understand local law, and want to stay hands-on. That's not the typical accidental-landlord profile, but it exists and it's a valid choice.
