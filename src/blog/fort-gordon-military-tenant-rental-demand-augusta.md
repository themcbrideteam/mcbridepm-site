If you own rental property within a 30-minute drive of Fort Gordon, you're positioned near one of the most resilient demand anchors in the entire Southeast. Military installations create something the open rental market doesn't reliably produce: predictable tenant flow, income backed by a federal housing allowance, and a population that moves on a schedule you can plan around. The question isn't whether military tenants are a good rental market — they are. The question is whether your property, your pricing, and your practices are set up to capture that demand.

This guide covers the 2026 numbers and what they mean for your property.

## Why Fort Gordon Creates Resilient Rental Demand

Fort Gordon isn't a garrison post that happens to be near Augusta. It's the Army's primary hub for cyber operations, signal training, and electronic warfare — home to the Army Cyber Center of Excellence, the Signal School, the NSA/CSS Georgia facility, and a rapidly expanding joint-force mission that has drawn Department of Defense civilians and private-sector contractors alongside uniformed personnel.

As of 2026, Fort Gordon's combined workforce stands at [31,155 personnel](https://www.army.mil/article-amp/293987/transforming_the_force_u_s_army_cyber_center_of_excellence_and_augusta_university_expand_partnership_to_recruit_and_retain_top_cyber_talent), generating nearly $2.4 billion in annual economic activity in the CSRA. That's not a transient workforce — it's a professional, credentialed population that needs housing.

The Army Cyber Center and Augusta University recently expanded a formal training partnership that awards academic credit for military coursework, creating stronger career incentives for service members to take and extend Fort Gordon assignments. The result for landlords: more personnel in the pipeline who are motivated to settle into the area rather than move through it quickly.

PCS cycles still produce the annual wave of tenant movement — heavy from May through August — but the off-cycle months bring steady contractor and DoD civilian demand that keeps vacancy pressure low year-round. The [CSRA rental market's fall 2026 vacancy rate](/blog/csra-rental-market-fall-2026-update-investors/) is running sub-5% in Columbia County's strongest submarkets, and Fort Gordon's workforce is a central reason why.

## 2026 BAH Rates for the Fort Gordon Area

The Basic Allowance for Housing is the monthly stipend the military pays to service members who live off-post. BAH rates are set annually by the [Defense Travel Management Office](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/) and vary by pay grade, dependency status, and duty station ZIP code. For Fort Gordon's housing area (GA073), the 2026 rates increased 6% over 2025.

Here are the anchor rates for 2026, which determine what military tenants at each rank tier can afford for monthly rent:

| Pay Grade | With Dependents | Without Dependents |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 (Private) | — | $1,506/mo |
| E-5 (Sergeant) | $1,890/mo | $1,644/mo |
| E-7 (Sergeant First Class) | ~$2,100–$2,200/mo | ~$1,800–$1,900/mo |
| O-3 (Captain) | ~$2,200–$2,400/mo | ~$1,900–$2,100/mo |
| O-7+ (General Officer) | $2,826/mo | — |

*Note: Intermediate grade rates are approximate — check [travel.dod.mil](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/) for exact current figures by pay grade.*

What this table means in practice: an E-5 with dependents receives $1,890/month for housing. If your property rents for $1,850–$1,890, that tenant can pay rent entirely out of their BAH with nothing coming out of base pay. Price it at $2,000 and they're covering $110–$150/month out-of-pocket — which often means they move on to the next listing.

Noah McBride, broker at McBride Property Management, frames the BAH alignment this way: "Military tenants aren't negotiating rent the same way a civilian might. Their housing budget is a specific number from the DoD. When your asking price matches their rate, the decision becomes easy. When it doesn't, they don't call back."

For current rates by exact pay grade and dependency status, bookmark the DTMO's official BAH calculator at [travel.dod.mil](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/). It updates every January 1.

## What Military Tenants Actually Prioritize

Understanding the decision-making process for military families helps you market your property effectively and screen for the right tenant. The criteria differ from the typical civilian renter.

### Commute to the Gate

Most military tenants target a maximum 20–30 minute drive to Fort Gordon's main gates on Chamberlain Avenue. Columbia County — particularly Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez — sits in that window for the majority of the installation's entry points. Augusta's west side (Wheeler Road, Gordon Highway corridor) also draws tenants who prioritize commute time above neighborhood amenities.

A property that's 12 minutes from the gate beats an equivalent property that's 35 minutes away, even if the farther one is newer or in a better school district. For military families, the commute calculation is often non-negotiable.

### Move-In Ready, Immediately

Military families operate on fixed report dates. When orders arrive, the search period is compressed to 60–90 days, and the family is often coordinating from across the country or from overseas. They cannot wait for a landlord to finish painting, replace the HVAC, or resolve a permit issue. A property that's clean, functional, and available on a specific date will win over a nicer property that's "almost ready."

Amber McBride, operations manager at McBride Property Management, emphasizes this in every pre-listing consultation: "A make-ready completed before listing means you're competing for military tenants. A make-ready that runs into their move-in date means you're not in the running at all."

The [pre-rental property prep checklist](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Pre-Rental-Property-Prep-Checklist.pdf) covers the key items, but the short version is: fresh interior paint, clean carpet or refinished floors, working HVAC, no deferred repairs that a tenant would call about on day one.

### Pet-Friendly Policy

A meaningful share of military families have pets — dogs, specifically. Blanket no-pet policies eliminate a large portion of the qualified military tenant pool. Landlords who allow pets with a reasonable deposit and a clear [pet addendum](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Pet-Addendum.pdf) open the door to applicants who have been turned down elsewhere, which often translates to faster leasing and grateful, stable tenants.

### Lease Flexibility for SCRA

Service members know their housing rights. They know that federal law lets them exit a lease early with proper notice and PCS orders. Landlords who understand this upfront — and build it into their expectations — maintain better relationships with military tenants and manage vacancy more effectively than those who resist or penalize the termination. The SCRA section below covers the specific mechanics.

## SCRA Compliance: What Every Fort Gordon Landlord Must Know

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act is federal law. It overrides your lease agreement when it conflicts. There is no lease language that removes a service member's SCRA rights, and attempting to enforce such language exposes you to legal liability. Here is the SCRA lease termination process in plain terms:

**How a Military Tenant Terminates Early Under SCRA:**

1. Tenant receives PCS or qualifying deployment orders (90 days or more)
2. Tenant delivers written notice to landlord — by hand delivery or certified mail with return receipt
3. Tenant includes a copy of the military orders (or a commanding officer's letter if orders are unavailable)
4. Notice must come at least 30 days before the intended termination date
5. Lease terminates on the last day of the month that is at least 30 days after the first rent due date following delivery of notice
6. Landlord collects current month's rent plus one additional month — that's it
7. Landlord **cannot** charge an early-termination fee or any penalty beyond normal damages
8. Security deposit is returned per normal Georgia deposit rules (within 30 days, with itemized deductions) — only legitimate damages may be withheld, not a penalty for leaving early

This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal advice; talk to a Georgia-licensed attorney for your specific situation.

The separate post on [SCRA lease termination](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/) covers edge cases in more detail — overlapping leases, joint tenants, and what happens when orders are revoked after notice has been delivered.

If you want to verify a tenant's active-duty status before or during the lease, the Defense Manpower Data Center publishes a free verification tool at [dmdc.osd.mil](https://www.dmdc.osd.mil/appj/scra/). It's the most reliable way to confirm military status without asking the tenant for additional documentation.

## Where Military Families Choose to Live Near Fort Gordon

The geography of where military tenants land reflects a mix of commute priorities, family situations, and housing preferences. Understanding this helps you position your property accurately.

**Columbia County — Evans and Grovetown.** These communities capture the majority of military families with children. The combination of newer housing stock, proximity to Fort Gordon's western gates, and good school-district zoning (Columbia County School District) makes Evans — particularly the 30809 ZIP code — the highest-demand submarket in the CSRA for military families. [Evans-area properties](/evans/) in the $1,600–$2,000/month range consistently generate strong interest from E-5 through O-3 families. [Grovetown](/grovetown/) is the second-tier market here — slightly older inventory and newer subdivisions both, with quick gate access.

**Martinez.** A solid middle-ground between Augusta and Evans. Families who couldn't find the right fit in Grovetown or Evans often land in Martinez, and the market is strong enough that well-priced properties move fast. The [Martinez and Columbia County rental market](/blog/why-columbia-county-outperforms-augusta-rental-investors/) remains one of the tighter submarkets in the metro.

**Augusta — West Side.** The Gordon Highway and Wheeler Road corridors appeal to single service members, junior enlisted without dependents, and personnel on shorter assignments who prioritize the shortest possible commute over neighborhood character. Properties here price below the Columbia County market and fill quickly from that pool. [Augusta properties](/augusta/) in the $1,100–$1,600 range match up well with junior enlisted BAH rates.

**North Augusta, SC.** A smaller segment of military tenants lives across the Savannah River in South Carolina, particularly around the Highway 25 corridor. The commute is manageable, and SC state income tax structure can be attractive for some tenants. If you own property in [North Augusta](/north-augusta/), it's worth ensuring your listing is visible on military housing platforms.

## Marketing Your Rental to Military Families

Military families don't find rentals the same way civilian renters do. Reaching them requires a few specific channels:

**AHRN — Automated Housing Referral Network.** The [AHRN](https://www.ahrn.com/) is the official military housing referral platform, managed in partnership with the DoD. Service members are directed to AHRN by their installation housing offices when they receive PCS orders. Listing your property here reaches the military tenant pool directly — not through a general search algorithm, but through the official relocation pipeline. This is the single highest-ROI listing channel for landlords near military installations.

**Fort Gordon Housing Services Office.** The installation's housing office maintains a landlord partner list and a referral process. Landlords who register with the Housing Services Office can have their properties recommended directly to incoming service members who call the office for housing assistance. This is a zero-cost channel with high-intent leads.

**Standard platforms.** Zillow, Apartments.com, and similar platforms still drive volume from military tenants who search there alongside civilian renters. Listing on AHRN does not replace these — it supplements them.

**Response time matters.** A military family receiving PCS orders is coordinating a cross-country move on a tight schedule. If you take three days to respond to an inquiry, they've already signed elsewhere. McBride Property Management responds to incoming rental inquiries within the same business day — which is a competitive advantage in a market where many self-managing landlords don't. For landlords managing their own properties, this is the single operational habit that moves the needle most.

## Timing Leases to the PCS Cycle

PCS moves peak between May and August. If your lease expirations fall in that window, you have the largest possible tenant pool when your property becomes available. Leases that expire in October through March typically lead to longer vacancy, because you're competing for a smaller pool of incoming tenants.

This doesn't mean you should force every lease to expire in summer. But it does mean that when you're structuring a renewal negotiation, a 14-month lease that ends in June is often worth more than a 12-month lease that ends in October. For more detail on renewal timing and pricing, the post on [lease renewal strategies](/blog/lease-renewal-strategies-augusta-ga-landlords/) covers the specific tradeoffs.
