You live in New Jersey. You've never been to Augusta. And you're asking the question every out-of-state investor eventually faces: how do I actually finance this?

The loan question matters more than most investors realize. With conventional rates, DSCR rates, and portfolio loan rates all in different ranges in late 2026, the difference between loan types on a $275,000 CSRA rental can mean $300–$500 per month in debt service — a gap that separates positive cash flow from breakeven. Beyond the rate, the choice determines whether you can hold the property in an LLC, how many properties you can own simultaneously, and what documentation your lender will demand.

## The three paths: an overview

Residential real estate investors have three primary financing structures for long-term rental properties:

| Feature | Conventional | DSCR | Portfolio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualification basis | Personal income + DTI | Property rental income | Lender discretion |
| Nov 2026 rate range | ~7.7%–8.2% | ~6.5%–8.75% | ~6.5%–10% |
| Down payment (SFR) | 20–25% | 20–25% | 20–30% |
| LLC vesting | No | Yes (most lenders) | Varies |
| Property count limit | 10 (Fannie Mae) | None | None |
| Income documentation | Full (tax returns, W-2) | None | Flexible |
| Sold to secondary market | Yes (GSE) | Yes (private capital) | No (held on balance sheet) |
| Best for | W-2 earners, 1–9 properties | Self-employed, 10+ properties | Non-standard deals |

Each path has a distinct use case. The wrong choice doesn't just cost you a quarter point — it can get you denied at closing.

## Conventional investor loans — the most familiar option

A conventional investment property loan uses the same Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac framework as a primary mortgage, with an important overlay: you're buying property you don't intend to occupy. That overlay adds cost and restrictions.

**How qualification works:** The lender underwrites your personal income — W-2s, tax returns, 1099s, business income — against your total debt obligations. Fannie Mae allows up to 10 financed properties, though the guidelines tighten at 5+ properties, requiring more reserves and a higher minimum credit score. Rental income from the subject property can offset the mortgage payment in underwriting, but lenders typically use 75% of gross rent to account for vacancy and expenses.

**Rate in November 2026:** [Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey for the week of November 5, 2026](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) put the primary 30-year fixed average at 7.18%. Investment properties carry a premium of roughly 0.5–1.0 percentage point above primary rates, putting conventional investor pricing in the 7.7%–8.2% range for strong-credit borrowers with standard down payments. Your actual quote depends on credit score, LTV, and the number of mortgaged properties you currently hold.

**Key limits to know:**

- **10-property ceiling.** [Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-4.1-01](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/s/article/B3-4-1-01-Minimum-Reserves-Requirements) caps financed properties at 10 per borrower for investment purchases. This includes your primary residence. Many investors hit this wall earlier than expected because they forgot to count a vacation property or second home financed years ago.
- **Personal vesting only.** Conventional conforming loans require the borrower to hold title personally. If you need LLC vesting from day one, conventional won't work without a commercial refinance later.
- **Full income documentation.** For investors with complex tax returns, heavy depreciation deductions, or Schedule C business income, conventional qualification can be difficult even with solid net worth. Write-offs that lower your taxable income also lower the qualifying income your lender counts.

**When conventional is the right call:** If you have clean W-2 income, fewer than 10 financed properties, and you're buying in your personal name, conventional typically delivers the best rate available. It's the starting point for most investors on their first few rentals.

## DSCR loans — when the property qualifies itself

A DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loan is a non-qualified mortgage (non-QM) product that skips personal income underwriting entirely. The lender looks at one number: does the property's rental income cover the full mortgage payment?

**How the ratio works:** DSCR = gross monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues). A ratio of 1.0 means rent exactly equals the payment. Most lenders require a minimum DSCR of 1.0, with better pricing at 1.25 and above. Some programs accept as low as 0.75, and a handful of "no-ratio" programs don't require rental income at all — useful for vacant properties being repositioned.

**Rate in November 2026:** Based on [reported lender pricing this month](https://themortgagereports.com/27698/investment-property-mortgage-rates-how-much-more-will-you-pay), DSCR loan rates for single-family rentals currently range from approximately 6.5% to 8.75% depending on DSCR ratio, credit score, loan-to-value, and program type. The best pricing requires DSCR above 1.25, credit score above 740, and LTV at or below 70%. Rates above 8% reflect borderline DSCR, lower credit, or higher leverage.

**What DSCR loans don't require:**

- Personal tax returns
- W-2 or pay stubs
- Employment verification
- Personal DTI calculation

**What they do require:** A property that generates enough rent, a down payment of 20–25% for single-family homes, and a credit score typically no lower than 660. Most DSCR lenders also allow LLC vesting from day one — which is a meaningful structural advantage for [investors building a multi-property portfolio in a Georgia LLC](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/llc-rental-property-augusta-georgia/).

The [CSRA's lower acquisition costs compared to gateway markets](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/out-of-state-rental-investor-guide-augusta-ga/) mean many Augusta-area properties can clear the 1.0 DSCR threshold that more expensive metros can't reach. The numbers on that calculation are in the section below.


## Portfolio loans — the local bank alternative

A portfolio loan is a mortgage that a lender originates and keeps on its own balance sheet rather than selling to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. Because the lender isn't bound by agency guidelines, it underwrites to its own judgment — and that flexibility is exactly when portfolio loans earn their place.

**Who holds them:** Community banks, credit unions, and regional banks in the Augusta area offer portfolio loan products. Because these institutions know the local market, they sometimes carry less concern about property types or geographic risks that make agency underwriters uncomfortable.

**When portfolio loans are the right answer:**

- Property doesn't meet conventional or DSCR requirements (older structure, non-warrantable condo, mixed-use, rural acreage outside agency guidelines)
- Investor has a credit event — short sale, foreclosure — that falls outside agency seasoning requirements
- Investor needs a blanket note structure: a single mortgage across two or more investment properties, simplifying management but creating cross-collateralization
- Investor has 10+ financed properties and is excluded from agency products
- Investor values a relationship with a local lender who understands Columbia County, Richmond County, and Aiken County dynamics

**Rate and terms:** Portfolio rates vary by institution, but current market positioning puts them in the 6.5%–10.0% range, with most community bank offerings in the 7.0%–8.5% range for strong deals. LTV typically maxes at 70%–75%, and minimum DSCR requirements (where applied) often run 1.20x or above. Unlike DSCR loans, there's no standardized pricing — the rate reflects the institution's own portfolio concentration and the relationship you've built.

**The tradeoff:** Portfolio loans often carry shorter terms (5–10 year amortization or balloon payments), and refinancing may require additional closing costs. They're less portable than a DSCR loan sold to private capital. But for deals that simply don't fit any other box, they're often the only option that works.

## The DSCR calculation every CSRA investor should run first

Before settling on a loan type, run the DSCR calculation against the specific property you're evaluating. Here's what that looks like with real November 2026 data for Evans, GA.

As of November 18, 2026, the three-bedroom homes we have listed in Evans are asking $1,895 to $2,400 a month. That's the range a lender's appraiser would draw from for comparable rentals in that area.

Here's the DSCR calculation for an illustrative $275,000 purchase with 25% down at a 7.5% DSCR rate:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | $275,000 |
| Down payment (25%) | $68,750 |
| Loan amount | $206,250 |
| Rate (30-year fixed DSCR) | 7.5% |
| Monthly P&I | ~$1,441 |
| Monthly taxes (est.) | ~$170 |
| Monthly insurance (est.) | ~$130 |
| Monthly PITIA | ~$1,741 |
| **Rent at $1,895/month** | **DSCR: 1.09** |
| **Rent at $2,400/month** | **DSCR: 1.38** |

A DSCR of 1.09 qualifies at most lenders but won't unlock best pricing. At 1.25 and above, most lenders drop the rate by 0.25–0.50 point. The $2,400 rental — a larger or nicer 3-bedroom — clears 1.25 easily and prices closer to the bottom of the DSCR rate range.

Compare that to a conventional investor loan on the same property at 8.0%:
- Monthly P&I: ~$1,513 on the same $206,250 loan
- PITIA: ~$1,813
- The property still cash-flows, but your DTI qualification depends entirely on your personal income documents

The key observation: in CSRA, the choice between DSCR and conventional often comes down to your personal income profile, not the property's math. The property can usually support both. Your tax returns or existing mortgage count typically force the decision.

## Matching the loan to the investor profile

Here's how to map your situation to the right starting point:

**Start with conventional if:**
- You have clean W-2 income and your DTI is comfortable
- You own fewer than 10 financed properties
- You're buying in your personal name
- Securing the lowest available rate is your first priority

**Start with DSCR if:**
- Your taxable income is significantly lower than gross income due to business deductions or depreciation
- You need LLC vesting from day one
- You already have 5+ mortgaged properties and are approaching the conventional ceiling
- You want to scale to 15+ properties without hitting an underwriting wall
- You're self-employed or run a business where income documentation is complicated

**Consider portfolio if:**
- The property is unusual — older vintage, non-standard construction, mixed-use, rural acreage
- You have a credit event that agency guidelines penalize but a relationship lender would overlook
- You want a blanket note across multiple CSRA properties
- You value a local lender relationship more than the lowest possible rate

## Common financing mistakes CSRA investors make

**Using P&I instead of PITIA in the DSCR calculation.** DSCR uses the full payment — principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues. Using just P&I overstates the DSCR by 10–20%, which means properties that appear to qualify actually don't. Columbia County taxes and landlord insurance together add $250–$350/month on a typical CSRA single-family rental.

**Forgetting the 10-mortgage cap until it's too late.** An investor who bought a primary home, a second property, and six rentals over ten years may not realize they're at 9 financed properties until a loan officer runs their credit. At that point, one more conventional loan exhausts the ceiling — and scrambling to close a DSCR product at the last minute costs time and potentially the deal.

**Underestimating closing costs for DSCR loans.** Non-QM products often carry origination fees and points that conventional loans don't. A DSCR loan with 1–2 origination points on a $200,000 loan adds $2,000–$4,000 at closing versus a conventional loan with no origination. For a first-time out-of-state buyer, this comes as a surprise.

**Not rate-shopping non-agency products.** Conventional pricing is fairly standardized — Fannie/Freddie guidelines set a floor. DSCR and portfolio rates vary by 0.50–1.00 percentage point across lenders on the same deal. Getting two to three DSCR quotes on the same property is worth the two days it takes. As [BiggerPockets investors have noted](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/517/topics/1284868-dscr-rate-update-april-13-2026), rate differences of 0.50% or more between lenders on the same DSCR file are common.

**Not pairing financing research with a property management conversation.** Lenders provide loan structure; they don't tell you what a 3-bedroom in Evans actually rents for today. A property manager's rent estimate is what feeds the DSCR calculation — and it's what determines whether the property makes sense in the first place.
