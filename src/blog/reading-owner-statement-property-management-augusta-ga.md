**What does each line on your AppFolio property management owner statement actually mean?**
Your owner statement is a monthly cash-flow report showing all income collected, all expenses charged, and the net amount transferred to your bank account. In Augusta-area properties managed through AppFolio, the statement has four sections: a header with property and period data, an income section, an expense section, and an owner distribution summary. Understanding each line helps you catch errors, plan for expenses, and file an accurate Schedule E.

</div>

You got the notification: your owner statement is ready in the portal. You log in on the 15th, pull up the PDF, and scan the numbers. Rent collected: $1,450. Management fee: $130.50. Maintenance: $275. Net distribution: $1,044.50. Somewhere in that math there's a $275 maintenance charge you don't have context for.

Is that normal? Did something break at the property? Is the charge accurate?

For owners with one or two rentals in Evans, Grovetown, or Augusta, the owner statement is the primary financial document they'll interact with every month. Most owners read it the same way they read a cell phone bill — they check the total, look for anything that seems obviously wrong, and move on. But the statement contains far more information than that. Each line is a data point about your property's health, your cash flow, and your annual tax position.

This guide walks through every section of a standard AppFolio owner statement so you know exactly what you're looking at — and what to do when something doesn't look right.

## How Your Statement Is Organized

Your owner statement covers a single calendar month and a single property (if you own multiple properties with McBride PM, each gets its own statement, plus a portfolio summary).

The document has four sections:

| Section | What It Contains |
|---|---|
| **Header** | Property address, statement period, PM contact, statement date |
| **Income** | All money received into the property's account during the period |
| **Expenses** | All charges applied against the property's account during the period |
| **Distribution Summary** | Beginning balance, ending balance, net owner distribution |

The income section and the expense section don't necessarily balance each other. The difference — net income minus net expenses — becomes your owner distribution after any reserve adjustment.

One important distinction: the statement reflects **cash received and cash paid**, not amounts owed. If your tenant paid rent on the 5th, it's in this month's income. If they paid two weeks late after the statement period closed, it will appear on next month's statement. This is cash accounting, which is also how you'll report rental income to the IRS on [Schedule E](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527).

## The Income Section: What Actually Came In

The income section lists every deposit or credit applied to your property account during the month.

**Beginning cash balance.** This is the carry-forward from last month. If your property had $350 in the account at the end of September, your October statement starts with $350. This is typically your maintenance reserve balance.

**Rent collected.** The gross rent payment from your tenant. Note the word "collected." If rent is $1,450 and the tenant paid $1,450, this line shows $1,450. If the tenant paid $1,200 and still owes $250, this line shows $1,200 — not $1,450. The shortfall is a collection matter, tracked separately. This distinction matters at tax time: you report what you actually received, not what was owed.

**Late fees.** If the tenant paid after the grace period and a late fee was assessed and collected, it appears here. Late fees are taxable income to you and deductible by your PM (as pass-through, they net to zero on the management side, but they're still income in your hands).

**Other income.** This catch-all covers:
- **Pet rent** — monthly pet fees collected per your lease addendum
- **Utility reimbursements** — if utilities are billed back to tenants under a lease provision
- **Application fees** — rare, but occasionally passed through if your management agreement specifies it
- **NSF fee recovery** — if a returned-check fee was collected from the tenant

A returned ACH payment (NSF) may also appear in this section as a negative entry on the day it was returned, followed by a re-collection credit on the day the replacement payment cleared. If you see paired negative/positive entries near the same amount, that's usually an NSF event.

## The Expense Section: Where Your Money Goes

This is the section that generates the most questions. Every line here is a deduction against your account — money paid out on your behalf during the month.

**Management fee.** Your monthly PM fee. At McBride PM, this is a percentage of collected rent — so it applies only to rent actually received. A month where no rent was collected (vacancy, or a nonpayment situation) produces no management fee. The management fee covers ongoing leasing oversight, tenant communication, maintenance coordination, financial reporting, and legal compliance — the full [service menu](/services/) your property requires to operate correctly.

For a detailed breakdown of what different CSRA property managers charge and what those fees include, the [property management fees guide for Augusta in 2026](/blog/property-management-fees-augusta-ga-2026/) is worth reading before you compare contracts.

**Leasing/placement fee.** A one-time fee charged when a new tenant is placed. It covers marketing, showings, application processing, screening, and lease execution. It appears on the month the new lease begins — not monthly. If you've had the same tenant for two years, you won't see this line until turnover.

**Lease renewal fee.** A smaller fee charged when an existing tenant signs a lease renewal. This covers the repricing analysis, renewal negotiation, and new lease execution. It appears once, in the month the renewal is signed.

**Maintenance and repair charges.** These are the lines that generate the most questions. Each maintenance line should show:
- A description (e.g., "Plumbing — kitchen faucet replacement")
- A date
- An amount

In AppFolio, every maintenance charge is linked to a work order. Click the line item in your owner portal and the work order — including vendor name, date of service, and invoice — is attached. If you see a $275 charge, you can drill down to the specific invoice within 30 seconds.

Common maintenance lines on CSRA rental statements include HVAC filter changes and tune-ups (especially heading into summer or winter), plumbing calls, pest control, and minor appliance repairs.

**Inspection fees.** If your management agreement includes periodic property inspections (McBride PM conducts annual inspections plus routine drive-bys), an inspection fee may appear. This covers the time to prepare the inspection report and owner-facing documentation.

**Professional services.** Legal fees, court filing costs, or other professional services paid on your behalf appear here. If a dispossessory was filed in Columbia County or Richmond County Magistrate Court, the filing fee ($75–$100) and any attorney coordination costs appear on this line. These are relatively rare, but they're real costs to anticipate in any landlord budget.

**Reserve replenishment.** If your maintenance reserve was drawn below its agreed minimum during the month, the statement will include a line to top it back up. More on reserves below.

## The Distribution Summary: What Hits Your Account

At the bottom of the statement is the net calculation:

```
Beginning balance:          $350.00
+ Total income:           $1,500.00
- Total expenses:          ($455.50)
= Ending balance:         $1,394.50
- Reserve held:            ($350.00)
= Net owner distribution: $1,044.50
```

The **ending balance** is all funds collected and not yet disbursed. The **reserve held** is the float kept in your account for future repairs. The **net owner distribution** is what gets transferred to your bank account — this is the number that should match your bank statement deposit.

Owner distributions at McBride PM are processed in the first half of the following month, after rent collection is reconciled. If your tenant pays by the 3rd (as most do via ACH through the [AppFolio resident portal](https://www.appfolio.com/)), your distribution is typically processed between the 8th and the 15th.

## The Maintenance Reserve: What It Is and Why It Exists

The maintenance reserve is a small operating balance — typically $200–$500 — held in your property account to cover routine repairs without requiring an owner wire each time. [NARPM](https://www.narpm.org/) recommends a reserve as industry-standard practice for responsive management.

Here's why it matters: if your HVAC filter costs $45 or a toilet flapper fails at $65, waiting for owner approval and a wire transfer before fixing it adds unnecessary delay, frustrates tenants, and — under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13 — potentially creates a habitability issue. The reserve exists so your PM can dispatch quickly, keep your tenant happy, and handle small items without interrupting your day.

The reserve **is your money**. It appears as a line item on your statement and carries forward month to month. When it's depleted by a repair, the next statement will show a replenishment deduction. If you close your account with McBride PM, the reserve balance is returned to you at settlement.

You can review the current reserve balance at any time in your [AppFolio owner portal](/blog/appfolio-owner-portal-property-management-augusta-ga/), along with the work orders that drew on it.

## Your Statement and Year-End Taxes

Your October statement, arriving in November, is a useful mid-year checkpoint. Your December statement, available by mid-January, carries your full-year totals. Here's how to use them at tax time:

**Income.** Sum the "Rent collected" lines across all 12 monthly statements. This should equal — or closely approximate — the gross rents reported on your 1099. Small timing differences can occur if a December rent payment clears in early January; your CPA can reconcile this.

**Expenses.** Sum each expense category across 12 statements: management fees (fully deductible), maintenance and repairs (fully deductible as repairs per [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)), leasing fees (deductible as professional fees), and inspection fees (deductible). Capital improvements — anything that materially extends the property's useful life — are depreciated over 27.5 years, not expensed in the year paid.

**The 1099.** McBride PM issues a 1099-MISC reporting gross rents paid to you by January 31. The 1099 reflects gross rents collected on your behalf, not your net distribution. Your net distribution is lower because expenses were deducted before the wire. Your Schedule E will reconcile the gross income (from the 1099) against allowable deductions (from your statements plus depreciation) to arrive at your taxable rental income. For a full deduction walkthrough, the [rental property tax deductions guide for Georgia landlords](/blog/rental-property-tax-deductions-georgia-landlords/) covers every category.

This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice; talk to a Georgia CPA familiar with rental real estate for your specific situation.

## Common Questions — and How to Flag a Discrepancy

**"I see a $275 charge labeled 'Maintenance — Plumbing.' What happened?"**
Log into the owner portal, click the line item, and the attached work order and invoice will show you the vendor, service date, and what was repaired. If you'd like more detail, email robert@mcbride-pm.com and we can pull the full work order within one business day.

**"My distribution is $200 lower than last month. Nothing seemed to change."**
The most common causes: a maintenance call was completed, a renewal fee was charged, or the reserve was replenished after a prior draw. Open the expense section — each deduction has a description.

**"I think I was overcharged for management fees this month."**
The management fee is calculated on collected rent. If you see a different number than expected, divide the management fee by your collected rent. If the percentage doesn't match your contract, email robert@mcbride-pm.com with the statement date and the calculated percentage.

**"The statement shows rent collected, but I know the tenant paid late."**
Late rent that was collected during the statement period still appears in that month's income. If it was collected after the statement period closed, it appears next month. The statement date tells you the cutoff.

The Georgia DCA's [Landlord-Tenant Handbook](https://consumer.georgia.gov/consumer-topics/landlord-tenant-issues) is a useful reference for understanding what landlords and tenants are entitled to in terms of documentation and accounting transparency.

For owners newer to professional management, the [owner FAQs page](/owner-faqs/) covers the most common first-year questions, and the [resources page](/resources/) has downloadable guides including the CSRA Landlord Field Guide — useful context for reading your statements in the broader framework of your investment.

## The Practical Habits That Make Statement Review Faster

1. **Set a calendar reminder for the 15th.** That's when statements post. Review it the same week it arrives, while the month is fresh.
2. **Look at maintenance first.** It's the most variable line and the most likely to raise questions.
3. **Compare to the prior month.** Is your management fee consistent? Is maintenance trending up at a specific property?
4. **Save every statement.** AppFolio stores them indefinitely, but keeping a local folder of PDFs is good backup.
5. **Flag questions within 30 days.** Legitimate discrepancies are much easier to resolve when the invoice is fresh and the vendor is accessible.
