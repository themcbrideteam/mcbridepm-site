## The Description That Gets Results vs. the Description That Gets Questions

Here is the difference between a maintenance request that gets dispatched the same day and one that gets kicked back for more information:

**Weak description (causes delay):**
> "My AC doesn't work."

**Strong description (gets dispatched):**
> "The HVAC unit runs — I can hear the blower — but the air coming from the vents is not cold. The thermostat is set to 72°F cooling. It has been this way for three days. The unit is the condenser on the left side of the house near the gate. I'm available Tuesday or Wednesday after 4 p.m. or any morning before 10 a.m."

The strong description tells the vendor: the type of problem (blower works but no cooling, likely a refrigerant or capacitor issue), the location (left-side condenser), the duration (three days), and the access window. They arrive with the right diagnostic approach, likely the right replacement parts, and a specific appointment time.

**What to always include:**
- Exact location in the unit (room, exterior side, fixture)
- What you observe vs. what you expect (running but not cooling vs. not running at all)
- How long it has been occurring
- Any changes that preceded the issue (new noise before the problem, recent event)
- At least one photo

**What to include for intermittent problems:**
- A short video (15–30 seconds) demonstrating the issue
- Time of day when the problem typically occurs
- Frequency ("happens every morning when I run the hot water, for about five minutes")

## What Georgia Law Requires: Your Repair Rights as a Tenant

Your right to a maintained rental home isn't just a lease clause — it's backed by state law. [Georgia's Safe at Home Act](https://gaappleseed.org/wp-content/uploads/SafeAtHome_BenchCard.pdf) (House Bill 404, effective 2024) established a formal warranty of habitability for residential tenants across the state. Under [O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-13/), landlords are required to keep rental premises in repair and maintain them as fit for human habitation. That obligation is non-waivable — a lease clause attempting to transfer all maintenance responsibility to a tenant is unenforceable.

What the Safe at Home Act specifically requires landlords to maintain:
- Functioning electrical systems
- Functioning plumbing (hot and cold running water)
- Heating AND cooling systems
- Structural soundness (roof, floors, walls, windows)
- Working smoke detectors
- Safe common areas
- Pest-free conditions (within reason — see below)

On repair timelines, Georgia courts apply a **"reasonable time" standard** after written notice. Practitioners widely interpret this as:
- **24–48 hours** for emergencies (no heat, no water, safety hazard)
- **14–30 days** for non-emergency repairs

These are not hard statutory deadlines — they are what courts have consistently found to be reasonable under Georgia law. The key word is *written notice*: your AppFolio submission constitutes written notice. This is one reason it matters that you submit maintenance requests through the portal rather than mentioning them verbally at rent drop-off.

The Safe at Home Act also added **retaliation protections**: if you report a habitability issue and your landlord retaliates with an eviction notice, lease non-renewal, or rent increase within three months of your complaint, Georgia law presumes the action was retaliatory. You may use this as a defense in court.

**A note on pest control:** Under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13 as interpreted through the Safe at Home Act, pest infestations that compromise habitability — rodents, cockroach infestations, bed bugs — are a landlord responsibility, provided the infestation was not caused or worsened by the tenant's conduct. Submit pest issues through the portal and document them promptly. For more on your rights as a CSRA renter, see our [Georgia Tenant Rights 2026 guide](/blog/georgia-tenant-rights-2026-csra-renter-guide/).

## What You Cannot Do: Repair-and-Deduct Is Not a Georgia Option

This is important enough to say clearly: **Georgia does not have a repair-and-deduct statute.**

In some states, tenants can hire their own contractor if a landlord fails to make repairs within a statutory deadline, then deduct the cost from rent. Georgia is not one of those states. If you hire a plumber because your landlord hasn't fixed a leak quickly enough and then subtract his bill from next month's rent, you have just put yourself in default for non-payment of rent. The landlord may then pursue eviction through the dispossessory process in Columbia County or Richmond County Magistrate Court.

Similarly, withholding rent entirely as leverage for repairs is not a legal strategy under current Georgia law. The Safe at Home Act does not include a rent escrow provision.

Your actual escalation path if repairs aren't made within a reasonable time:
1. Follow up in writing through AppFolio — create a documented record of your notice and the lag
2. Call (706) 339-2874 and escalate verbally, then document that call in writing
3. For habitability failures, contact the Columbia County Building Standards Department or Richmond County Code Enforcement to request an inspection
4. Consult a Georgia tenant-rights attorney or your local legal aid office if you believe a habitability violation exists and the landlord has failed to act after reasonable written notice

The [Georgia Security Deposit Return Guide](/blog/georgia-security-deposit-return-csra-tenant-guide/) also explains how documented maintenance requests protect you at move-out — a repair request on record demonstrates you flagged a condition, which matters when deposit deductions are disputed.

## Fall in the CSRA: The Requests We See Most This Time of Year

If you are a McBride PM tenant in Evans, Grovetown, Martinez, or Hephzibah, here is what typically surfaces when the calendar turns to late September and October:

**HVAC failing to heat on first use.** Many HVAC units run all summer in cooling mode without issue, then stumble when switched to heat for the first time. The most common culprits are a failed heating element (heat pump) or a cracked heat exchanger (gas furnace). Submit a maintenance request the moment you notice the heat not producing warmth — don't wait a week to see if it resolves on its own. As temperatures drop toward the 40s overnight, a non-functional heating system quickly becomes an emergency.

**Doors and windows swollen from summer humidity.** Columbia County's hot, humid summers cause wood to swell. Doors that dragged all summer may suddenly bind tightly as temperatures cool. This is a legitimate maintenance issue — submit it. Don't force a door by repeatedly slamming it; that causes damage.

**Water heater age showing.** Water heaters in the CSRA typically last 8–12 years. If yours is making popping or rumbling sounds when heating, taking longer than usual to recover, or producing only lukewarm water, document it in a maintenance request now. A properly submitted request creates a record if the water heater fails completely later.

**Pest activity picking up.** Rodents and cockroaches seek warmth as outdoor temperatures drop. A sighting or two in October is not unusual in any home — submit a maintenance request immediately so pest control can be dispatched before a sighting becomes an infestation.

If you're not sure whether something qualifies as a maintenance issue worth submitting, the answer is almost always: yes, submit it. A question costs nothing. An undocumented condition can cost you money if it worsens and the origin becomes disputed at move-out.

## After You Submit: Understanding the Vendor Visit

Once your request is scheduled, a vendor will contact you (or enter with advance notice if you've given permission) to perform the repair. A few things to know:

**Vendors are vetted and licensed.** McBride PM uses a network of licensed, insured contractors — licensed plumbers for plumbing, licensed HVAC technicians for heating and cooling, licensed electricians for electrical. For work over Georgia's $2,500 contractor licensing threshold, you can verify licensing through the [Georgia Secretary of State GOALS portal](https://secure.sos.state.ga.us/myverification/).

**The vendor may need a return trip.** On the first visit, the technician diagnoses and often makes the repair. But if a part must be ordered, they will document the diagnosis and schedule a second visit. You will see this reflected in AppFolio as the status moves to a waiting stage.

**Rate the visit.** AppFolio may prompt you to leave a brief rating after the visit is marked complete. Please do — it helps McBride PM evaluate vendor performance and prioritize quality contractors for future work.

**Something wasn't fixed right?** Reopen the maintenance request directly in AppFolio or call (706) 339-2874. Don't submit a brand-new request for the same issue — that duplicates dispatch and slows resolution. Flag the existing request as incomplete.

For a deeper look at how we vet and manage the vendors who come to your home, see [How Property Managers Vet Maintenance Vendors in the CSRA](/blog/property-management-maintenance-vendors-csra-augusta-ga/).
