**What does the Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center opening mean for CSRA rental demand?**
Columbia County's first-ever inpatient hospital — a $380 million, 100-bed Wellstar facility in Grovetown — opens in late August 2026. At full staffing, hospitals of this size employ 400–800+ workers. Healthcare employees are among the most stable, highest-qualified rental tenants in any market. For Evans- and Grovetown-area rental owners, this is the most significant new demand signal in years.

</div>

For nearly two decades, Columbia County residents who needed inpatient hospital care drove to Augusta. That changes this August.

Wellstar Health System, in partnership with Augusta University's medical school, is opening the Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center at 5000 Gateway Blvd. in Grovetown — a six-story, 254,000-square-foot facility with 100 inpatient beds and its own emergency department. It is, by any measure, a transformational piece of infrastructure for the county. And for rental property owners in Evans, Grovetown, and Martinez, it introduces a category of tenant demand that will be present for a very long time.

This post breaks down what the hospital actually is, who it will employ, what those employees typically earn, and what that means for rental demand and pricing in the corridor closest to the facility. If you own rentals in Columbia County or are evaluating whether now is the time to buy, read this before making your next move.

## What's Actually Opening at 5000 Gateway Boulevard

The Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center is not a satellite clinic or an urgent care expansion. It is a full-service acute care hospital — and it is the county's first.

The facility is six stories tall and 254,000 square feet. It will open with 100 inpatient beds: 16 in the intensive care unit and 84 on medical-surgical floors. The emergency department is designed for Level II trauma designation. There are operating rooms, catheterization labs, interventional radiology, and endoscopy suites. The hospital is positioned to handle the acute-care volume that Columbia County residents have, until now, sent to Augusta University Medical Center or Doctors Hospital on the Augusta side of the county line.

Adjacent to the main building is a four-story, 92,000-square-foot medical office building. That structure will house outpatient clinics: primary care, women's health, specialty medicine, and diagnostic services. The MOB is not a separate enterprise — it is operationally integrated with the hospital. That matters for employment because MOB-based physicians and their clinical support staff are as committed to this location as the inpatient nursing teams.

The partnership between Wellstar Health System and Augusta University (MCG Health) carries institutional weight. Wellstar is a major multi-hospital system; Augusta University runs one of Georgia's few public medical schools. [Wellstar has confirmed](https://www.wellstar.org/articles/wellstar-columbia-county-medical-center-expected-to-open-in-late-august) the late August opening publicly, and WRDW reported in May 2026 that the facility is on track.

This is a permanent, capital-intensive commitment to Columbia County. It does not relocate. It does not deploy. It does not expire when a federal contract ends.

## The Employment Picture: Who Gets Hired to Staff a 100-Bed Hospital

Hospitals are among the most labor-intensive facilities in any local economy. The industry benchmark runs five to eight full-time equivalents per inpatient bed — meaning a 100-bed hospital typically employs between 500 and 800 FTEs before accounting for the medical office building staff.

The breakdown of those roles matters for rental property owners, because different employee categories carry different income levels and different tenure patterns:

**Clinical — Nursing (the largest single category)**
Registered nurses represent 35–45% of total hospital staff, often more. They work rotating shifts — days, nights, weekends — and most prefer housing close enough to avoid a hard commute. Based on [2026 data from Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/general/registered-nurse-rn-salary/augusta-ga), registered nurses in Augusta, GA earn between $61,000 and $108,000 annually depending on specialty and experience, with the midpoint around $77,000–$83,000. A nurse earning $77,000 clears roughly $5,400–$5,600 per month after Georgia income tax — which means a $1,400–$1,700 Evans or Grovetown single-family rental sits at roughly 25–31% of gross monthly income. Most property managers qualify applicants at one-third of gross monthly income as the ceiling. Healthcare RNs pass that bar with room.

**Clinical — Allied Health (imaging, lab, rehab, surgical services)**
Radiologic technologists, laboratory technicians, respiratory therapists, physical and occupational therapists. Annual income range in the Augusta market: $45,000–$75,000 depending on specialty and certification level. These employees typically carry associate or bachelor's degrees, maintain stable employment history, and stay at single institutions for years. A certified rad tech who relocated specifically for this position has verified income and no institutional mechanism pushing them to move again. That is a favorable tenant profile.

**Surgical, ICU, and Specialty Services**
OR nurses, surgical technologists, cath lab techs, intensive care specialists. These roles tend toward the upper end of the nursing income range — $70,000–$95,000 — and represent the most difficult positions for hospitals to recruit. That difficulty creates retention incentive on the employer's side, which in turn means longer tenure for the tenant.

**Administrative and Support**
Patient access coordinators, medical records staff, dietary, environmental services, security. This group typically earns $28,000–$48,000 annually and represents roughly 25–35% of total headcount. These employees are part of the demand picture, but at that income level, they are more likely to qualify for two-bedroom apartments and smaller homes than for the three-bedroom single-family rentals that dominate Columbia County's investor-owned supply.

**Hospitalist and Specialist Physicians**
Wellstar is actively recruiting hospitalists, ER physicians, and specialist physicians for the Columbia County facility, with [career listings visible on the Wellstar careers portal](https://careers.wellstar.org/us/en/columbia-county-medical-center). Physicians at this level earn $250,000–$450,000+ and typically transition from renter to owner within 12–24 months of establishing themselves in a market. Their short-term rental window is the landing period — but that window is real, and it is valuable for a landlord who prices and markets well.

## Healthcare Workers as Rental Tenants: The Qualification Profile

Property managers pay close attention to tenant source because it predicts tenancy quality. Here is how a healthcare-worker applicant profile compares to the Augusta-area baseline:

**Income verification is clean.** Healthcare employment income is employer-verifiable, W-2 earned, and consistent across pay periods. There is no gig-economy ambiguity, no seasonal fluctuation, no business that could close. For a landlord who has wrestled with self-employment income documentation, healthcare W-2s are straightforward.

**Income-to-rent ratios work.** An RN at $77,000 gross ($6,417/month) qualifies at the standard 3× monthly income threshold for rentals up to roughly $2,139 per month. In practice, a $1,500–$1,700 Evans three-bedroom represents 22–26% of their gross monthly income — well inside qualifying range and below the threshold where financial stress typically triggers non-payment problems.

**Tenure is longer than the military tenant average.** Unlike active-duty tenants near Fort Gordon — who McBride Property Management manages extensively and who can receive PCS orders on 12–18 month cycles — hospital employees relocate for personal reasons, not institutional command. The national average tenure for a registered nurse at a single employer runs 2.5–3.5 years. In a county that, until now, had no competing hospital, that tenure stretches further. There is nowhere to take a comparable role without a major commute.

**Pre-screening by the employer.** Hospital hiring involves background checks and drug screening before any applicant walks through your door. Employers in clinical settings have already filtered for certain categories of risk before your tenant screening process does.

An honest caveat applies: travel nurses and contract clinical staff are a different calculation. Contract nurses often earn $80,000–$120,000 annualized, but their employment is typically 13-week assignments — not permanent. McBride PM's [tenant screening standards](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Tenant-Screening-Standards.pdf) require verifiable employment for the full lease term, which makes short-term contract staff a less clean fit for standard 12-month leases. Permanent hires are the target tenant profile here.

## Where the Demand Lands: A Proximity Map for Rental Owners

The hospital at 5000 Gateway Boulevard is in Grovetown, close to the I-20 interchange that connects the county's western end to Augusta proper. That specific location creates a distinct demand radius — not distributed evenly across Columbia County, but concentrated in a band where the commute is genuinely convenient.

**Grovetown (30813) — closest and most underpriced.**
The hospital is in Grovetown. Rental prices here have historically run $150–$250/month below comparable Evans properties — a function of buyer preference and market familiarity. Healthcare workers relocating from out of state — from Texas, the Mid-Atlantic, the Midwest — do not carry that Evans-vs.-Grovetown pricing psychology. They look at the map, measure the commute, and price accordingly. Grovetown investors who have been patient with the market may be the most directly positioned for this shift.

**Evans (30809) — 10–15 minutes, established price point.**
Evans is within a straightforward commute via William Few Parkway and Washington Road from the hospital. The Washington Road corridor already hosts multiple Wellstar outpatient clinics — the medical ecosystem was pre-existing, and the hospital deepens it. Evans is the address most recognizable to relocating professionals arriving from larger metros without CSRA context. Our [guide for out-of-state investors](/blog/out-of-state-rental-investor-guide-augusta-ga/) covers why that name-recognition premium matters and how investors can position for it.

**Martinez (30907) — 15–20 minutes, lower entry price.**
Martinez provides more affordable acquisition prices for investors and the largest stock of 1980s–2000s vintage single-family homes in Columbia County. Healthcare workers priced out of Evans or who arrive during a competitive inventory window will consider Martinez. It is also a strong market for the administrative and support employee category — the $35,000–$48,000 earners who need a solid two-bedroom.

**North Augusta, SC — a different market altogether.**
For rental owners across the Savannah River, it is worth being direct: the Columbia County hospital's demand pull does not extend strongly to North Augusta. Wellstar is a Georgia-based employer, and the South Carolina commute adds 20–30 minutes under typical conditions. The [North Augusta rental investment guide](/blog/north-augusta-sc-rental-investment-guide/) covers that market's independent dynamics — the Riverside Village development and the South Carolina regulatory framework — which stand on their own merits.

## Rental Pricing: What the Hospital Addition Actually Changes

Let's be direct about what can be claimed here, because intellectually honest analysis is more useful than market optimism.

**What the hospital does not do:**
It does not trigger an immediate rent spike. Rent levels are set by supply and demand across the full market, and one employer adding 400–800 jobs into a county workforce of roughly 100,000 does not move aggregate rents overnight. If Columbia County continues to add new single-family inventory — and it is, given the active development pipeline our [Grovetown-Harlem growth corridor analysis](/blog/grovetown-harlem-growth-corridor-rental-investment/) covered — new supply can absorb significant demand without price compression on existing rentals.

**What the hospital does:**
It adds a durable, institutional demand anchor to a market that already has strong ones. Fort Gordon generates rental demand tied to the military PCS cycle. The Savannah River Site generates demand tied to Department of Energy contract cycles. The hospital adds demand tied to healthcare employment — a sector that does not deploy, does not relocate based on congressional appropriations, and does not close when a federal contract expires.

Augusta's rental market as of mid-2026 shows an average rent of approximately $1,230 per month across apartment types, with single-family Columbia County rentals commanding a premium — typically $1,400–$2,000 for a three-bedroom depending on age, condition, and location, per [RentCafe's current Augusta market data](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ga/augusta/). Year-over-year rent growth runs approximately 1.3%, reflecting a tightening market rather than a surge. The hospital adds a demand signal into that environment.

The practical implication for existing owners: the hospital provides a defensible basis for holding current Evans or Grovetown rentals rather than selling, and it supports pricing at the higher end of the comparable range on your next lease renewal. Amber McBride, our operations manager, has been advising current portfolio clients to audit lease expiration dates against the late-August opening — owners with leases expiring in July through October are in the optimal repricing window.

For investors evaluating a first Columbia County purchase: the hospital is confirmation of the thesis, not the thesis itself. If the numbers work at current pricing — if the property cash flows at a 5–7% cap rate on realistic market rents — the hospital is a compelling layer on top of sound fundamentals. If you are counting on a hospital-driven rent spike to make a marginal deal work, you are carrying speculative risk that current data does not support.

## How This Fits the Broader Columbia County Story

The hospital does not arrive in isolation. Columbia County has been one of Georgia's most consistently growing counties for two decades — [population up approximately 77%](https://developcolumbiacounty.com/) from 2000 to 2025, with a current population around 165,000. It is the primary off-post residential concentration for Fort Gordon, the preferred bedroom community for Augusta University healthcare professionals, and the county where the McBride PM portfolio is most heavily concentrated.

A note on the Vogtle nuclear plant picture, because it comes up in investor conversations: both Vogtle Units 3 and 4 entered commercial operation in 2023 and 2024 respectively. The peak construction workforce — over 9,000 workers — has dispersed. What remained is approximately 800 permanent operations positions, concentrated in Burke County (Waynesboro area), not in Augusta or Columbia County. Any temporary CSRA rental demand from the construction workforce has already unwound. The Vogtle story going forward is steady-state employment, not a growth driver for Columbia County rentals specifically.

The hospital addition is meaningful precisely because Columbia County has always had three reliable demand pillars — military (Fort Gordon), energy/federal (Savannah River Site), and healthcare (Augusta University Medical Center / Wellstar MCG). Until now, the healthcare pillar's physical footprint was entirely in Richmond County. The Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center moves the healthcare pillar's center of gravity west — directly into the county where most of the residential rental investment is concentrated.

## What CSRA Rental Owners Should Do Before Late August

**If you already own in Evans, Grovetown, or Martinez:**

Review your current lease expiration dates. If a lease expires between July and October 2026, you are in the window where the hospital's demand signal is directly relevant to repricing. Run comparables in your specific submarket before deciding on renewal terms — do not assume the 2025 market rate is the right anchor. If you are working with McBride PM, this is worth a conversation before the renewal letter goes out.

If you are currently holding a tenant on month-to-month terms, assess whether your current rent reflects current market. Month-to-month flexibility carries real value, but month-to-month at below-market rent is a carrying cost. Our [lease renewal strategies guide](/blog/lease-renewal-strategies-augusta-ga-landlords/) walks through how to approach that conversation with an existing tenant without triggering an unnecessary vacancy.

Make sure the property is market-ready. Healthcare professionals relocating from Atlanta, Northern Virginia, or the Houston medical corridor arrive with expectations calibrated to larger-market rentals. Clean, functional, and recently updated wins at application time. The [pre-rental prep checklist](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Pre-Rental-Property-Prep-Checklist.pdf) is worth running through before your next listing.

**If you are evaluating a first or additional Columbia County purchase:**

Run the analysis at current rents, not projected rents. The hospital strengthens the demand case — it does not change what a property costs to acquire and operate today.

Focus the search within the 15-minute commute radius from 5000 Gateway Blvd.: Grovetown, western Evans, and upper Martinez. Properties in that band are where this specific demand signal is most direct.

Consider Grovetown specifically. It has historically traded at a discount to Evans, but it is the submarket physically closest to the hospital. The discount may compress as the hospital establishes itself and healthcare workers begin to self-sort toward proximity.
