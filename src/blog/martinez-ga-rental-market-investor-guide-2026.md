## What Martinez Actually Is (and Isn't)

Martinez is an unincorporated community, not an incorporated city, which means it has no city government of its own. It falls under Columbia County's jurisdiction for zoning, code enforcement, and tax administration. Its primary ZIP code is 30907; parts of western Martinez bleed into 30809, which is Evans' ZIP.

That administrative fact matters for investors: you're getting Columbia County regulations and millage rates even though the address says "Martinez." Columbia County has rolled back or held flat its millage rate nine of the last ten years, which is meaningful when you're running a five-year pro forma. You're also getting Columbia County School District zoning, which is the same school district that covers Evans and Grovetown — a consistent draw for families renting single-family homes.

The housing stock in Martinez ranges from brick ranches built in the 1970s and 1980s to colonial-style two-stories from the late 1990s and early 2000s. The older sections east of Washington Road (near the Augusta city line) tend to have lower purchase prices and more deferred-maintenance risk. The newer sections west of Washington Road — subdivisions like Lake Montoya, River Hills Plantation, and Lake Olmstead-area neighborhoods — command stronger rents and attract more stable tenants.

[Washington Road is the major commercial spine running through Martinez, connecting Augusta's commercial district to Evans Town Center.] The corridor includes hospitals, grocery anchors, national retail, and Augusta University's Summerville Campus — which means tenants in Martinez have meaningful amenities within a short drive, not a 20-minute highway commute to basics.

## Martinez 2026 Market Snapshot

[Zillow's August 2026 data](https://www.zillow.com/martinez-ga/home-values/) puts Martinez's typical home value at **$286,339**, up 1.9% year-over-year. That's below Evans ($330K–$360K range) and roughly comparable to Grovetown ($270K–$300K for similar property types), though Grovetown's newer construction is pushing prices higher near the Wellstar Columbia County Medical Center corridor.

On the rent side, [Trulia's June 2026 figures](https://www.trulia.com/average-rent-market-trends/martinez-ga/) for Martinez show:

- One-bedroom: ~$1,167/month
- Two-bedroom: ~$1,236/month
- Three-bedroom: ~$1,841/month
- Four-bedroom: ~$2,200/month

Zillow's observed rent index puts the Martinez average at $1,712/month across all property types. Apartments.com reported a modest 0.9% year-over-year decline in asking rents as of mid-2026 — part of a national softening in the multifamily sector that affected CSRA asking prices less severely than other markets, but worth noting if you're underwriting tight.

For Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) context: [HUD's FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rent](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr.html) for ZIP 30907 is **$1,420/month for a two-bedroom** unit. This represents the ceiling for what the Augusta Housing Authority will approve for a voucher tenant in this ZIP. Market-rate two-bedrooms in Martinez routinely clear this ceiling, which means the voucher program competes for units rather than setting the ceiling on your rents.

The broader CSRA single-family market has tracked below 5% vacancy in 2026, per the fall 2026 market update we published in September. Martinez's vacancy within that is consistent with the county average — well-priced properties in solid condition lease in three to six weeks.


## The Demand Anchors Behind Martinez Rentals

Martinez sits at the intersection of four institutional demand drivers that make the CSRA one of the more resilient cash-flow markets in the Southeast. Understanding which of these actually reaches the 30907 submarket is the starting point for any serious underwriting.

**Fort Gordon.** Fort Gordon (U.S. Army Cyber Center of Excellence) is approximately 12–15 miles from central Martinez via I-20, with a roughly 20-minute commute to the main gate. The [Alliance for Fort Gordon](https://www.fortgordonalliance.com/) estimates the installation generates $2.26 billion in annual regional economic impact and has $1.6 billion in construction ongoing, projecting 17,000+ personnel additions by 2028. Service members and civilian contractors renting in Martinez are typically mid-career, stable earners whose rents align with Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH). 2026 Fort Gordon BAH rates for an E-6 with dependents run $1,854/month — well within the three-bedroom rent range for Martinez.

**Augusta University Medical Center / Wellstar MCG.** The AU Health system and Wellstar MCG employ tens of thousands of healthcare workers across their campuses on 15th Street in Augusta — roughly 10 miles from central Martinez. Healthcare workers (nurses, physicians, residents, allied health) tend to sign 12-month leases and prioritize stability. The proximity of Martinez to the Medical District makes it attractive for this tenant profile.

**Savannah River Site (SRS).** The SRS nuclear cleanup complex in Aiken County employs thousands of federal contractors, engineers, and support staff. Many commute from Columbia County because the schools, retail, and housing options are stronger there than in Aiken's smaller communities. SRS's workforce is expanding: the DOE Office of Environmental Management made a five-year Workforce Opportunities in Regional Careers grant effective May 2026 to build the SRS talent pipeline, signaling sustained hiring through the decade.

**Columbia County Growth.** Columbia County is Georgia's fastest-growing county by percentage over the last decade. Private-sector employment in professional services, healthcare, and retail continues to fill in around the institutional anchors, broadening the tenant pool beyond military and government workers. That breadth is what gives Columbia County landlords lower vacancy risk than comparable markets with single-employer dependency.

Read the [full breakdown of the CSRA's four demand anchors](/blog/four-demand-anchors-augusta-ga-rental-market/) for context on why these drivers are structurally different from the boom-and-bust employers that make other mid-size markets volatile.

## The Tenant Pool in Martinez

The 36.1% professional/managerial occupation share in Martinez (per the most recent Census profile) is higher than Richmond County averages and reflects the suburban Columbia County profile. Tenants in Martinez skew toward working couples and small families who want Columbia County School District access and don't want to pay Evans premiums.

A typical Martinez tenant profile:
- Dual-income household or single professional earning $75,000–$110,000
- Renting while evaluating whether to buy (price-to-income ratio is driving more long-term renters)
- Prioritizes school district, commute time to Fort Gordon or AU Med, and access to I-20
- Wants a well-maintained property with functional HVAC, updated appliances, and a garage

This is the same tenant pool that drives low vacancy in Columbia County broadly. The relative affordability of Martinez compared to Evans can attract households who qualify to rent in either submarket — your competitive set is the rental inventory in Evans and Grovetown, not the inventory in Augusta's lower-income corridors.

One nuance: the older housing stock east of Washington Road can attract a different tenant segment — more transient, more cost-sensitive. If you're buying in that zone, your underwriting needs to account for higher maintenance frequency and modestly higher turnover. A property manager familiar with the specific streets can tell you immediately which side of that line a given property falls on.

## A Due Diligence Checklist for Buying in Martinez from Out of State

Buying remotely isn't inherently riskier than buying in person — it's riskier if you skip the steps that compensate for not being there. Here's what a responsible Martinez purchase looks like:

1. **Get an independent home inspection.** Never use the seller's inspector. Hire your own inspector who has no relationship with the seller's agent. For homes built before 1985 — common in Martinez's older sections — specifically request testing for polybutylene plumbing, which failed broadly and was used heavily in Georgia construction in the 1970s–1990s.

2. **Pull the Columbia County property tax record.** The Columbia County Tax Commissioner's website shows the assessed value, millage rate, and most recent tax bill. Confirm the numbers in your pro forma match what the county actually bills, not what the listing agent estimated.

3. **Order a flood zone check.** Some sections of Martinez near the Savannah River wetlands carry FEMA flood zone designations. Flood insurance premiums materially change your pro forma. Check the specific parcel on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center before proceeding.

4. **Verify rent comps with a local property manager — not Zillow.** Zillow's Zestimate Rent is a national algorithm that doesn't know about specific street dynamics in Martinez. A property manager who currently manages homes in 30907 can tell you what a specific address would actually rent for within two or three business days.

5. **Check the CC&Rs if there's an HOA.** Some Martinez subdivisions restrict rentals. Pull the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions from the Columbia County Clerk's website before you're under contract. An HOA rental cap found after closing is an expensive surprise.

6. **Review utility history.** Request 12 months of utility data from the seller. A home with unusually high electric bills in summer often has HVAC that's failing or improperly sized — a $6,000–$10,000 fix that should be in your offer, not your year-two capex budget.

7. **Engage a property manager before closing.** Hire your PM first, then close. A good local PM will verify market rent, flag inspection issues, and have a leasing plan ready before your closing date. See the [full guide for out-of-state CSRA buyers](/blog/out-of-state-rental-investor-guide-augusta-ga/) for the complete remote purchase process.

8. **Run a true operating expense model.** Download our [CSRA Operating Expenses Worksheet](/pdfs/McBride-PM_Operating-Expenses-Worksheet.pdf) for the standard expense ratios we apply to Columbia County single-family rentals, including maintenance reserves, management fees, insurance, and vacancy. Don't model 5% vacancy if you're buying a deferred-maintenance home that needs work first.

## Working with a Property Manager in Martinez

Martinez is not a market where remote self-management is a reasonable strategy. The leasing market moves quickly when prices are right and slowly when they aren't — and the difference between a 21-day lease and an 84-day vacancy on a $1,750/month rental is $4,375 in lost gross rent, which erases your yield advantage for the year.

A licensed property manager in Martinez handles:
- Accurate local rent pricing (critical — overpricing by $50–$75/month can add 6–8 weeks of vacancy)
- Tenant screening against objective criteria (income, credit, background) that comply with Fair Housing
- Lease execution under Georgia landlord-tenant law ([O.C.G.A. Title 44-7](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/))
- Maintenance coordination through a vetted local vendor network
- Rent collection, late fee enforcement, and dispossessory filing when needed
- Monthly owner statements and annual 1099 reporting
