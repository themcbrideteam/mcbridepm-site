**Does the BRRRR strategy work in Augusta, GA in 2026?**
Yes — with important caveats. The CSRA offers affordable purchase prices, rising rental demand, and a refinanceable market, but current DSCR rates of 6.5–7.5% mean BRRRR works best as a capital-recycling strategy rather than a cash-flow engine. Buying at 60–65% of after-repair value and targeting Columbia County ZIP codes near Fort Gordon is the approach that pencils most reliably in today's market.

You've done the reading. You understand the cycle: buy a distressed property with cash or hard money, renovate it, rent it, then refinance at the improved value and pull your original capital out. Buy again. Repeat.

The question you're actually asking isn't "how does BRRRR work?" — it's whether the specific numbers of Augusta, GA and the broader Central Savannah River Area support this strategy in 2026. You want a market where you can find distressed deals, where contractors exist and rehab costs are predictable, where rental demand is strong enough to support post-renovation rents, and where a DSCR lender will refinance you into a loan that at minimum breaks even on cash flow.

The CSRA clears most of those bars. The place it gets tricky — and where most underwritten projections go sideways — is the refinance. Here's the full picture.

## Why Out-of-State BRRRR Investors Are Looking at the CSRA

Augusta isn't a speculative market. Five institutions anchor rental demand in a way most secondary markets can't match: Fort Gordon (home of the Army Cyber Center of Excellence), the Savannah River Site, Plant Vogtle, Augusta University Medical Center / Wellstar MCG, and USC Aiken across the South Carolina line. The Alliance for Fort Gordon describes the region as being in its ["largest economic renaissance"](https://www.fortgordonalliance.com/csra-is-in-its-largest-economic-renaissance-with-more-investment-coming-out-of-srs-and-fort-gordon/) with more than $7.5 billion in estimated annual economic impact.

That demand base shows up in the rental numbers. [WRDW reported in September 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/09/09/nearly-half-augusta-households-rent-prices-climb/) that nearly half of Augusta households now rent, with prices continuing to climb. SFR Analytics' [metro-level data from July 2026](https://sfranalytics.com/rental-yields/ga/augusta-richmond-county-ga-sc) pegs the median asking rent for active single-family listings at $1,700 — a floor that Columbia County (Evans, Grovetown, Martinez) beats consistently by $300–$500.

For BRRRR specifically, the CSRA offers three structural advantages over gateway markets:

1. **Purchase prices are still affordable.** A distressed 3BR/2BA in Columbia County or Richmond County lists at $110,000–$180,000. Gateway coastal markets rarely see those numbers, which means your acquisition capital goes further.
2. **ARV supports the refinance math.** Renovated single-family homes in Columbia County are regularly appraising at $240,000–$290,000, giving you a spread that can support a 75% LTV cash-out.
3. **Military and institutional workers are durable renters.** BAH-funded military tenants, hospital employees, and SRS contractors are low-default, long-tenure renters who minimize the vacancy risk that breaks BRRRR math.

The market isn't perfect for BRRRR — no market is. But the fundamentals are real, and they're not going away.

## Understanding the Full BRRRR Cycle in CSRA Terms

Before the numbers, the framework. BRRRR has five phases: Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. The goal is not necessarily maximum monthly cash flow — it's recycling your original invested capital so you can acquire again. Done well, you end up owning a rented property with a loan on it, having pulled most or all of your original capital back out at the refinance. Done poorly, you're stuck with a property that won't appraise high enough to refinance, or a loan payment that eats your rental income.

Here's the cycle mapped to CSRA numbers:

| Phase | What It Means in the CSRA | Typical Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Distressed SFR, often estate sale, REO, or off-market | 60–65% of post-repair ARV |
| **Rehab** | Cosmetic to mid-level renovation | $25,000–$55,000 |
| **Rent** | List within 2 weeks of CO | $1,900–$2,300/mo (3BR, Columbia Co.) |
| **Refinance** | DSCR cash-out at 75% LTV | 6.5–7.5% rate range, 2026 |
| **Repeat** | Recycled capital → next acquisition | Most capital recovered if bought at 65% ARV |

*This is general guidance from a property manager — not legal or tax advice; talk to a Georgia or South Carolina attorney and a CPA for your situation.*

## The Buy Phase: Finding BRRRR Properties in the Augusta Area

The buy phase is where BRRRR either works or doesn't. You need to purchase at a meaningful discount to after-repair value — typically 60–65% — which means finding properties that have been neglected, inherited, estate-priced, or sitting long enough that the owner wants out. The CSRA has these; it's not a hot market in the way Miami or Nashville is. But it's also not the deep distress of some Midwest markets.

Where to find the deals:

**Richmond County (Augusta proper):** More distressed inventory than Columbia County. Homes near ZIP codes 30904 and 30906 can be found at $80,000–$140,000 with realistic ARVs of $160,000–$210,000 after renovation. The challenge is ARV variability — values in some Augusta neighborhoods are hard to predict, and your appraisal can come in 10–15% below your model. Buffer your ARV conservatively.

**Columbia County (Evans, Grovetown, Martinez):** Less distressed inventory, but higher and more predictable ARV. Distressed homes in Grovetown (ZIP 30813) and Evans (ZIP 30809) rarely appear below $150,000 — most are priced in the $155,000–$185,000 range even in rough condition. Post-renovation ARV in these submarkets ranges from $240,000 to $290,000 for a typical 3BR/2BA. The buy-to-ARV math is tighter but the appraisal risk is lower.

**North Augusta, SC (Aiken County):** Homes on the SC side serve SRS and Plant Vogtle workers. Prices and ARVs are similar to Richmond County. SC landlord-tenant law differs from Georgia, so budget time to understand the [South Carolina Residential Landlord and Tenant Act](https://mcbride-pm.com/blog/south-carolina-landlord-tenant-laws-aiken-rental-owners/) before acquiring across the border.

**Off-market sourcing:** Direct mail, door-knocking, and probate/estate networking are the most effective channels. Many of the best BRRRR deals in the CSRA never hit Zillow. Building a relationship with a local property manager who knows which homes are sitting vacant is a real edge — we see these before they're listed.

## The Rehab Phase: What Renovations Cost in the CSRA

Rehab cost accuracy is the difference between a successful BRRRR and a capital trap. The CSRA has a mid-sized contractor market — large enough to find licensed professionals, tight enough that good GCs are booked out 4–8 weeks and you'll need to build relationships before you need them.

**Cost benchmarks for CSRA rental renovations (2026):**

- Paint (interior, 1,200 sq ft): $3,500–$6,000
- Flooring replacement (LVP, 1,000 sq ft): $4,500–$8,000
- Kitchen refresh (new cabinets, counters, sink, appliances): $12,000–$22,000
- Bathroom refresh (tile, vanity, toilet, lighting): $4,500–$9,000 per bath
- HVAC replacement (3-ton split system): $6,000–$10,000 installed
- Water heater replacement: $1,200–$2,200
- Roof replacement (2,000 sq ft): $9,000–$16,000
- Electrical panel upgrade to 200-amp: $2,500–$5,000
- Exterior paint: $3,500–$7,000

A light cosmetic rehab (paint, flooring, fixtures, landscaping) on a structurally sound home typically runs $15,000–$30,000. A mid-level renovation that also updates the kitchen and one bathroom and addresses one system (HVAC or water heater) runs $35,000–$55,000. Any home that needs roof work, electrical upgrading, or plumbing re-do in addition to cosmetics can exceed $65,000.

Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. Title 43), licensed contractors are required for work above $2,500 that involves licensed trades — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, structural. This is not optional and not negotiable. Working with an unlicensed contractor to save money on a BRRRR project is one of the fastest ways to fail an appraisal, trigger a permit violation, or face liability under the Georgia Safe at Home Act if a habitability failure occurs after a tenant moves in. See the full breakdown in [DIY vs. Licensed Contractor: Georgia Rental Repairs That Require a Pro](/blog/diy-vs-licensed-contractor-georgia-rental-property-repairs/).

**A note on scope creep.** BRRRR rehabs in the CSRA regularly run 15–25% over initial budget. Build that buffer into your underwriting from the start, not as an afterthought. If your deal only pencils at the low-end rehab estimate, it's not a deal.

## The Refinance Phase: Running the DSCR Numbers for the CSRA

This is where BRRRR either recycles your capital or buries it. Run these numbers before you buy — not after the rehab.

**A worked example using a Grovetown, GA 3BR/2BA:**

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| After-repair value (ARV) | $265,000 |
| Purchase price (65% of ARV) | $172,250 |
| Rehab cost | $42,000 |
| Total all-in cost | $214,250 |
| Refinance at 75% LTV of ARV | $198,750 |
| Capital remaining in deal | $15,500 |
| Monthly mortgage (7%, 30-yr) | $1,323 |
| Gross monthly rent | $2,100 |
| **DSCR (rent ÷ P&I)** | **1.59** |

A DSCR of 1.59 comfortably clears the typical 1.25 lender threshold. You've recycled $198,750 of your $214,250 all-in cost — leaving only $15,500 in the deal.

Now the monthly cash flow picture, using realistic CSRA operating benchmarks:

| Expense | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Gross rent | $2,100 |
| Vacancy allowance (5%) | -$105 |
| Property management (10%) | -$210 |
| Maintenance reserve (8%) | -$168 |
| Property taxes (Columbia Co.) | -$245 |
| Landlord insurance | -$130 |
| **Net operating income (NOI)** | **$1,242** |
| Mortgage (P&I, 7%) | -$1,323 |
| **Monthly cash flow** | **-$81** |

Negative cash flow of $81/month. That's a realistic result at 7% DSCR rates. It's not catastrophic — it's a holding cost of less than $1,000/year on a property where you've recycled $198,750 and control $265,000 in real estate. But it means BRRRR in the CSRA at today's rates is not a passive income vehicle from day one; it's a capital recycling and equity-building vehicle.

**When does it cash flow positively?** Two scenarios change the math:

1. **Buy at 60% ARV instead of 65%:** Purchase at $159,000. All-in with $42K rehab = $201,000. Same refinance to $198,750. Left in deal: $2,250. Same P&I payment. Cash flow unchanged at -$81/month — but you've recycled virtually all your capital and your effective cash-on-cash return on the $2,250 remaining is -$972/year ÷ $2,250 = -43%, which matters less when the capital is essentially fully recycled.

2. **Rates fall to 6.0–6.25%:** Monthly P&I on $198,750 drops to $1,193–$1,217. NOI of $1,242 now exceeds debt service. Positive cash flow of $25–$49/month. This is why BRRRR investors who executed in 2025–2026 are often planning to rate-and-term refinance when DSCR rates move below 6.5%.

## The Repeat Phase: Cycling Capital Back Into the Next CSRA Acquisition

The power of BRRRR isn't the first deal — it's what you do after the refinance check clears.

In the Grovetown example above, you deployed $214,250, refinanced and pulled back $198,750, and now have $15,500 tied up in a property that is breaking roughly even on cash flow but building equity as the loan amortizes and rents rise. You have $198,750 to work with.

If you can repeat the cycle with similar efficiency, two transactions leave you owning two properties totaling $530,000 in real estate — with roughly $198,750 in remaining liquid capital and two loans totaling $397,500 — having started with ~$215,000 in cash. That's the compounding effect the strategy targets. Even at negative cash flow of $81/month per property, your equity position grows by thousands per year through amortization and appreciation.

**Scaling considerations specific to the CSRA:**

Finding two strong BRRRR deals per year in the same market is more realistic than four or five. The distressed inventory exists, but it isn't infinite. Build relationships with:
- Estate and probate attorneys in Columbia and Richmond counties
- Local wholesalers who understand your buy criteria
- A reliable general contractor (critical — without one, your rehab schedule and budget collapse)
- A property manager who can list on completion day, not 30 days after (vacancy is cash burning)

## BRRRR Risk Factors Specific to the Augusta Market

No BRRRR pro forma leaves out the risks. Here are the ones most specific to the CSRA:

**Appraisal risk in Richmond County.** Richmond County (Augusta proper) has more value variation than Columbia County. Distressed properties in some Augusta ZIP codes may appraise $20,000–$40,000 below your projected ARV due to neighboring distressed properties depressing comparables. If your refinance appraisal comes in low, you recover less capital. Run conservative ARV comps and get a second opinion before closing on any Richmond County acquisition.

**Contractor availability.** The CSRA has a solid but not deep contractor market. Good licensed GCs are frequently booked 6–8 weeks out. If you haven't secured a contractor before closing on the acquisition, you may sit on a vacant, unrenovated property for 2–3 months — burning holding costs and extending your time to rent.

**Rate sensitivity.** As shown above, BRRRR cash flow in the CSRA is thin at 7%+ DSCR rates. A deal that pencils at 6.5% doesn't pencil at 7.5%. Build a rate buffer of at least 75 basis points above your underwritten rate before committing to a purchase.

**Military tenant SCRA exposure.** A Fort Gordon tenant who receives PCS orders can terminate their lease with 30 days' notice under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, regardless of remaining lease term. This is not a reason to avoid military tenants — they're excellent renters — but it means your vacancy reserve and BRRRR model should assume one turnover in the first three to four years. The [SCRA lease termination guide for Augusta-area landlords](/blog/scra-lease-termination-fort-gordon-landlord-guide/) covers what you're legally required to accommodate.

**Georgia Safe at Home Act compliance.** HB 404 imposes specific habitability obligations that affect what you must complete in a rehab before the property can be leased. Missing these items can delay rental readiness and add unbudgeted costs at the worst possible time — right before your planned listing date. See [Georgia Safe at Home Act: An Augusta Landlord's Guide](/blog/georgia-safe-at-home-act-landlord-guide-augusta-ga/) for a full breakdown.

## A Realistic BRRRR Checklist for the CSRA

Before you submit an offer on a BRRRR candidate in the Augusta area, confirm the following:

- [ ] Purchase price is at or below 65% of a conservatively underwritten ARV
- [ ] Rehab scope and budget have been reviewed by a licensed GC with CSRA experience
- [ ] Rehab budget includes a 20% contingency for scope creep
- [ ] Post-renovation rent is benchmarked against 5+ comparable active leases, not asking prices
- [ ] DSCR calculation at 75% LTV of ARV produces DSCR ≥ 1.25 at current rates
- [ ] Monthly cash flow is modeled at the refinance rate plus 75 bps (rate buffer)
- [ ] Holding costs during rehab (insurance, taxes, any hard money interest) are included in all-in cost
- [ ] Property management is arranged before listing day — not after first vacancy
- [ ] All licensed trade work (electrical, plumbing, HVAC) is scoped with licensed CSRA contractors
- [ ] Title search confirms no liens, back taxes, or HOA violations on the distressed property
- [ ] A Georgia-licensed real estate attorney has reviewed the purchase contract if it's an off-market deal

## What BRRRR Investors Get Wrong About the CSRA

**Overestimating ARV on the high end.** Investors coming from coastal markets where properties sell at 110% of list price sometimes project aggressive ARVs in Augusta. The CSRA is not that market. Renovated comps are a real ceiling; don't project past them.

**Underestimating property management complexity.** BRRRR investors who self-manage remote CSRA rentals consistently report more problems than those who use professional management from day one. The [true cost of self-managing analysis](/blog/true-cost-self-managing-rental-property-csra/) covers why Georgia's regulatory environment — including the Safe at Home Act's mandatory response timelines — makes remote DIY management particularly risky.

**Skipping the move-in inspection.** BRRRR properties are freshly renovated, which makes skipping the move-in inspection tempting. Don't. Under O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33, a properly documented move-in inspection is what gives you legal standing to deduct renovation damage from the security deposit at tenant turnover. Skip it and you've just handed your first tenant a potential free pass to damage your fresh rehab.

**Treating BRRRR as a one-decision strategy.** BRRRR is a system, not a one-time transaction. The repeat phase requires capital discipline, local market knowledge, and an operator network (contractor, lender, PM) that you build over multiple cycles. Investors who try to BRRRR a single property and then stop rarely capture the strategy's real value.
